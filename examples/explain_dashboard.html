CertGate Explain demo

A safety gate sits in front of this computer model: it only answers when it is confident enough, and hands everything else to a person. Pick a case below to see why it decided the way it did — and try changing the inputs yourself.
Read this first. This page describes the computer program, never the patient. When it shows "what would need to change for the computer to answer", that is a fact about how the program decides — not medical advice, not a treatment suggestion, and not something anyone could actually do to a patient (you can't change one measurement and keep everything else the same). Any case flipped by the smallest change is answered with the lowest confidence the program is allowed to have — barely over the line. And the program's safety promise is about its average mistakes across many hospitals, not a promise about any one case on this page. The data here is a . Read this first. Everything interactive on this page — sliders, flip buttons, curves, waterfalls, the threshold explorer — asks a score-space question of the gate: what would the model's inputs need to be for the case to clear the answering bar. These are not causal claims, not treatment suggestions, and not clinically achievable actions (features are not independently changeable). A case flipped by the minimal change is answered at the weakest possible confidence, a hair above the bar itself. The error-rate certificate is a site-population-average guarantee over answered cases: no individual record carries a certified property of its own. This page shows a .
How to read this page (30 seconds)
  1. Pick a case on the left. Green means the program answered; red means it wasn't sure enough and handed the case to a person.
  2. The meter shows how sure the program was. It must clear the dark line (the bar) to answer.
  3. The bars show what pushed its confidence up or down. When pushes and pulls cancel out, it hands the case over.
  4. Try the sliders — move a value and watch it re-decide. The red buttons put everything back to the real values.
