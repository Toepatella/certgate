# ultracode — CertGate pre-submission editorial panel

This is an ultracode run: convene the full editorial panel of *Discover Computing* and put `paper/draft.md` (pandoc markdown; `@citekeys` resolve in `paper/references.bib`) through the process it will face on submission. Run end to end without check-ins. Use at most 12 subagents; the stages below need about eleven. Author/affiliation fields such as `[AUTHOR NAME(S)]` are deliberate placeholders, never a defect. Outputs go under `paper/review/`; if that directory already holds reports from an earlier run, move them to `paper/review/prior/` first — no Stage-1 agent may read an earlier run's output.

**Venue card** (goes into every brief; all fit judgments use it). *Discover Computing*, Springer Nature: fully open access, JIF 1.9 (2025), 22-day median to first decision. Target: the Collection "Intelligent Medicine: Machine Learning and Explainable AI for Next-Generation Healthcare," deadline 2026-10-05. Scope: ML/AI for clinical, epidemiological, and public-health contexts; explainability is the central emphasis — a transparency requirement and an educational aid for clinicians. Encouraged: fairness, uncertainty quantification, calibration, OOD robustness, clinical auditability, human-centered design, federated/privacy-preserving learning, multimodal EHR. Out of scope: sentiment/opinion mining of AI adoption. Editors: Enrico Barbierato (performance evaluation, Green AI, philosophy of AI); Manuel Striani (AI in healthcare, process mining, medical informatics, XAI education).

**Manuscript card — orchestrator context only, never pasted into any brief; referees meet the paper cold.** "CertGate: finite-sample certified selective prediction for multi-site clinical risk models, with label-shift robustness and explainable abstention": a selective-prediction gate certifying answered-set error with the site (hospital cluster), not the record, as the unit of independence; claimed contributions span the certified gate, a label-shift mode, attribution-based explanations, and the validation design. Evaluation is synthetic-only: 208 sites, ~9.5% prevalence.

**Access rules — restate in every brief; every finding must trace to a passage in files its author was allowed to read.**

- Desk screen, R1, R2, R3, R5: `paper/draft.md` and `paper/references.bib` only, plus any figure files the draft embeds (those are part of the manuscript, not repo internals) — like real referees.
- R4 adds `README.md`, `requirements.txt`, `certgate/`, `tests/`, `experiments/` (including `experiments/out/`), and may run the test suite. README and the dependency manifest are evidence about the code and its availability, not a source of claims about the work's quality — do not import their framing into findings.
- Stage-2 examiners are panelists too: manuscript and bibliography, plus — when prosecuting an R4 finding — the repo artifacts R4 cited. The Stage-3 editor: the same, plus `PAPER-OUTLINE.md` and the Stage-1/2 output files.
- Off-limits to everyone until Stage 3: `PAPER-OUTLINE.md`, `REDTEAM.md`, `REVIEW-FABLE.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `SPEC.md`, `METHODS.md`, `paper/TODO.md`, and any other internal planning or self-review file in the repo. Findings must arise fresh.
- Project context about CertGate auto-loads into every agent's session (status claims, headline results, internal-audit outcomes). Treat it as off-limits material that leaked: never cite it, quote it, or rely on it, and never let it settle a question. If the draft does not state something, it is not stated.

**Briefs.** Each subagent brief carries the venue card, the persona and mandate, the ground rules, the placeholder exemption, its access rules, and its output path — nothing from the manuscript card, nothing from off-limits files.

**Ground rules for every referee.** Sweep the entire manuscript and report everything found, fatal to trivial — severity thresholds are the editor's job, not yours. Anchor every criticism with a quote or precise citation of the passage it targets. Number findings DS-01…, R1-01…, R2-01…, and keep IDs stable through every later file. Test the draft's claimed contributions against the prior art you know: name the closest work, say whether the draft cites it, and whether the positioning survives contact with it — missing prior art is a finding, and so is a citation that does not support the sentence hanging on it (R1 owns the risk-control / conformal / selective-prediction lineage; R3 owns the attribution and abstention-explanation lineage). Police register with the right sign: the authors intend restrained prose with one hedged headline claim — hunt hype, novelty-spam, and unhedged generalization; never penalize hedging.

**Stage 1 — one parallel wave, six independent agents** (none sees another's output).

- **Desk screen (handling editor).** Scope fit against the collection's clinical framing and its explainability emphasis; whether the evidence base supports the claims made for it; desk-reject risks; open-access Springer norms — abstract, structure, declarations (data/code availability, competing interests, funding, ethics), citation and figure hygiene. Referee review proceeds regardless of the screen's verdict. → `paper/review/desk-screen.md`
- **R1 — statistics, distribution-free inference, selective prediction.** Interrogate every guarantee claim against what is actually proved: what "finite-sample" means here, the independence unit and its justification, δ accounting across modes and thresholds, the sequential test and influence caps, whether the violation protocol measures what it claims, and statistics-reporting standards wherever a number appears.
- **R2 — clinical ML, health-informatics deployment.** The deployment story: what abstention costs operationally and who absorbs it; whether the cohort and its parameters are clinically realistic; what the evidence licenses about clinical use; the auditability and fairness expectations of this collection.
- **R3 — XAI, trustworthy ML.** The explanation contribution: what the attributions are technically and what they add over the underlying model; what abstention explanations would do for a clinician; how explanation quality is evaluated (faithfulness versus plausibility); whether explainability is load-bearing or ornamental for this venue.
- **R4 — reproducibility and code.** Trace every number in the draft to `experiments/out/` artifacts; run the tests and judge the suite's credibility; probe determinism and seed claims; check the availability statements against repo reality.
- **R5 — forensic reader, draft only.** Reconcile every abstract number against body, tables, and captions; parse the guarantee statement clause by clause and state exactly what is and is not certified — and what a hurried reader could mistake it for; quote any characterization of cited work that rings false; audit register line by line.

Report format (journal style): **Summary** in the referee's own words; numbered **Major** and **Minor points** — that split is advisory convention, and nothing may be dropped for being small; **Questions to authors** (unproven suspicions); **Confidential comments to the editor** (sharpest doubts, stated plainly); recommendation (accept / minor revision / major revision / reject). → `paper/review/referee-1-statistics.md`, `referee-2-clinical.md`, `referee-3-xai.md`, `referee-4-reproducibility.md`, `referee-5-forensic.md`.

**Stage 2 — the editorial office audits the reviews (up to four parallel examiners).** Pool all Stage-1 findings, split them across examiners, and prosecute each claim against the manuscript (R4's against the repo artifacts it cited). A finding dies if: the passage does not say what it claims; the manuscript answers it elsewhere (cite where); its numbers are wrong; it flags placeholder fields; it demands what the paper explicitly and defensibly scopes out (quote the scoping passage); or it complains about material the referee's access rules withheld. Merge duplicates across reports, noting co-discovery. Survivors are CONFIRMED with their anchor, or PLAUSIBLE with the single check that would settle them. Log every kill with its reason — the kill log is a deliverable. → `paper/review/verification.md`

**Stage 3 — editorial synthesis (one agent, after Stage 2).** Only now open `PAPER-OUTLINE.md`: tag each surviving finding independently-rediscovered or genuinely-new against its reviewer-risk table, and list table risks the panel missed. → `paper/review/risk-crosscheck.md`. Then write `paper/review/decision-letter.md` — decision first (accept / minor / major / reject), rationale grounded in collection scope, referees synthesized by number — and `paper/review/revision-plan.md`: every CONFIRMED or PLAUSIBLE finding by ID, ranked S1 (would sink or desk-reject the paper at this venue) / S2 (forces major revision or threatens the 2026-10-05 deadline) / S3 (polish), each with what to change, where (file, section), and what would satisfy the objecting referee.

**Reporting.** The files carry the substance — no padding, no boilerplate summaries. Chat narration stays brief; the final reply leads with the decision, then the counts (raised, killed, confirmed), the top confirmed findings, and the files written.
