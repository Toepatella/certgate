# METHODS — paper-ready specification

Written to become the paper's Methods section with minimal editing. One caveat: the E1–E9 experiment labels used in section 8 are INTERNAL only. The manuscript carries none of them; it names each study descriptively and maps the `E1_`–`E9_` artifact prefixes in its Code availability section. Strip the labels when pasting. Notation: sites (clusters) indexed c; records i; binary outcome y ∈ {0,1}; features x; risk score s(x) ∈ [0.5, 1] (max-softmax of a probabilistic classifier); threshold τ; error budget α; confidence 1−δ.

## 1. Problem setting

Multi-site clinical data: ~200 collection sites contributing 20–5,000 records each, outcome prevalence ~9–10%, site-level heterogeneity in prevalence and case mix. A deployed risk model answers a case only when its confidence clears a threshold; we want a **certificate**: with probability ≥ 1−δ over the draw of calibration sites, the influence-weighted error rate among answered cases, **averaged over the population of sites** from which the calibration sites were drawn, is ≤ α — under a stated, tagged assumption about how deployment differs from calibration. This is the exchangeable form; under the label-shift tag the bounded quantity is that risk reweighted to the target class prevalence (§5 states which prevalence exactly), with the probability over the joint draw of the calibration sites, the S_aux split and the target pool. This is a site-population-average guarantee, not a per-site one; §3 states exactly what that does and does not bound.

**Clinical target, decision rule, loss.** The certified quantity is an error rate, so three things must be supplied by the deployment and are taken as given. *Outcome:* y ∈ {0,1} pinned by an index event, a prediction time at which x is frozen, and an ascertainment window; CertGate certifies whatever binary label the caller supplies and does not itself resolve censoring, competing risks, or a time-to-event horizon. *Decision:* ŷ_i = 1[p̂(x_i) ≥ 1/2]; err_i = 1[ŷ_i ≠ y_i], the quantity entering the atom of §3. The selective score s(x) = max(p̂, 1−p̂) is symmetric about p̂ = 1/2, so the gate withholds cases near the decision boundary in either direction and adds no clinical threshold of its own. *Loss:* err_i is symmetric 0–1 — FN and FP weigh equally and α budgets their sum. At single-digit prevalence most answered errors are FN (measured in E1), and an α set near the prevalence is close to what a constant always-negative rule achieves, so meeting the budget is not by itself evidence the gate is doing anything: the answered/declined operating characteristics and the composition instrument (§6) carry that evidence. A cost-weighted loss needs a different atom and is out of scope.

Records within a site share equipment, populations, and practice patterns; the honest unit of independence is the **site**. All guarantees here are cluster-level: ~83 site-level observations, not ~10⁵ record-level ones. This is the load-bearing design decision and the reason naive record-level selective prediction is silently overconfident on multi-site data.

## 2. Data discipline

Sites are split disjointly: **S_train 40%** (fit the risk model), **S_aux 20%** (threshold ordering, label-shift correction machinery, feasibility diagnostics), **S_cal 40%** (certification only — it enters the *certified path* only through the certification walks; the estimated and diagnostic report tiers also read it, downstream of and without feedback into the certificate). Every fitted object is a function of (S_train, S_aux, target features) only; the code enforces split disjointness with a hard assertion at pipeline entry. All constants below are pinned to their literal values by a unit test, and all are fixed a priori in `constants.py` except the post-hoc S_aux floor of §5 (vi), which lives in `shift.py`, so any drift fails CI — a lightweight, verifiable version of pre-registration.

## 3. Certified estimand

Site sizes are heavy-tailed, so an unweighted mean over records would let one giant site dominate; capping realized contributions is anti-conservative — a construction with 17.5% record-weighted risk certifies at α=5% under naive truncation, kept as a regression test. Instead each site gets an outcome-independent **influence weight** g_c = min(n_c, M), M = 100 (a function of site size only, never of errors), and the certified quantity is the **influence-weighted answered-set risk**: R_M ≤ α where R_M = Σ_c g_c a_c e_c / Σ_c g_c a_c (a_c = answered fraction, e_c = answered-set error rate at site c). The cap acts on influence only; it scales each site's whole (signed) contribution and never censors bad news.

Certification is via the linearized per-site atom
Z_c = (g_c / (M·n_c)) · Σ_{i∈c} ans_i·(err_i − α) + α ∈ [0, 1],
so E[Z] ≤ α ⟺ R_M ≤ α whenever E[g_c a_c] > 0 (if nothing answers, R_M is undefined, every atom equals α, and nothing certifies). Sites with zero answered-eligible records enter as neutral atoms Z_c = α. A neutral atom's own wealth factor in the §4 test is exactly 1, but it still counts toward n and enters the running mean and variance that set later bets, so it can move the outcome in either direction (never validity). Neutral atoms are never dropped: dropping them would leave R_M unchanged (they carry zero weight in it) but make the number of atoms depend on τ. The minimum-cluster gate counts only **record-carrying** sites.

**Scope (what is and is not bounded).** E[Z] is an expectation over the *site draw*: the certificate bounds the influence-weighted answered risk averaged over the site population, with the probability statement over the calibration draw (exchangeable mode; in BBSE mode the bounded risk is reweighted to the target class prevalence and the probability is over the joint draw of the calibration sites, the S_aux split and the target pool). Two things it deliberately does **not** bound. (i) *Individual sites*: under between-site heterogeneity a site's own answered risk can exceed α while the population average stays within budget; the fraction of sites doing so is governed by the between-site dispersion, which the certificate neither measures nor bounds (E1's sensitivity arm quantifies this: raising the site random-effect s_u from 0.5 to 2.0 leaves the certified aggregate within budget while per-site exceedances grow). A per-site guarantee would require a tolerance bound on an upper quantile of the per-site risk distribution — a different, more cluster-hungry test. (ii) *Realized counts*: the bound is not a bound on any single batch's realized error count, which exceeds α at binomial-dispersion rates even under a valid certificate — the validation protocol (§7) is built around this distinction. In the exchangeable mode the certified thresholds are a single function of the calibration draw, shared by every target pool they are applied to (one 1−δ event per draw), while coverage and diagnostics remain target-dependent; in BBSE mode the fit depends on the target pool through q, so certificates are per-pool. The operative rung is the strictest certified α — a data-driven selection over the two-rung ladder, so the selected claim holds jointly at 1−2δ.

## 4. Certified test: betting on the calibration sites

We test H0: E[Z] ≥ α with the Waudby-Smith–Ramdas betting martingale: wealth K_t = Π_{s≤t} (1 + λ_s(α − Z_s)) with predictable λ_t = min( sqrt(2 ln(1/δ) / (σ̂²_{t−1} n)), 0.9/(1−α) ), σ̂²_{t−1} = (1/4 + Σ_{i<t} (Z_i − μ̂_i)²)/t with μ̂_i = (1/2 + Σ_{j≤i} Z_j)/(i+1) (WSR's predictable plug-in: the start (0.5, 0.25) counts as one pseudo-observation), variance floor 1e-8; certify iff sup_t K_t ≥ 1/δ (Ville's inequality gives finite-sample level δ). Cluster order is deterministic: each walk draws its cluster permutations from a stream seeded by numpy SeedSequence([protocol seed, α index, mode index, w₁, w₂]), where (w₁, w₂) are the first eight bytes of sha256 of the walk's stream label read as two big-endian 32-bit words — the empty label for the baseline walk, "lo"/"hi" for the two BBSE endpoint walks — and the k-th threshold tested consumes the stream's k-th permutation. The order is reproducible and free of any target identifier, so one calibration draw yields one set of baseline certified thresholds shared by every target pool. At n clusters and atom-unit margin m, every valid level-δ test certifies with probability at most δ(1+m/(1−α))^n ≤ δ·e^{nm/(1−α)}; the right-hand bound is below one exactly when m is below the information floor ln(1/δ)(1−α)/n, so below the floor a test may still certify on a favourable draw but never with certainty, and certainty needs m ≥ (1−α)(δ^{−1/n}−1), of which the floor is the first-order term. We report margin/floor (margin: the record-pooled S_aux proxy max_τ cov(τ)·(α − risk(τ))·mean_c(g_c/M), not the mean-atom margin that orders the walk) as a feasibility diagnostic, and the experiment grid maps this frontier over cluster counts.

**Threshold selection without multiplicity cost.** A grid of 23 thresholds in [0.55, 0.99] is walked in a fixed sequence (ordered by estimated certification margin on S_aux, largest first: α − mean_{c∈S_aux} Z_c(τ) = mean_c (g_c/M)·a_c(τ)·(α − e_c(τ)), each site's error gap weighted by its influence and answered fraction, so the order need not be monotone in τ; BBSE computes it on S_aux atoms reweighted at the point estimate ρ̂), each tested at the mode's full betting budget (δ for the baseline, δ_bet for BBSE), stopping at the first failure. The stopping rule makes the first false-certifiable threshold a single gate, so no δ-splitting across the grid is needed (fixed-sequence learn-then-test).

**Deployment is selected at three levels, and they do not conflict.** *Within a mode:* deploy the maximum-coverage threshold in the certified prefix — operationally the lowest certified τ. No pool is named for that comparison and none is needed: the answered set {x : s(x) ≥ τ} is nested and decreasing in τ, so the lowest certified threshold maximizes coverage on every pool simultaneously; it is a property of the grid, not a measurement. (Reported coverage is a separate quantity, measured on the target pool *after* τ* is fixed.) *Across modes:* deploy the most conservative certified threshold — the largest of the modes' own within-mode choices — so the deployed τ is never laxer than any certifying mode would have deployed alone. *Across rungs:* the operative α is the strictest certified one, a data-driven selection over the two-rung ladder whose selected claim holds jointly at 1−2δ.

**Budget ladder.** Both α ∈ {0.05, 0.10} are certified by separate walks and reported as a coverage-vs-α curve. Expectation stated up front and confirmed by the sweep experiment (E4): at the realistic 208-site scale (83 calibration clusters) α=0.10 is the operative rung (certifies every draw; the coverage figure lives in the E4 artifact and the draft) while α=0.05 is not yet reachable; α=0.10 becomes certifiable from ~150 sites upward, and the stricter α=0.05 budget requires substantially more sites — first appearing around 300 and reliable by 400. The information floor scaling as 1/n_cal is what sets this frontier, which E4 maps directly.

## 5. Assumption modes

**Baseline (exchangeable).** Target site exchangeable with calibration sites; weights ≡ 1. Tag: exchangeability (emitted as "Under the tagged assumption (exchangeability)").

**Label shift (BBSE).** Only outcome prevalence differs; P(x|y) invariant. Black-box shift estimation: with c0 = P(ŷ=1|y=0), c1 = P(ŷ=1|y=1) estimated on S_aux and q the target *population* predicted-positive rate (a record-level rate: for a single declared site, that site's own rate; for ten or more declared sites, the record-pooled rate of the site population they were drawn from, which a Clopper–Pearson interval on the pooled count would under-cover — so for a multi-site pool π_t is likewise a record-pooled prevalence), target prevalence π_t solves q = c0(1−π_t) + c1π_t. Certification re-weights calibration records by class: w_i = ρ for y_i=1, 1 for y_i=0, with ρ the target/source odds ratio of the positive class. The source odds in ρ are those of the record-pooled prevalence π_rec = E[n_c π_c]/E[n_c], which the S_aux bootstrap estimates, while the estimand's class mix is influence-weighted, π_g = E[g_c π_c]/E[g_c]: the ρ-reweighted site population's influence-weighted prevalence has odds ρ·π_g/(1−π_g), so the certified risk sits at π_t exactly where π_g = π_rec — as in the synthetic generator, where site size is drawn independently of site prevalence — and otherwise at that shifted prevalence: conservative when π_g > π_rec and answered positives err more often than answered negatives (P(x|y) site-invariant), anti-conservative when π_g < π_rec under that ordering.

Estimation uncertainty is part of the guarantee, for **all four** estimated parameters. The observed target rate q̂ is a noisy estimate of q — the target pool is finite — and treating it as known issues false certificates on small pools (we measured up to 3×δ before this correction), so q receives its own confidence share: an exact two-sided Clopper–Pearson interval at level δ_conf/4 for a single-site pool (finite-sample for records iid within the declared site), or a percentile cluster bootstrap over target sites for a pool declaring ten or more sites (asymptotic). (c0, c1, π_source) get a **cluster bootstrap over S_aux sites** (2,000 valid resamples; percentile intervals at Bonferroni δ_conf/4). The four intervals form a box propagated to [ρ_lo, ρ_hi] over its 16 corners — with π_t and π_s clipped to [1e-4, 1−1e-4], ρ is monotone in each box parameter on the gated region, so the corner extremes bound it over the whole box; the clip can exclude odds ratios implied by box points whose target or source prevalence lies outside that range, but the interval covers the true ρ whenever the box covers the true parameters and both true prevalences lie in [1e-4, 1−1e-4]; certification then tests both ρ endpoints at δ_bet. Soundness of the endpoint reduction: the certified statistic is scale-invariant in the record weights, so sign(E[Z(ρ)] − α) = sign(A + ρB) with (A, B) free of ρ — affine in ρ even though the two endpoint atom sets use different normalizations w_max = max(1, ρ), under which the atom mean itself is piecewise with a kink at ρ=1. The certifiable set {ρ : E[Z(ρ)] ≤ α} is therefore convex, so both endpoints passing covers every interior ρ, and any ρ in the box with E[Z(ρ)] > α forces an endpoint whose level-δ_bet test bounds false certification (verified numerically in the test suite on the deployed normalization, including an interval straddling ρ=1); δ_conf + δ_bet = δ restores 1−δ by union bound, with δ_conf spent across the four-parameter box (the S_aux bootstrap intervals plus the target-pool q interval). **Disclosure:** the q interval is finite-sample (Clopper–Pearson) for single-site pools, but the three S_aux percentile bootstrap intervals are asymptotic — as is the q interval for a pool declaring ten or more sites, so all four are asymptotic there — and their finite-cluster error is material at the design's own scale: with the head held fixed so (c0, c1, π_s) are exact population constants, we measure the realized joint miscoverage of the three-parameter box at roughly two to three times its nominal budget at auxiliary-site counts bracketing the design's. The probe's configuration and numbers are recorded in SI A.2 and in `experiments/out-bbse-probe/` (post-hoc; head fixed, one declared target site, 400 fits at each of 36, 42 and 74 auxiliary sites; the multi-site mode is outside the probe): the four-parameter box misses at 0.042–0.056 against a nominal 0.025, so the box exceeds its budget. The union bound, however, consumes only the event ρ ∈ [ρ_lo, ρ_hi], of which box coverage is a sufficient condition, and the propagated interval covered the declared site's odds ratio in all 1,163 certifiable fits (exact 95% upper end of the miss 0.0032), so in the probe's regime the certificate level is, by measurement rather than proof, at least 1 − (0.0096 + 0.025) ≈ 0.965 at each auxiliary-site count (exact 95% upper ends 0.0094–0.0096) and 1 − (0.0032 + 0.025) = 0.9718 pooled; charging every box miss instead, 1 − (box miss + δ_bet) ≈ 0.93 (0.914 at the upper end of the pooled interval), is a looser lower bound on that level, not the level itself. Every BBSE guarantee statement carries the asymptotic caveat, and the printed "≥ 0.95" should be read as resting on this measurement rather than on the box's nominal budget, and as unmeasured by any released probe outside its regime (multi-site pools — for which §9 cites an unreleased audit Monte Carlo — larger target pools, other heads, other auxiliary-site counts); replacing the percentile box with a finite-sample cluster bound (inverting the same WSR machinery) is planned and would retire the caveat for single-site pools; the multi-site q interval would need the same replacement. Declines (never fallbacks): (i) box denominator gap worst-case (c1−c0) < 0.10 ⇒ "ill-conditioned"; (ii) fewer than 2,000 valid resamples within 4,000 attempts (degenerate pool) ⇒ decline, never quantiles over a silently reduced draw count; (iii) the q interval not wholly inside the box's [c0_lo, c1_hi] range ⇒ bbse-misspecified; corners that still imply a prevalence outside (0,1) are clipped to [1e-4, 1−1e-4], not declined, and a fit whose clipped box certifies at neither endpoint resolves as failsafe; (iv) an empty target pool ⇒ decline; (v) a multi-site target pool declaring 2–9 sites (fewer than 10) ⇒ "bbse-target-clustering", since a percentile bootstrap over so few clusters cannot approach nominal coverage; (vi) an S_aux split in which fewer than ten sites carry a positive, or fewer than ten carry a negative ⇒ "bbse-aux-clustering" (post-hoc, 2026-10-29), before any resampling: c1 is resampled only across positive-carrying sites and c0 only across negative-carrying ones, so a single such site makes every resample reproduce the point estimate and the interval has zero width. That floor removes the degenerate regime only; the asymptotic shortfall disclosed above is unchanged, and no released fit moves: a replay of every synthetic label-shift stream found at least 28 auxiliary sites carrying each class, and a 20-re-split eICU re-run on the restricted extract reproduced all 20 released fits exactly. No shrinkage, no fallback-to-uncorrected, anywhere. Budgeting q makes BBSE strictly more conservative on small pools — it declines rather than certify on target-pool noise, which is the correct behavior given the false-certification rate measured above.

**Combination.** Modes run as alternatives, each spending its own betting budget (baseline δ, BBSE δ_bet — NOT "full δ each"); deploy the most conservative certified threshold, i.e. the larger of the two modes' within-mode maximum-coverage choices (§4, three-level rule); a mode is listed in the OR-guarantee only if it certified the deployed threshold itself. The guarantee holds if either tagged assumption holds (emitted as "under the tagged assumption (exchangeability or label shift)"). Mode agreement is not confirmation.

**What no mode covers.** Concept shift (a change in P(y|x) that neither tag accounts for — label shift itself moves P(y|x), by ln ρ on the log-odds scale, and exchangeability admits the between-site variation calibration sampled) is invisible to any unlabeled-data method; under it the certificate can be confidently wrong. We do not paper over this: experiment E3 injects concept shift *verified capable of producing violations* and shows the certificate failing — evidence that the assumption tags are load-bearing, and the honest boundary of the method.

## 6. Explainability (answers *and* abstentions)

The deployed head is L2-regularized logistic regression (C=1.0), chosen so the answer/abstain decision is a transparent linear function of the features rather than a black box; a stronger black-box model can be swapped in at a measured cost in coverage, which the gate makes visible (validity never depends on model quality or calibration; the operating point does — the threshold grid is fixed in score units, so even an order-preserving rescaling such as temperature scaling can move it).

- **Global:** standardized coefficients w_j, the head's coefficients on z_j = (x_j − μ_j)/sd_j, with μ_j the S_train mean of feature j and sd_j its S_train standard deviation, set to 1 where that is at or below SD_REL_TOL·max(1, |μ_j|) (the guarded standardization) — direction and strength of each feature.
- **Local (answered cases):** exact additive attributions φ_j(x) = w_j·z_j, with logit(p̂(x)) = base + Σ_j φ_j and base the head's intercept (the logit at x = μ) — for a linear model these are the exact **interventional** Shapley values with the training-mean baseline (the Linear SHAP result), no approximation or sampling. The value function matters: under correlated features the *conditional* Shapley values differ from these, and the efficiency identity alone does not distinguish the two.
- **Abstention explanations (declined cases):** answering requires |logit p̂(x)| ≥ logit τ*; we report the margin-to-answer m(x) = logit τ* − |logit p̂(x)| > 0 and the signed feature attributions of the confidence deficit — "declined because feature A pulls toward positive while features B, C pull toward negative, leaving confidence below the certified bar." Cohort level: attribution profiles of answered vs declined populations identify systematic abstention drivers.
- **Contrastive abstention counterfactuals (declined cases):** the same linearity gives a closed-form answer to "what is the smallest change that would make this case answerable?" — with s = sign(logit p̂(x)) the case's current side, the minimal standardized-L2 move is s·m(x)·w/‖w‖² (no smaller move flips, in any direction), and the single-feature counterfactual is Δz_j = s·m(x)/w_j (∞ where w_j = 0), i.e. Δx_j = sd_j·Δz_j in raw units. The reported distances are those exact minima; the emitted deltas carry 10⁻⁹ of logit-space headroom so the flip holds under the deployed floating-point answering rule (a delta landing exactly on the bar is declined by that rule on a measurable fraction of cases), and each flip is re-verified against that rule with no tolerance. The resulting answer carries the predicted class of the current side at the weakest answerable confidence — τ*, within ~3·10⁻¹⁰ — and is stated as such. These are score-space recourse statements about the gate, not causal or clinically achievable actions: features are not independently manipulable (a missingness indicator cannot "move 0.4"), and the artifact answers *what the gate would need*, never *what the clinician should do*.
- **Composition transparency:** answered-set class composition reported three ways — predicted-class (estimated), BBSE-implied true-class (estimated, assumption-tagged; the head's odds shifted from the S_train prevalence to the BBSE π̂_t), oracle (harness only) — the instrument that reveals whether a certificate was earned by answering only easy negatives on a 9–10%-prevalence task. The post-hoc panel's composition block is the CI-bearing three-scope (answered / declined / all) sibling of this point estimate; the two report the same answered predicted-positive fraction and a test pins their agreement.
- **Skill decomposition (post-hoc, added 2026-08-01):** at single-digit prevalence an α near the prevalence is close to what a constant always-negative rule achieves, so meeting the budget is not by itself evidence the gate is doing anything (§"Loss"). The panel reports, per scope, the model error rate against the constant-majority baseline's error rate and their difference (skill_margin), plus the answered-minus-all and answered-minus-declined contrasts as **single paired statistics inside one shared resample** (differencing two marginal interval endpoints ignores their dependence, is typically far wider, and carries no coverage claim). A certificate earned by answering only easy negatives shows a skill margin at or near zero while its answered error rate looks excellent. Every interval in the panel is a one-stage cluster bootstrap over sites and is **marginal**: there is no joint-coverage claim across the panel's statistics.

## 7. Validation protocol (oracle labels, synthetic harness)

The conformance metric matches the certified estimand (it must — scoring a population-average guarantee against single-site draws tests a claim the certificate does not make):

1. **Aggregate conformance:** per calibration draw, the certified τ is applied to a fresh 200-site evaluation pool and the influence-weighted answered risk R_M is computed on it; conformance = fraction of certified draws whose pool R_M exceeds α, target ≤ δ. This scores the estimand the betting test bounds but is not the bounded quantity itself, in two respects. (a) The test bounds the joint probability, over the calibration draw, that a rung certifies while the population R_M is at or above α; conformance estimates the conditional probability given certification, which equals the joint one when every draw certifies and is otherwise bounded only by δ divided by the certify probability (the joint rate is conformance × certify rate), so holding the conditional rate to ≤ δ is the stricter target. (b) The pool R_M is a finite-sample estimate of the population R_M, with sampling noise of its own (standard deviation about 0.002 at the E1 operating point, against a margin of about 0.043 below α = 0.10) that can move a draw near the boundary to either side of α.
2. **Per-site dispersion diagnostics (no δ target):** the single-fresh-site hard-violation rate — violated only when the one-sided 95% Wilson lower confidence bound on that site's answered error exceeds α — together with the fraction of answering evaluation-pool sites (at least one answered record) whose own answered error exceeds α. These measure what the certificate deliberately does not bound; under heterogeneity they rise while the aggregate stays within budget (E1's s_u arm reports both side by side).
3. **Realized exceedance rate:** raw fraction of pools with answered error > α, reported as a diagnostic against its binomial reference curve, stratified by answered-set size bins [0, 30), [30, 100), [100, 300), [300, ∞) (small answered sets exceed α by luck at binomial rates; a batch whose records err independently at exactly α exceeds it at a rate that oscillates for small sets and approaches 50% as batches grow, while a single-site batch from a heterogeneous site population can exceed at a different rate even when the certified average is exactly α — a naive exceedance count would condemn a valid system).

The Wilson criterion in (2) is deliberately conservative — it protects a valid system from false condemnation. Meeting the ≤ δ target in (1) evidences the absence of gross violations at the tested power, rather than confirming validity against arbitrarily small excesses; the guarantee itself rests on the finite-sample level of the betting test (§4), not on this harness.

## 8. Experiments (grid in `experiments/run_synthetic.py`)

- **E1 Validity (in-distribution):** R = 200 calibration draws × exchangeable targets; certify rate, coverage, aggregate R_M conformance on fresh 200-site pools (target ≤ δ), per-site dispersion diagnostics, exceedance vs reference; plus an s_u ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} heterogeneity arm showing the aggregate holding while per-site exceedances grow.
- **E2 Label shift:** site-level base rate 0.095 → 0.22 (population prevalence 0.104 → 0.231) under the same generator as every other experiment (sep = 2.2); uncorrected baseline certifies and violates (the failure that motivates the correction); BBSE certifies-or-declines with violations ≤ δ — with the q budget it declines heavily on small single-site pools, which is the corrected behavior.
- **E3 Concept-shift negative control:** posterior tilt verified (by construction check) to push true answered risk above α at certifiable coverage; certificates violate — demonstrating the tag is load-bearing; reported openly.
- **E4 Cluster-count sweep:** n_sites ∈ {60, 100, 150, 208, 300, 400} × both α rungs → the feasibility frontier (certify rate and coverage vs cluster count), the paper's capacity-planning figure.
- **E5 Explainability case studies:** global coefficients; local attributions for representative answered/declined/near-threshold cases; abstention-driver cohort profile.
- **E6 Site-level fairness table:** per-site coverage and answered error, stratified by site size; answered-set composition three ways; and (POST-HOC, added 2026-08-01) a selective reliability panel over the same answered/declined partition — reliability curve and plug-in ECE on fixed a-priori probability bins, a weak-calibration slope/intercept pair, the Brier score, and a constant-majority skill decomposition, every interval a one-stage cluster bootstrap over sites.
- **E7 Record-as-unit comparator (added 2026-07-30, panel S1-13):** the identical betting machinery with each *record* as one atom (g_i = 1, no cap, 2,000-record subsample), scored against the same influence-weighted fresh-pool estimand over the same draws at s_u ∈ {0.5, 2.0} — the in-harness demonstration of the record-level overconfidence the design premise cites: it grants the stricter rung the site-unit walk refuses, then violates its aggregate budget at roughly 2δ under heterogeneity (numbers: `experiments/out/summary.md` E7 block, draft §4.8).

Beyond the synthetic grid, the same pipeline runs on eICU-CRD v2.0 under the pre-frozen real-data protocol (`EICU-PROTOCOL.md`; runner `experiments/run_eicu.py`; results in the paper's Section 4.10) — that arm is specified by the protocol document, not by this section.

## 9. Limitations (stated, not solved)

The guarantee is a site-population average: individual sites are not bounded, per-site dispersion under heterogeneity is measured (E1 s_u arm) but not controlled, and a per-site guarantee would need a quantile-tolerance test costing substantially more clusters. Concept shift undetectable from unlabeled data (E3 is the honest demonstration); covariate-shift importance weighting excluded because a design-time estimate puts certification out of reach below ~400 calibration clusters at practical clip caps (the paper states only the conclusion; the mechanism: weights clipped at C enter the atoms as w/C to stay in [0, 1], as in the weighted path of `influence_atoms`, which divides the atom margin m by C while the §4 floor does not move, so the margin reaches the floor only when n_cal ≥ C·ln(1/δ)(1−α)/m — with the weights averaging one over the calibration sites m ≤ α, so at C = 5 even a perfect head stays below the floor under 135 calibration clusters at α = 0.10 and under 285 at α = 0.05; the C and m behind ~400 are not recorded in this repository, so the figure is an estimate, not a derived bound); no out-of-support screen (confidence gate + BBSE carry robustness; a kNN screen is future work); temporal common-shock correlation across the shared collection window not modeled; the certification package never imputes — features must arrive finite, and a missing value is rejected loudly at the input boundary; for eICU the extract-to-feature step (`experiments/eicu_etl.py`) fills a missing numeric with the S_train column mean beside a per-column missingness indicator, so the head can read missingness as a signal while the certified path sees only finite inputs — and there is no dedicated positivity diagnostic. The BBSE (c0, c1, π_s) percentile bootstrap intervals are asymptotic with measured joint under-coverage at the design scale (§5) — disclosed wherever the guarantee is stated; the q interval is finite-sample for single-site pools and an asymptotic percentile cluster bootstrap otherwise, with pools of 2–9 declared target sites declined outright ("bbse-target-clustering") because a percentile bootstrap over so few clusters cannot approach nominal coverage; on the same reasoning (post-hoc, 2026-10-29), an S_aux split with fewer than ten sites carrying a positive, or fewer than ten carrying a negative, declines ("bbse-aux-clustering"). Ten or more declared target sites make the q bootstrap admissible, not exact: an audit Monte Carlo at the synthetic generator's defaults puts its per-parameter miss of the site-population rate at about 2.5–10 times nominal for 10–40 declared sites, while the propagated ρ interval still covered the population odds ratio in 2,398 of 2,400 fits (CODE-AUDIT.md, multisite-q). The post-hoc panel's expected calibration error is a **biased plug-in estimate on fixed bins**: the absolute value inflates each bin's gap, an upward bias that grows as per-bin counts shrink and dominates near calibration, while the record total in its denominator varies with the sites drawn and adds a bias of order 1/K over K sites, of either sign — negative when the estimate rises with that total, as when large gaps sit in the sites contributing the most records — so the net bias is not always upward. A percentile interval does not correct bias — the interval covers the biased plug-in estimand, not true calibration error (the panel emits this as `notes[2]`, whose frozen wording names only the upward term, and it must not be quietly dropped when the number is quoted). Data-quality preconditions are enforced, not assumed: site identity is canonicalized with near-duplicate ids rejected loudly (a dirty site column would otherwise fabricate independent clusters and buy unearned certification strength), the target pool is asserted site-disjoint from every fitting split, and multi-site target pools must declare their clustering.
