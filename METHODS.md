# METHODS — paper-ready specification

Written to become the paper's Methods section with minimal editing. Notation: sites (clusters) indexed c; records i; binary outcome y ∈ {0,1}; features x; risk score s(x) ∈ [0.5, 1] (max-softmax of a probabilistic classifier); threshold τ; error budget α; confidence 1−δ.

## 1. Problem setting

Multi-site clinical data: ~200 collection sites contributing 20–5,000 records each, outcome prevalence ~9–10%, site-level heterogeneity in prevalence and case mix. A deployed risk model answers a case only when its confidence clears a threshold; we want a **certificate**: with probability ≥ 1−δ over the draw of calibration sites, the influence-weighted error rate among answered cases, **averaged over the population of sites** from which the calibration sites were drawn, is ≤ α — under a stated, tagged assumption about how deployment differs from calibration. This is a site-population-average guarantee, not a per-site one; §3 states exactly what that does and does not bound.

**Clinical target, decision rule, loss.** The certified quantity is an error rate, so three things must be supplied by the deployment and are taken as given. *Outcome:* y ∈ {0,1} pinned by an index event, a prediction time at which x is frozen, and an ascertainment window; CertGate certifies whatever binary label the caller supplies and does not itself resolve censoring, competing risks, or a time-to-event horizon. *Decision:* ŷ_i = 1[p̂(x_i) ≥ 1/2]; err_i = 1[ŷ_i ≠ y_i], the quantity entering the atom of §3. The selective score s(x) = max(p̂, 1−p̂) is symmetric about p̂ = 1/2, so the gate withholds cases near the decision boundary in either direction and adds no clinical threshold of its own. *Loss:* err_i is symmetric 0–1 — FN and FP weigh equally and α budgets their sum. At single-digit prevalence most answered errors are FN (measured 77% in E1), and an α set near the prevalence is close to what a constant always-negative rule achieves, so meeting the budget is not by itself evidence the gate is doing anything: the answered/declined operating characteristics and the composition instrument (§6) carry that evidence. A cost-weighted loss needs a different atom and is out of scope.

Records within a site share equipment, populations, and practice patterns; the honest unit of independence is the **site**. All guarantees here are cluster-level: ~80 site-level observations, not ~10⁵ record-level ones. This is the load-bearing design decision and the reason naive record-level selective prediction is silently overconfident on multi-site data.

## 2. Data discipline

Sites are split disjointly: **S_train 40%** (fit the risk model), **S_aux 20%** (threshold ordering, label-shift correction machinery, feasibility diagnostics), **S_cal 40%** (certification only — it enters the *certified path* only through the certification walks; the estimated and diagnostic report tiers also read it, downstream of and without feedback into the certificate). Every fitted object is a function of (S_train, S_aux, target features) only; the code enforces split disjointness with a hard assertion at pipeline entry. All constants below are fixed a priori in `constants.py` and pinned to their literal values by a unit test, so any drift fails CI — a lightweight, verifiable version of pre-registration.

## 3. Certified estimand

Site sizes are heavy-tailed, so an unweighted mean over records would let one giant site dominate; capping realized contributions is provably anti-conservative (a 17.5%-true-risk construction certifies at α=5% under naive truncation — kept as a regression test). Instead each site gets a data-independent **influence weight** g_c = min(n_c, M), M = 100, and the certified quantity is the **influence-weighted answered-set risk**: R_M ≤ α where R_M = Σ_c g_c a_c e_c / Σ_c g_c a_c (a_c = answered fraction, e_c = answered-set error rate at site c). The cap acts on influence only; it scales each site's whole (signed) contribution and never censors bad news.

Certification is via the linearized per-site atom
Z_c = (g_c / (M·n_c)) · Σ_{i∈c} ans_i·(err_i − α) + α ∈ [0, 1],
so E[Z] ≤ α ⟺ R_M ≤ α. Sites with zero answered-eligible records enter as neutral atoms Z_c = α (never dropped — dropping redefines the site population post hoc); the minimum-cluster gate counts only **record-carrying** sites.

**Scope (what is and is not bounded).** E[Z] is an expectation over the *site draw*: the certificate bounds the influence-weighted answered risk averaged over the site population, with the probability statement over the calibration draw. Two things it deliberately does **not** bound. (i) *Individual sites*: under between-site heterogeneity a site's own answered risk can exceed α while the population average stays within budget; the fraction of sites doing so is governed by the between-site dispersion, which the certificate neither measures nor bounds (E1's sensitivity arm quantifies this: raising the site random-effect s_u from 0.5 to 2.0 leaves the certified aggregate within budget while per-site exceedances grow). A per-site guarantee would require a tolerance bound on an upper quantile of the per-site risk distribution — a different, more cluster-hungry test. (ii) *Realized counts*: the bound is not a bound on any single batch's realized error count, which exceeds α at binomial-dispersion rates even under a valid certificate — the validation protocol (§7) is built around this distinction. In the exchangeable mode the certificate is a single function of the calibration draw, shared by every target pool it is applied to (one 1−δ event per draw); in BBSE mode the fit depends on the target pool through q, so certificates are per-pool. The operative rung is the strictest certified α — a data-driven selection over the two-rung ladder, so the selected claim holds jointly at 1−2δ.

## 4. Certified test: betting on the calibration sites

We test H0: E[Z] ≥ α with the Waudby-Smith–Ramdas betting martingale: wealth K_t = Π_{s≤t} (1 + λ_s(α − Z_s)) with predictable λ_t = min( sqrt(2 ln(1/δ) / (σ̂²_{t−1} n)), 0.9/(1−α) ), variance floor 1e-8, running (μ̂, σ̂²) initialized (0.5, 0.25); certify iff sup_t K_t ≥ 1/δ (Ville's inequality gives finite-sample level δ). Cluster order is a deterministic permutation seeded by sha256 of (protocol seed, α index, mode index, and — for the BBSE endpoint walks only — the endpoint discriminator) — reproducible and free of any target identifier, so one calibration draw yields one baseline certificate shared by every target pool. At n clusters no valid level-δ test certifies with margin below the information floor ln(1/δ)(1−α)/n; we report margin/floor as a feasibility diagnostic, and the experiment grid maps this frontier over cluster counts.

**Threshold selection without multiplicity cost.** A grid of 23 thresholds in [0.55, 0.99] is walked in a fixed sequence (ordered by estimated certification margin on S_aux, most conservative first), each tested at the mode's full betting budget (δ for the baseline, δ_bet for BBSE), stopping at the first failure. The stopping rule makes the first false-certifiable threshold a single gate, so no δ-splitting across the grid is needed (fixed-sequence learn-then-test).

**Deployment is selected at three levels, and they do not conflict.** *Within a mode:* deploy the maximum-coverage threshold in the certified prefix — operationally the lowest certified τ. No pool is named for that comparison and none is needed: the answered set {x : s(x) ≥ τ} is nested and decreasing in τ, so the lowest certified threshold maximizes coverage on every pool simultaneously; it is a property of the grid, not a measurement. (Reported coverage is a separate quantity, measured on the target pool *after* τ* is fixed.) *Across modes:* deploy the most conservative certified threshold — the largest of the modes' own within-mode choices — so the deployed τ is never laxer than any certifying mode would have deployed alone. *Across rungs:* the operative α is the strictest certified one, a data-driven selection over the two-rung ladder whose selected claim holds jointly at 1−2δ.

**Budget ladder.** Both α ∈ {0.05, 0.10} are certified by separate walks and reported as a coverage-vs-α curve. Expectation stated up front and confirmed by the sweep experiment (E4): at the realistic 208-site scale (~83 calibration clusters) α=0.10 is the operative rung (certifies every draw at ~0.97 coverage) while α=0.05 is not yet reachable; α=0.10 becomes certifiable from ~150 sites upward, and the stricter α=0.05 budget requires substantially more sites — first appearing around 300 and reliable by 400. The information floor scaling as 1/n_cal is what sets this frontier, which E4 maps directly.

## 5. Assumption modes

**Baseline (exchangeable).** Target site exchangeable with calibration sites; weights ≡ 1. Tag: "if exchangeability holds."

**Label shift (BBSE).** Only outcome prevalence differs; P(x|y) invariant. Black-box shift estimation: with c0 = P(ŷ=1|y=0), c1 = P(ŷ=1|y=1) estimated on S_aux and q the target *population* predicted-positive rate, target prevalence π_t solves q = c0(1−π_t) + c1π_t. Certification re-weights calibration records by class: w_i = ρ for y_i=1, 1 for y_i=0, with ρ the target/source odds ratio of the positive class.

Estimation uncertainty is part of the guarantee, for **all four** estimated parameters. The observed target rate q̂ is a noisy estimate of q — the target pool is finite — and treating it as known issues false certificates on small pools (we measured up to 3×δ before this correction), so q receives its own confidence share: an exact two-sided Clopper–Pearson interval at level δ_conf/4 for a single-site pool (finite-sample), or a cluster bootstrap over target sites for a multi-site pool. (c0, c1, π_source) get a **cluster bootstrap over S_aux sites** (2,000 valid resamples; percentile intervals at Bonferroni δ_conf/4). The four intervals form a box propagated to [ρ_lo, ρ_hi] over its 16 corners — ρ is coordinate-wise monotone on the gated region, so corner extremes cover the interior, and the π_t clip only widens the interval; certification then tests both ρ endpoints at δ_bet. Soundness of the endpoint reduction: the certified statistic is scale-invariant in the record weights, so sign(E[Z(ρ)] − α) = sign(A + ρB) with (A, B) free of ρ — affine in ρ even though the two endpoint atom sets use different normalizations w_max = max(1, ρ), under which the atom mean itself is piecewise with a kink at ρ=1. The certifiable set {ρ : E[Z(ρ)] ≤ α} is therefore convex, so both endpoints passing covers every interior ρ, and any ρ in the box with E[Z(ρ)] > α forces an endpoint whose level-δ_bet test bounds false certification (verified numerically in the test suite on the deployed normalization, including an interval straddling ρ=1); δ_conf + δ_bet = δ restores 1−δ by union bound, with δ_conf spent across the four-parameter box (the S_aux bootstrap intervals plus the target-pool q interval). **Disclosure:** the q interval is finite-sample (Clopper–Pearson) for single-site pools, but the three percentile bootstrap intervals are asymptotic, and their finite-cluster error is material at the design's own scale: with the head held fixed so (c0, c1, π_s) are exact population constants, we measure the realized joint miscoverage of the three-parameter box at **0.038 against a nominal ≤ 0.019** (joint coverage 0.962 vs 0.981 nominal; 400 replicates at both 42 and 83 auxiliary sites, per-parameter miss ~0.010–0.015 against nominal 0.003). Every BBSE guarantee statement carries the asymptotic caveat, and the printed "≥ 0.95" should be read with this measured shortfall in mind; replacing the percentile box with a finite-sample cluster bound (inverting the same WSR machinery) is planned and would retire the caveat. Declines (never fallbacks): (i) box denominator gap worst-case (c1−c0) < 0.10 ⇒ "ill-conditioned"; (ii) fewer than 2,000 valid resamples within 4,000 attempts (degenerate pool) ⇒ decline, never quantiles over a silently reduced draw count; (iii) the q interval not wholly inside the box's [c0_lo, c1_hi] range (BBSE misspecified / implied prevalence outside (0,1)) ⇒ decline; (iv) an empty target pool ⇒ decline. No shrinkage, no fallback-to-uncorrected, anywhere. Budgeting q makes BBSE strictly more conservative on small pools — it declines rather than certify on target-pool noise, which is the correct behavior: before the correction, those extra certificates were false at up to 3×δ.

**Combination.** Modes run as alternatives, each spending its own betting budget (baseline δ, BBSE δ_bet — NOT "full δ each"); deploy the most conservative certified threshold, i.e. the larger of the two modes' within-mode maximum-coverage choices (§2, three-level rule); a mode is listed in the OR-guarantee only if it certified the deployed threshold itself. Guarantee reads: "if either tagged assumption holds…". Mode agreement is not confirmation.

**What no mode covers.** Concept shift (P(y|x) changes) is invisible to any unlabeled-data method; under it the certificate can be confidently wrong. We do not paper over this: experiment E3 injects concept shift *verified capable of producing violations* and shows the certificate failing — evidence that the assumption tags are load-bearing, and the honest boundary of the method.

## 6. Explainability (answers *and* abstentions)

The deployed head is L2-regularized logistic regression (C=1.0) — intrinsically interpretable by design; a stronger black-box model can be swapped in at a measured cost in coverage, which the gate makes visible (the score only ranks; validity never depends on model quality or calibration).

- **Global:** standardized coefficients w_j·sd_j(S_train) — direction and strength of each feature.
- **Local (answered cases):** exact additive attributions φ_j(x) = w_j(x_j − μ_j), with logit(p̂(x)) = base + Σ_j φ_j — for a linear model these are the exact **interventional** Shapley values with the training-mean baseline (the Linear SHAP result), no approximation or sampling. The value function matters: under correlated features the *conditional* Shapley values differ from these, and the efficiency identity alone does not distinguish the two.
- **Abstention explanations (declined cases):** answering requires |logit p̂(x)| ≥ logit τ*; we report the margin-to-answer m(x) = logit τ* − |logit p̂(x)| > 0 and the signed feature attributions of the confidence deficit — "declined because feature A pulls toward positive while features B, C pull toward negative, leaving confidence below the certified bar." Cohort level: attribution profiles of answered vs declined populations identify systematic abstention drivers.
- **Contrastive abstention counterfactuals (declined cases):** the same linearity gives a closed-form answer to "what is the smallest change that would make this case answerable?" — with s = sign(logit p̂(x)) the case's current side, the minimal standardized-L2 move is s·m(x)·w/‖w‖² (no smaller move flips, in any direction), and the single-feature counterfactual is Δz_j = s·m(x)/w_j (∞ where w_j = 0). The reported distances are those exact minima; the emitted deltas carry 10⁻⁹ of logit-space headroom so the flip holds under the deployed floating-point answering rule (a delta landing exactly on the bar is declined by that rule on a measurable fraction of cases), and each flip is re-verified against that rule with no tolerance. The resulting answer carries the predicted class of the current side at the weakest answerable confidence — τ*, within ~3·10⁻¹⁰ — and is stated as such. These are score-space recourse statements about the gate, not causal or clinically achievable actions: features are not independently manipulable (a missingness indicator cannot "move 0.4"), and the artifact answers *what the gate would need*, never *what the clinician should do*.
- **Composition transparency:** answered-set class composition reported three ways — predicted-class (estimated), BBSE-implied true-class (estimated, assumption-tagged), oracle (harness only) — the instrument that reveals whether a certificate was earned by answering only easy negatives on a 9–10%-prevalence task.

## 7. Validation protocol (oracle labels, synthetic harness)

The conformance metric matches the certified estimand (it must — scoring a population-average guarantee against single-site draws tests a claim the certificate does not make):

1. **Aggregate conformance:** per calibration draw, the certified τ is applied to a fresh 200-site evaluation pool and the influence-weighted answered risk R_M is computed on it; conformance = fraction of certified draws with R_M > α, target ≤ δ. This is the quantity the betting test bounds.
2. **Per-site dispersion diagnostic (no δ target):** the single-fresh-site hard-violation rate — violated only when the one-sided 95% Wilson lower confidence bound on that site's answered error exceeds α — together with the fraction of evaluation-pool sites whose own answered error exceeds α. These measure what the certificate deliberately does not bound; under heterogeneity they rise while the aggregate stays within budget (E1's s_u arm reports both side by side).
3. **Realized exceedance rate:** raw fraction of pools with answered error > α, reported as a diagnostic against its binomial reference curve, stratified by answered-set size bins {<30, 30–100, 100–300, >300} (small answered sets exceed α by luck at binomial rates; at the boundary the exceedance rate approaches 50% as batches grow — a naive exceedance count would condemn a valid system).

The Wilson criterion in (2) is deliberately conservative — it protects a valid system from false condemnation. Meeting the ≤ δ target in (1) evidences the absence of gross violations at the tested power, rather than confirming validity against arbitrarily small excesses; the guarantee itself rests on the finite-sample level of the betting test (§4), not on this harness.

## 8. Experiments (grid in `experiments/run_synthetic.py`)

- **E1 Validity (in-distribution):** R = 200 calibration draws × exchangeable targets; certify rate, coverage, aggregate R_M conformance on fresh 200-site pools (target ≤ δ), per-site dispersion diagnostics, exceedance vs reference; plus an s_u ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} heterogeneity arm showing the aggregate holding while per-site exceedances grow.
- **E2 Label shift:** site-level prevalence 0.095 → 0.22 under the same generator as every other experiment (sep = 2.2); uncorrected baseline certifies and violates (the failure that motivates the correction); BBSE certifies-or-declines with violations ≤ δ — with the q budget it declines heavily on small single-site pools, which is the corrected behavior.
- **E3 Concept-shift negative control:** posterior tilt verified (by construction check) to push true answered risk above α at certifiable coverage; certificates violate — demonstrating the tag is load-bearing; reported openly.
- **E4 Cluster-count sweep:** n_sites ∈ {60, 100, 150, 208, 300, 400} × both α rungs → the feasibility frontier (certify rate and coverage vs cluster count), the paper's capacity-planning figure.
- **E5 Explainability case studies:** global coefficients; local attributions for representative answered/declined/near-threshold cases; abstention-driver cohort profile.
- **E6 Site-level fairness table:** per-site coverage and answered error, stratified by site size; answered-set composition three ways.

## 9. Limitations (stated, not solved)

The guarantee is a site-population average: individual sites are not bounded, per-site dispersion under heterogeneity is measured (E1 s_u arm) but not controlled, and a per-site guarantee would need a quantile-tolerance test costing substantially more clusters. Concept shift undetectable from unlabeled data (E3 is the honest demonstration); covariate-shift importance weighting excluded because it provably cannot certify below ~400 clusters at practical clip caps (arithmetic in the paper); no out-of-support screen (confidence gate + BBSE carry robustness; a kNN screen is future work); temporal common-shock correlation across the shared collection window not modeled; missing feature values are rejected loudly at the input boundary (imputation is the caller's ETL responsibility — the pipeline itself performs none), with no dedicated positivity diagnostic. The BBSE (c0, c1, π_s) percentile bootstrap intervals are asymptotic with measured joint under-coverage at the design scale (0.962 realized vs 0.981 nominal, §5) — disclosed wherever the guarantee is stated; the q interval is finite-sample for single-site pools and a cluster bootstrap otherwise, with pools of 2–9 declared target sites declined outright ("bbse-target-clustering") because a percentile bootstrap over so few clusters cannot approach nominal coverage. Data-quality preconditions are enforced, not assumed: site identity is canonicalized with near-duplicate ids rejected loudly (a dirty site column would otherwise fabricate independent clusters and buy unearned certification strength), the target pool is asserted site-disjoint from every fitting split, and multi-site target pools must declare their clustering.
