# CertGate: finite-sample certified selective prediction for multi-site clinical risk models, with label-shift robustness and explainable abstention

Tony Zhang$^{1}$

$^{1}$ Department of Physiology and Pharmacology, Western University, London, Ontario, Canada

**Corresponding author:** Tony Zhang, tzhan659@uwo.ca

---

**Abstract**

**Background** Records cluster by hospital, so a record-level confidence guarantee is silently overconfident once a clinical risk model is deployed across institutions. **Methods** CertGate is a selective-prediction gate that answers a case only when confident and otherwise defers it to a clinician, and certifies — with finite-sample confidence $1-\delta$, treating the hospital rather than the record as the unit of independence — that the error rate among answered cases, averaged over the hospital population, stays at or below a budget $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$; the average does not bound individual hospitals. A label-shift mode budgets its correction's uncertainty inside the guarantee; a linear head attaches an exact attribution to every answer and abstention. **Results** On a 208-site synthetic cohort the $\alpha=0.10$ rung certifies all 200 replications without violation; under a prevalence shift the uncorrected gate violates in 39.5% of replications while the corrected mode declines; a deliberate concept shift defeats the certificate, as designed. On eICU-CRD v2.0 (164,322 stays, 207 hospitals), under a protocol fixed before the extract was read, $\alpha=0.10$ certifies on all 20 by-site re-splits at 0.890 coverage and the stricter rung is refused. The abstentions are stable and legible: the Glasgow motor score is the leading driver on every re-split, with oxygenation and airway status behind it. **Conclusions** A hospital-level certificate is practical at realistic scale, read with its disclosures: the answered set's mortality is 0.046 against 0.090 in its pool — part of the budget is met by abstaining where deaths concentrate, and disproportionately among the oldest patients.

**Keywords** Explainable AI · Trustworthy AI · Selective prediction · Distribution-free uncertainty quantification · Cluster-robust inference · Label shift · Explainable abstention · Clinical risk prediction · Intensive care (eICU-CRD)

# 1 Introduction

A clinical risk model rarely stays where it was built: a score calibrated on a handful of academic centers is deployed at a community hospital that contributed no training records, and it emits a probability for every patient regardless. A safer design wraps the model in a *selective gate* that answers the cases it is confident about and routes the rest to a clinician [@chow1970reject; @elyaniv2010selective]. The promise attached to such a gate is a high-confidence bound on the error rate among the answered cases — what a clinician weighs before trusting automated triage, and what an auditor asks to see documented. Under the way multi-site clinical data is actually distributed, that promise is often false, invisibly to the deployed system.

The object to certify is an *answered-set error rate* whose unit of independence is the **site** (hospital), not the record: records within a site share case mix, coding, and instrumentation, and a new site's prevalence is generally not the one calibration saw [@tripodcluster2023; @internalexternal2021]. A certificate treating records as exchangeable overruns its stated confidence once deployment is grouped by site [@zhou2026falsesense; @triage2026audit]. Selective prediction, cluster-as-unit distribution-free inference, and label-shift correction each have mature literatures (Section 2); multi-site deployment needs all three at once.

We present CertGate, a selective-prediction gate for this setting. The certificate treats the site as the atom of inference: each hospital's calibration data is compressed into one bounded summary, and a sequential "betting" test — the Waudby-Smith–Ramdas (WSR) martingale, which accumulates evidence hospital by hospital and certifies only when it crosses a preset bar [@waudbysmith2024betting; @ville1939] — decides whether the site-level mean error is within budget. The operating threshold comes from walking an ordered grid of candidates in a fixed sequence, which spends no extra confidence on the multiple looks [@angelopoulos2021ltt; @westfall2001fixedsequence], in the risk-control tradition [@bates2021rcps; @angelopoulos2024crc]. The gate ships in two assumption modes, and the mode is part of the guarantee text: an exchangeable baseline, and a label-shift mode that drops the shared-prevalence assumption through black-box shift estimation (BBSE) [@lipton2018bbse; @garg2020unified], whose four estimated parameters carry their own uncertainty inside the certificate [@si2024pac]. Because the deployed head is linear by design, every answer and every abstention carries exact linear attributions [@lundberg2017shap], attaching explanation to a certified decision rather than to an ungoverned reject option [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain; @fernandes2026abductivereject]. One assumption stays deliberately outside the guarantee: concept shift — a change in $P(y\mid x)$ — is undetectable from unlabeled data, so we do not claim to survive it.

Section 4 evaluates the gate twice over: nine synthetic experiment families — a validity grid, a label-shift stress test, a concept-shift negative control, a site-count frontier, an attribution study, a coverage-and-composition study, a record-as-unit comparator, a certificate stress and comparator suite, and two power frontiers — on a 208-site cohort where ground truth is available by construction, then the same pipeline end to end on eICU-CRD v2.0 under a protocol frozen before the extract was read (Section 4.10), with the explanation layer read on the real cohort (Section 4.11). Which rung is reachable turns out to be a property of how many hospitals contribute, not of the method. A reader who wants the clinical content first needs Section 3.7 (what the certificate promises, in seven clauses), Section 4.10 (the real-data certificate and its disclosures) and Section 4.11 (what the abstentions point at); the comparator, stress and capacity arms (Sections 4.8, 4.12–4.14) are for the reader checking the statistics.

The primary contributions are:

1. **A site-as-unit certified selective-prediction gate.** A finite-sample $(\alpha, 1-\delta)$ certificate bounding the influence-weighted error rate among answered cases, averaged over the site population, with the site rather than the record as the unit of independence. Its necessity is demonstrated, not assumed: on the same draws the record-as-unit certifier grants the $\alpha = 0.05$ budget the site-unit walk refuses, then violates its aggregate budget at nearly twice $\delta$ under high heterogeneity (Section 4.8); the site-count frontier prices the stricter budget in hospitals (Sections 4.2, 4.5).
2. **A label-shift mode that carries its own uncertainty.** A correction based on black-box shift estimation folds the estimation uncertainty of all four corrected parameters into the $1-\delta$ budget through a box of cluster-robust confidence intervals, with the assumption mode named in the guarantee text; it certifies or declines rather than issue a certificate it cannot support — replacing a $39.5\%$-violation baseline (Section 4.3).
3. **Explained abstention on a certified gate, and what it shows on real data.** Each answer and abstention carries exact additive attributions under a named value function, including which features pushed a declined case below the confidence bar [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain; @fernandes2026abductivereject]. The attribution method is not new; what is measured is its behavior on a certified gate: on eICU the abstention driver is stable and clinically legible — the Glasgow motor score on all 20 re-splits, with oxygenation and airway status behind it — where the symmetric synthetic generator correctly yields none (Sections 4.6, 4.11), and the leading driver, unlike the ranking beneath it, survives the interventional-to-conditional value-function change.
4. **A validation design that can fail.** A concept-shift negative control verified capable of failing before it is run, and a protocol scoring the certificate against the estimand it actually claims (Section 4.4), answering a reported diagnosis of overconfident selective certificates [@zhou2026falsesense].

# 2 Related work

CertGate draws on four literatures that each solve part of the problem and stop one axis short.

*Certified selective prediction.* The reject option dates to Chow [@chow1970reject]; El-Yaniv and Wiener [@elyaniv2010selective] formalized the risk–coverage tradeoff, and Geifman and El-Yaniv [@geifman2017selective] turned it into a finite-sample selective-risk certificate on i.i.d. records, since sharpened by the distribution-free risk-control tradition [@bates2021rcps; @angelopoulos2021ltt; @angelopoulos2024crc], a 2025–26 wave of selective-risk work [@scrc2025; @score2026; @yu2026joint; @wang2026lec; @liu2026certifyrefuse], and betting-based tracking of a deployed model's risk — sequential monitoring rather than a one-shot certificate [@podkopaev2022tracking]. Yu and Liu [@yu2026joint] are closest — the same certificate shape, with an acceptance floor and a decline option — but, like the rest of the wave, over i.i.d. records; CertGate makes the site the unit of independence and carries a label-shift correction inside the budget.

*Conformal prediction and cluster exchangeability.* Conformal prediction [@vovk2005algorithmic] gives distribution-free coverage under an exchangeability that multi-site clustering breaks. Dunn, Wasserman and Ramdas [@dunn2023hierarchical] and Lee, Barber and Willett [@lee2025hierarchical] supply the cluster-as-unit machinery CertGate reuses; conformal e-values over hierarchical data reach selection with false-discovery control — a different error criterion, and no gate certificate [@lee2025hierarchicalevalues]; Farinhas et al. [@farinhas2024nonexchangeable] relax record exchangeability inside conformal risk control itself, though controlling risk in expectation; a hierarchical conformal framework has since reached multi-hospital length-of-stay intervals [@shahbazi2026hierarchical]; and Shahid [@fedcrc2026] makes sites first-class in a federated risk control. These deliver coverage or in-expectation risk, not a high-probability budget on the answered subset.

*Label shift.* Black-box shift estimation [@lipton2018bbse] corrects a moving prevalence by confusion-matrix inversion; regularized [@azizzadenesheli2019rlls], maximum-likelihood [@alexandari2020labelshift] and unified [@garg2020unified] variants refine the weights, and Podkopaev and Ramdas [@podkopaev2021labelshift] give reweighted-conformal coverage — all record-level point corrections. Abstention has been adapted to label shift without a guarantee [@alexandari2018abstention], and federated conformal prediction carries the correction across clients for coverage rather than answered-set risk [@plassier2023federatedlabelshift]. Si et al. [@si2024pac] are nearest, propagating confusion-matrix uncertainty into the guarantee rather than treating the correction as exact; CertGate adopts that pattern for a bound on answered-set risk, with the uncertainty made cluster-robust by a bootstrap over sites.

*Multi-site clinical validation and explainable abstention.* Clinical practice already treats the site as the unit — TRIPOD-Cluster [@tripodcluster2023] and internal–external cross-validation [@internalexternal2021] — but reports point estimates, not certificates. Two recent audits sharpen the gap: record-level selective-risk rules overrun their budget by 9–30% under grouped deployment [@zhou2026falsesense], and marginal coverage cannot certify a grouped release under shift [@triage2026audit]; conformal selective prediction with cost-aware deferral has meanwhile been proposed for clinical triage under distribution shift [@scireports2026deferral], and conformal prediction with SHAP explanations has been validated on eICU itself, descriptively [@cpmors2024jmir]. Reject-option explanation methods — up to provably minimal explanations of a linear model's rejections [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain; @l2lore2025; @fernandes2026abductivereject] — attach no statistical guarantee to the decision; CertGate's difference is exact attributions on a certified gate.

# 3 Methods

## 3.1 Problem setting: the site as the unit of independence

We consider multi-site clinical data: on the order of two hundred sites contributing 20 to 5,000 records each, outcome prevalence near 9–10%, sites differing in both prevalence and case mix. A deployed risk model produces a bounded score $s(x) \in [0.5, 1]$ — the model's confidence in its own call for the binary outcome $y \in \{0,1\}$, made precise below — and answers a case only when that score clears an operating threshold $\tau$; otherwise it abstains and defers to human judgment. The object we construct is a *certificate*: with probability at least $1-\delta$ over the draw of calibration sites, the influence-weighted answered-set risk — the parameter $R_M$ of Section 3.3 — *averaged over the population of sites from which the calibration sites were drawn* is at most $\alpha$, under an explicitly tagged assumption about how deployment differs from calibration. This is a site-population average, not a per-site promise: under between-site heterogeneity an individual site's answered risk can exceed $\alpha$ while the average stays inside budget, at a rate the certificate neither measures nor bounds (clauses in Section 3.7; dispersion measured in Section 4.2). Figure 1 lays out the pipeline.

The certified quantity is an *error* rate, so its meaning is fixed by what the deployment supplies: an index event, a prediction time at which the features freeze, and an ascertainment window resolving $y$; CertGate certifies whatever binary label the caller supplies and does not itself resolve censoring, competing risks, or a time horizon. The head's hard label is $\hat{y}_i = \mathbb{1}[\hat{p}(x_i) \ge 1/2]$, the per-record error is $\text{err}_i = \mathbb{1}[\hat{y}_i \neq y_i]$, and the selective score $s(x) = \max(\hat{p}(x), 1 - \hat{p}(x))$ withholds cases near the decision boundary in either direction. The loss is symmetric 0–1 — a modeling choice, and not a neutral one: at single-digit prevalence most answered errors are false negatives (77% on the validity grid of Section 4.2), and an error budget near the outcome prevalence is close to what a constant always-negative rule achieves — the operating characteristics of Section 4.2 and the composition instrument of Section 3.8 exist to make that visible. A cost-weighted loss would require a different atom (Section 6.1).

## 3.2 Data discipline and lightweight pre-registration

Sites are partitioned disjointly — the fractions count sites, not records — into $S_{\text{train}}$ (40%, fits the risk model), $S_{\text{aux}}$ (20%, orders the threshold walk and fits the label-shift machinery), and $S_{\text{cal}}$ (40%, certification only); every fitted object is a function of $(S_{\text{train}}, S_{\text{aux}}, \text{target features})$ alone, and a site appearing in two pools aborts the run — internal–external cross-validation discipline [@internalexternal2021]. All governing constants are fixed a priori and pinned literally by an automated test — a machine-verifiable substitute for third-party pre-registration; pinning is not justifying, so Supplementary Table S1 lists every frozen constant with its role and basis. The one constant that materially moves the certificate, the influence cap $M$, is swept in Section 4.9.

## 3.3 The certified estimand: influence-weighted answered-set risk

Site sizes are heavy-tailed, so an unweighted record mean would let a single large site dominate the certified quantity. The natural fix — capping each site's *realized* contribution — is demonstrably anti-conservative (a construction with 17.5% true risk certifies at $\alpha = 5\%$; Supplementary Information A.3). Instead, each site receives an *outcome-independent* influence weight $g_c = \min(n_c, M)$ with $M = 100$ — a function of site size only, never of errors — and the certified quantity is

$$R_M = \frac{\sum_c g_c\, a_c\, e_c}{\sum_c g_c\, a_c},$$

where $a_c$ is the answered fraction and $e_c$ the answered-set error rate at site $c$. The cap acts on influence only — it scales each site's whole signed contribution, never censoring the error itself. On this size distribution $89.5\%$ of sites exceed 100 records, so $R_M$ is close to an unweighted site average: a 5,000-record hospital and a 100-record one carry identical influence — a substantive choice about whose errors count, measured at other $M$ in Section 4.9.

Certification compresses each calibration hospital into one bounded number — its *atom* — carrying how far its answered error runs above or below budget:

$$Z_c = \frac{g_c}{M\, n_c}\sum_{i \in c} \text{ans}_i\,(\text{err}_i - \alpha) + \alpha \;\in\; [0, 1],$$

constructed so that $\mathbb{E}[Z] \le \alpha \iff R_M \le \alpha$: the inner sum collapses to $n_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)$, so $Z_c - \alpha = g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)/M$, tying each atom to the estimand term by term. The construction extends to class-conditional budgets: label-weighting the contributions ($\text{weights} = y$, cap unchanged) makes the certified quantity the influence-weighted false-negative rate among answered positives, boundedness and weight outcome-independence preserved; Section 4.14 measures where such budgets become issuable. Sites that answer nothing enter as *neutral* atoms $Z_c = \alpha$ rather than being dropped, which would narrow the population the guarantee refers to; separately, the *minimum-cluster gate* requires at least 50 record-carrying calibration hospitals. The expectation defining the estimand is over the *site draw*: $R_M$ is a population aggregate, and what follows from that — the scope of the claim, the dispersion it does not bound, the difference between a parameter and a realized count, and the event every rung shares — is stated once in Section 3.7.

## 3.4 The certification test: betting on the calibration sites

Once each calibration hospital has been reduced to one bounded atom $Z_c$, certification becomes a finite-sample test of whether the mean of those atoms exceeds the budget. We test the null $H_0: \mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ with the Waudby-Smith–Ramdas betting martingale for means of bounded random variables [@waudbysmith2024betting]. The test needs one assumption:

> **Assumption 1 (calibration sampling).** The calibration sites are independent and identically distributed draws from the site population over which the estimand $R_M$ of Section 3.3 is defined. In particular it implies the weaker condition the argument actually uses: given the sites already processed (the history $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$), the next site's atom has the population mean, $\mathbb{E}[Z_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = \mathbb{E}[Z]$.

It does not follow from boundedness; sites violating it are outside the argument (Section 6.1 records the unmodeled temporal dependence). Processing the calibration sites in a fixed order, the wealth process is

$$K_t = \prod_{s \le t}\bigl(1 + \lambda_s(\alpha - Z_s)\bigr), \qquad \lambda_t = \min\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)}{\hat{\sigma}^2_{t-1}\, n}},\; \frac{0.9}{1-\alpha}\right),$$

with $n$ the number of calibration clusters, a variance floor of $10^{-8}$, and running moments initialized at $(0.5, 0.25)$; we certify if and only if $\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta$. In betting terms, the test starts with unit capital, stakes a fraction $\lambda_t$ on each successive hospital's atom landing below budget, and certifies only if the capital $K_t$ ever grows by $1/\delta$. Under $H_0$ the bet is *predictable* — computed only from hospitals already processed — and capped so every wealth factor stays positive, making $K_t$ a nonnegative supermartingale (it cannot grow in expectation), and Ville's inequality bounds the probability of ever crossing the bar by $\delta$ [@ville1939] — finite-sample, with no distributional assumption beyond boundedness and the sampling condition of Assumption 1 (Supplementary Information A.1 gives the four steps). The processing order is a deterministic permutation seeded from the protocol constants, carrying no target identifier — predictable, and unchoosable to flatter the outcome. The *information floor* $\ln(1/\delta)(1-\alpha)/n$ — the smallest margin certifiable at $n$ clusters, a feasibility diagnostic, never a gate — sets the frontier the site-count sweep of Section 4.5 maps.

## 3.5 Threshold selection and the budget ladder

The operating threshold is chosen from a grid of 23 values in $[0.55, 0.99]$, walked in a fixed sequence ordered by estimated certification margin on $S_{\text{aux}}$ — how far each candidate's estimated answered error sits below $\alpha$ — most conservative first; each is tested at the mode's full betting budget, stopping at the first failure, so the multiple looks cost no confidence [@westfall2001fixedsequence; @angelopoulos2021ltt; @bates2021rcps]. Selection happens at three levels: *within a mode*, the maximum-coverage threshold in the certified prefix (the lowest certified threshold maximizes coverage); *across modes*, the most conservative (highest) certified threshold; *across rungs*, the strictest certified $\alpha$. Each candidate budget is a *rung* of the two-rung ladder $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$, certified by separate walks and reported rung by rung (Section 4.5, Supplementary Table S6) — each valid at $1-\delta$, the selected claim jointly at $1-2\delta = 0.90$, as the guarantee states. At 208 sites (about 83 calibration clusters) the expected *operative* rung — the strictest that certifies — is $\alpha = 0.10$.

## 3.6 Assumption modes

**Baseline (exchangeable).** In plain terms: the new hospital is assumed to be the kind of hospital the calibration hospitals were drawn from. The target site is assumed exchangeable with the calibration sites and all record weights are one. Tag: "if exchangeability holds."

**Label shift (black-box shift estimation, BBSE).** In plain terms: the new hospital may see more or fewer deaths than the calibration hospitals, but a patient who dies or survives looks the same there; the gate estimates how much the death rate has moved from the model's own positive-call rate, and treats that estimate as uncertain rather than exact. Only the outcome prevalence may differ between target and calibration; $P(x \mid y)$ is held invariant. We estimate the head's confusion rates on $S_{\text{aux}}$ — its false- and true-positive rates $c_0, c_1$ — observe the target predicted-positive rate $q$, and invert $q = c_0(1-\pi_t) + c_1\pi_t$ for the target prevalence [@lipton2018bbse; @garg2020unified]; certification reweights calibration records by class with the target-to-source odds ratio $\rho$. One approximation is disclosed rather than modeled: the correction estimates the record-pooled source prevalence, while the estimand's effective source mix is the influence-weighted site average — exact where the two coincide, approximate otherwise. Estimation uncertainty rides inside the guarantee for all four parameters [@si2024pac]: one interval each — cluster bootstraps over $S_{\text{aux}}$ sites for $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}})$ [@efron1979; @fieldwelsh2007]; exact Clopper–Pearson [@clopperpearson1934] (single-site pools) or cluster bootstrap (ten-plus declared sites) for $q$ — held as a box at Bonferroni [@dunn1961] $\delta_{\text{conf}}/4$ and propagated to $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ over the sixteen corners. Certification tests both endpoints at $\delta_{\text{bet}}$; this suffices because the sign deciding certification is affine in $\rho$, so the certifiable set is convex (Supplementary Information A.2). The budgets combine as $\delta_{\text{conf}} + \delta_{\text{bet}} = \delta$ with $\delta_{\text{conf}} = \delta_{\text{bet}} = 0.025$. One disclosure travels with every BBSE statement: the three percentile bootstraps are asymptotic, and their finite-cluster error is material at this scale [@cameron2008cluster] — a design-time probe (head held fixed; recorded in the repository's methods document) measured the box's joint miscoverage at $0.038$ against a nominal $\le 0.019$ — so read this mode's printed confidence with that shortfall in mind; the finite-sample claim holds in full only for the exchangeable mode. The mode *declines* whenever its inputs cannot support a correction — an empty target pool, two to nine declared target sites, a degenerate bootstrap, a confusion gap $(c_1 - c_0) < 0.10$, or a $q$ interval implying a prevalence outside $(0,1)$; no shrinkage, no fallback to the uncorrected baseline. Declines are reported as *misspecified* (an input gate fired) or *failsafe* (the mode fit but no threshold certified at both endpoints).

**Combination, and what no mode covers.** The modes run as alternatives, each spending its own betting budget; we deploy the most conservative certified threshold, and a mode is listed only if its certified prefix contains it, so the combined guarantee reads "if *either* tagged assumption holds." Concept shift — a change in $P(y \mid x)$ — is invisible to unlabeled target data, and under it the certificate can be confidently wrong; the control of Section 4.4 injects a posterior shift *verified* capable of pushing answered error above $\alpha$ and shows the certificate fail — the tags mark a real boundary.

## 3.7 The certified guarantee

In plain terms, the certificate promises one thing and refuses four: it promises that, across the kind of hospitals calibration sampled and with each hospital weighted comparably, the share of answered cases the model gets wrong is at most $\alpha$, with probability $1-\delta$ over which hospitals happened to calibrate it; it does not promise this for any single hospital, for any single batch's realized count, for a hospital whose feature–outcome relationship has changed, or at a confidence stronger than the one printed. The clauses below are the deployed guarantee text, frozen as exact strings per mode combination; weakening or strengthening one is a specification change, not an editorial one.
(1) *Estimand.* Under exchangeability the certificate bounds the influence-weighted answered-set risk averaged over the population of sites from which the calibration sites were drawn, with the probability taken over the calibration draw; under label shift it bounds that same site-population risk reweighted to the target class prevalence the BBSE correction identifies, with the probability taken over the joint draw of the calibration sites, the auxiliary split, and the target pool. (2) *Dispersion.* This bounds a site-population average and not any individual site's answered error rate: under between-site heterogeneity individual sites can exceed $\alpha$ while the average stays within budget, at a rate the certificate does not measure or bound. (3) *Parameter, not count.* It does not bound any batch's realized error count, which exceeds $\alpha$ at binomial-dispersion rates even under a valid certificate — at the $\alpha$ boundary approaching one batch in two as batches grow. (4) *Shared event (exchangeable mode).* In the exchangeable mode the certified thresholds are a function of the calibration draw alone, so one $1-\delta$ event is shared by every target pool the certificate is applied to; the clause is omitted from certificates carrying the label-shift mode, because the BBSE fit depends on the target pool through $q$ and no shared event exists there. (5) *Operative rung.* The operative rung is the strictest certified $\alpha$, a data-driven selection over the $\{0.05, 0.10\}$ ladder, so the selected claim holds jointly at $1 - 2\delta = 0.90$. (6) *Concept shift.* Concept shift and combined shift are out of scope and undetectable from unlabeled data; the certificate is void there. (7) *Label-shift additions.* The $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ box covers four estimated parameters $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}}, q_{\text{target}})$ at Bonferroni $\delta_{\text{conf}} = 0.025$, spent over the auxiliary split and the target pool rather than the calibration draw; the $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}})$ intervals are percentile cluster bootstraps — asymptotic, with realized joint coverage measurably below nominal at small cluster counts (Section 3.6) — while the $q_{\text{target}}$ interval is finite-sample Clopper–Pearson for single-site pools and a cluster bootstrap for multi-site pools, with $\delta_{\text{bet}} = 0.025$ spent on the betting test.

## 3.8 Explainability: answers and abstentions

The deployed head is L2-regularized logistic regression ($C = 1.0$), chosen so the answer/abstain decision is intrinsically interpretable; the gate itself is model-agnostic — the score only *ranks* cases, and the certificate's validity never depends on the model's quality — so a different head can be substituted: validity is unaffected and selective quality is priced as coverage, measured rather than asserted in Section 4.13.

Three artifacts make the gate explainable. *Globally*, standardized coefficients give each feature's direction and strength. *Locally*, an answered case carries exact additive attributions $\phi_j(x) = w_j(x_j - \mu_j)$ with $\text{logit}(\hat{p}(x)) = \text{base} + \sum_j \phi_j$, where $w_j$ is the raw-feature-space coefficient; for a linear model these attributions coincide, in closed form and with no sampling, with the Shapley values of the interventional, independent-baseline game [@lundberg2017shap]. *For declined cases*, answering requires $|\text{logit}\,\hat{p}(x)| \ge \text{logit}\,\tau^*$, so the gate reports the margin-to-answer $m(x) = \text{logit}\,\tau^* - |\text{logit}\,\hat{p}(x)| > 0$ together with the signed attributions of the confidence deficit — which features pulled toward each class, leaving confidence below the certified bar. Explaining reject options is itself established [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain]; what the gate adds is that the same exact attributions accompany a *certified* decision. The released code renders these artifacts as one self-contained page in two registers — a plain-language reading for clinicians, ward staff and governance reviewers, and an arithmetic register (logits, margins, attributions, a what-if workbench running the deployed rule) for auditors — with the certificate's scope clauses and the score-space-not-clinical-advice caveat fixed on the page rather than left to the reader; it is a transparency and teaching surface, and its effect on clinical decisions is not evaluated here (Section 6.1).

A fourth artifact guards a subtler failure: the answered-set class composition is reported three ways — predicted-class, BBSE-implied true-class (tagged), and oracle true-class (a retrospective diagnostic) — together revealing whether a certificate was earned by answering only easy negatives.

## 3.9 Validation protocol

Validation scores the certificate against the estimand it actually claims — scoring a population-average guarantee against single-site draws would test a claim it does not make. One conformance metric carries the $\delta$ target: **aggregate conformance**, the fraction of certified draws whose $R_M$ on a fresh 200-site evaluation pool exceeds $\alpha$, required to stay at or below $\delta$ over 200 independent calibration draws. Two diagnostics deliberately carry none. The first is the single-fresh-site **hard-violation rate** (a site counts as violated only when the one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound [@wilson1927] on its answered error exceeds $\alpha$), reported beside the mean fraction of evaluation-pool sites exceeding $\alpha$ — the between-site dispersion the certificate declines to bound. The second is the **realized exceedance rate** against its binomial reference, since small answered sets exceed $\alpha$ by chance at rates approaching 50% at the boundary. The Wilson criterion is deliberately conservative, so meeting the target shows the *absence of gross violations at the power this harness has*; the guarantee itself rests on the finite-sample level of the betting test, not on this harness. Every rate reported over the replication draws — the conformance metric and both diagnostics — carries an exact Clopper–Pearson 95% interval [@clopperpearson1934] on its numerator and denominator, emitted beside the rate by the experiment runner, so a rate near zero is read with the width the replication count actually supports rather than as a point.

## 3.10 Reproducibility

Every result regenerates deterministically from the released code: one protocol seed drives the whole grid through a fixed hashing rule, and a single command reproduces the synthetic grid's figures and tables (Figures 4 and 5 in the main text; Supplementary Figures S3–S9 and Tables S2–S3, S6 and S9), with identical inputs yielding byte-identical certificates. The real-data results of Section 4.10 come from a separate runner over the eICU extract, which we cannot redistribute; the command, protocol, and aggregate artifacts are released, so any credentialed holder of the data can reproduce the analysis. Figure 3 and Supplementary Table S10 are likewise derived: the figure is rendered by a read-only command from the released aggregate diagnostics, and the table by the eICU runner's post-hoc block on a separate output directory. Supplementary Information A.3 details these commands, package versions, the seeding rule, and provenance.

# 4 Results

We evaluate CertGate on a synthetic multi-site cohort whose every generative mechanism is known, then on eICU-CRD v2.0. The synthetic harness exists for *oracle access*: the true answered-set risk is computable, so a certificate can be checked rather than assumed — a real cohort can measure a violation but cannot separate a sound method from a benign cohort. Because record-level selective certificates have been shown to overrun under grouped deployment [@zhou2026falsesense], the design includes a verified-falsifiable negative control and a record-as-unit comparator on the same draws.

## 4.1 Experimental setup

The generator follows a frozen specification (released as `data.py`): 208 sites with clipped-lognormal sizes spanning roughly 20 to 5,000 records; $d = 8$ features with the class signal on one normalized direction supported by features 0–3; base prevalence 0.095 with a site random effect $u_c \sim \mathcal{N}(0, s_u^2)$, $s_u = 0.5$, on the outcome log-odds, so per-site prevalence varies and no two sites share a case mix — the distributional profile reported for large multi-site clinical cohorts [@tripodcluster2023; @internalexternal2021]. Labels are fully ascertained and carry no time axis; the clinical-target specification of Section 3.1 binds real deployments, not these experiments. Splits are site-disjoint 40/20/40; the head is L2 logistic regression fit on $S_{\text{train}}$. Everything runs under protocol seed 20260721 over the $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$ ladder at $\delta = 0.05$; statistical experiments replicate over $R = 200$ calibration draws (the draw is the unit of replication), scored per Section 3.9 with exact Clopper–Pearson 95% intervals.

## 4.2 Validity in distribution

With target sites exchangeable with calibration, the $\alpha = 0.10$ rung certifies all 200 draws at mean coverage 0.9828 (minimum 0.871) and conforms on every one: 0 of 200 exceed $\alpha$ (exact 95% CI $[0, 0.018]$) at mean fresh-pool $R_M$ 0.0567, half the budget (Supplementary Figure S3). The per-site dispersion diagnostic records what the certificate declines to bound: the single-fresh-site hard-violation rate is 0.02, and raising the site random effect $s_u$ from 0.5 to 1.0 and 2.0 leaves aggregate conformance untouched (0.0 exceedances in all three arms) while the diagnostic detaches — the hard rate moves to 0.10 and 0.095 and the mean per-site exceedance fraction climbs 0.055, 0.1285, 0.1634 — the aggregate-versus-individual signature named by the guarantee's dispersion clause (Section 3.7), and why the $\delta$ target attaches to the aggregate, not the diagnostic. The realized exceedance rate, 0.08 overall, sits far below its binomial reference in every answered-set size bin (Supplementary Table S2) — the parameter-versus-realized-count distinction in action. The stricter $\alpha = 0.05$ rung certifies nothing at 208 sites; Section 4.5 maps what that budget would require.

**Operating characteristics.** A budget on an error rate can be met by a degenerate rule, so Supplementary Table S5 reports what the answered and declined sets contain: sensitivity 0.550 with $76.9\%$ of answered errors false negatives — the asymmetry the symmetric loss does not price — while the declined 1.8% of records carries positive fraction 0.492, roughly $4.7\times$ the pools' prevalence, routing $8.6\%$ of all positives to a clinician. The gate is not meeting its budget by answering only easy negatives (answered positive fraction 0.0968 against the pools' 0.1039); a constant always-negative rule errs at 0.1039 here, so the error budget alone is a weak test — the sensitivity and composition columns carry the evidence.

## 4.3 Label-shift stress test

We shift site-level prevalence from 0.095 to 0.22 — the kind of increase a changed referral pattern produces — and compare the exchangeable baseline against the BBSE mode [@lipton2018bbse]; the target pool is declared as a single site, as a deployment batch would be. The uncorrected baseline is the motivating failure: at $\alpha = 0.10$ it certifies all 200 draws and hard-violates 39.5% of them (exact 95% CI $[0.327, 0.466]$, far above $\delta = 0.05$) — and against the certified estimand itself, the influence-weighted answered risk on a fresh label-shifted pool exceeds $\alpha$ for 97.5% of its certificates (Figure S9, left). BBSE issues no certificate on this configuration — it declines all 200 draws (194 failsafe, 6 misspecified), the designed behavior: once the target predicted-positive rate carries its own confidence share, a single-site target pool of this size cannot support a certified rung under this shift. A magnitude sweep through target base rates $\{0.095, 0.13, 0.16, 0.19, 0.22\}$ separates a working correction from a reflexive decliner (Figure S9, right): the baseline's failure is dose-responsive while BBSE's certify-and-violate rate is 0.0 at *every* magnitude — it certifies 9% of draws at the null point, never above 5% under genuine shift at this scale. The mode certifies when the pool supports it and refuses in proportion to what the shift destroys; at this scale its demonstrated value under a genuine shift is the refusal itself — Section 4.14 measures the source capacity at which that changes.

## 4.4 Concept-shift negative control

The harness first *verifies the poison*: the injected posterior shift (concept intercept 2.0) is confirmed to raise mean realized answered error to 0.161, above budget; a shift failing the check aborts the run. Given a verified poison the certificate certifies all 200 draws and hard-violates 70.0% (exact 95% CI $[0.631, 0.763]$); against the certified estimand the failure is total — 100% exceed $\alpha$ on a fresh tilted pool (Supplementary Figure S4). This is the intended result: concept shift is the failure mode the guarantee explicitly excludes, and the exclusion marks a real boundary rather than a rhetorical one [@zhou2026falsesense]. The control runs at one verified magnitude — it demonstrates that the harness can register a violation, not how the certificate degrades across concept-shift severities.

## 4.5 Site-count frontier

This is the capacity-planning study: it sweeps the site count over $\{60, 100, 150, 208, 300, 400\}$ at both rungs (Supplementary Table S6, Figure S7). At $\alpha = 0.10$ certification switches on at 150 sites and holds (coverage 0.9818 at the 208-site operating point); the 60- and 100-site declines come from the minimum-cluster gate of Section 3.3, not the information floor — different failure modes with different remedies. At $\alpha = 0.05$ the budget first appears at 300 (certify rate 0.285) and is reliable only at 400. The frontier is set by the information floor scaling as $1/n_{\text{cal}}$: a stricter answered-set budget is a claim about how many hospitals one has, not about the method.

## 4.6 Attribution study

Because the head is linear, the answer/abstain decision carries the exact attributions of Section 3.8. In a single-deployment case study ($\tau^* = 0.55$; 200 answered, 2 declined) the global importances recover the generator — features 0–3 dominate, features 4–7 are negligible — and each declined case's margin-to-answer localizes its deficit to named features (Supplementary Figure S5). Replicated over $R = 200$ draws (186 with declines; 2,644 declined cases pooled; pooled decline rate 2.15%), the single-draw reading does not survive: no stable single abstention driver exists (modal top-feature share 0.274 against a 0.25 chance level) — exactly what the generator's symmetric construction predicts, and we report the null as a null. What does replicate is the *shape*: declined cases carry systematically larger attribution magnitudes on every informative feature (mean gaps $-0.21$ to $-0.26$) — contested cases whose large pulls cancel. The instrument correctly finds no privileged feature here; the real cohort (Section 4.10) yields a stable one.

## 4.7 Per-site coverage and composition

The coverage-and-composition study deploys a single draw ($\tau^* = 0.55$; 40 target sites) and asks whether coverage is uniform across site sizes — do small hospitals receive worse selective service? — and how the gate earns its low answered error. Coverage is essentially flat across size bins (0.9896, 0.9800, 0.9856; Supplementary Table S3) with mean answered error below $\alpha$ in every bin (0.0523, 0.0670, 0.0595; Supplementary Figure S8), and no site answers nothing. Here, unlike on the validity grid, the certified quantity and the record-level average separate: $R_M$ is 0.0619 against an unweighted answered error of 0.0555 — the influence weighting counting the 15 mid-sized sites (answered error 0.0670) as heavily as the 21 largest (0.0595) — which is what it exists to do.

The composition analysis (Supplementary Table S9) is the instrument that tells us *how* a 9.5%-prevalence task yields a sub-$\alpha$ answered error. Over 25,314 answered records the predicted-class positive fraction is 0.0630, the BBSE-implied true-class fraction 0.0858, and the oracle fraction 0.0915 — just under the cohort prevalence, so the sub-$\alpha$ error is not bought by answering only easy negatives; the predicted-class column alone would have understated the true positive load by about a third. A post-hoc reliability panel over the same partition — a descriptive diagnostic added after the synthetic grid was published; it moves no certified quantity — adds that the answered set is close to calibrated at the deployed threshold (expected calibration error 0.002914), that its skill margin over the constant rule is 0.035948, and that the answered-minus-all contrast straddles zero — the composition reading by a second instrument.

## 4.8 Record-as-unit comparator

The design's premise — record-as-unit certificates are overconfident on clustered data — entered as a citation [@zhou2026falsesense]; the comparator tests it on the same draws: identical betting machinery with each *record* as one atom, on a 2,000-record subsample of $S_{\text{cal}}$ — still roughly 24 times the site count. At $\alpha = 0.05$ — the rung the site-unit walk refuses on every one of 400 draws across both heterogeneity arms — the record-unit certifier *grants* the budget almost always (certify rate 1.0 at $s_u = 0.5$, 0.99 at $s_u = 2.0$) and then violates the aggregate budget in 3.5% and 9.6% of those draws respectively — the latter nearly twice $\delta$ (Supplementary Figure S6).

Two readings temper the demonstration. At the default heterogeneity the 3.5% violation rate sits within $\delta$ — the outright failure belongs to the high-heterogeneity arm — and at $\alpha = 0.10$ both units certify on all but one of 400 draws, the difference showing in conduct rather than validity: the record unit deploys laxer thresholds under heterogeneity (mean $\tau$ 0.587 against 0.747) and runs at fresh-pool risk 0.081 against the site unit's 0.052, most of the way to the boundary. The record-as-unit certifier buys its extra rungs and coverage by spending confidence clustered data may not have; the price surfaces as heterogeneity grows, and the real cohort's dispersion (Section 4.10) sits below even the default arm.

## 4.9 Sensitivity to the frozen influence cap

Of the frozen constants, $M$ is the one that visibly moves the certificate, so the validity grid's exchangeable arm is re-run at $M \in \{25, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 5000\}$, re-deriving the walk and rescoring against $R_M$ at each value (Supplementary Table S4): a plateau across $M \le 100$, then certification erodes toward the record-proportional end, where nothing certifies at 208 sites. No $M$ certifies *more* than the frozen value and the stricter rung is unreachable at every $M$, so the frontier of Section 4.5 is not an artifact of this constant: at this cluster count only the site-equal end of the site/record continuum is certifiable at all.

## 4.10 eICU-CRD v2.0 — the certificate on real multi-site data

This section reports what the same pipeline did on a cohort nobody constructed. The eICU Collaborative Research Database v2.0 [@pollard2019eicudb; @pollard2018eicu; @pollard2026physionet; @goldberger2000physionet] contains 200,859 ICU stays from 139,367 patients at 208 US hospitals in 2014–2015, under credentialed access and a data use agreement forbidding sharing access. We extend that obligation, as our own release policy, to any record-level derived artifact: every artifact behind this section is aggregate, and the release code refuses to write anything record-level.

The cohort predicates, a 161-column feature allowlist (everything not explicitly allowed is excluded), a 36-entry leak denylist, the split arithmetic, seven numbered predictions and five failure criteria were written from the database's published schema and column documentation and committed before the extract was downloaded (commit `9f25b491b2554d0a4bd7aaaf44081c185d01715f`, 2026-07-30); their outcomes are reported where they arise, and no failure criterion fired. A dated commit in a repository the author controls is weaker evidence than a third-party registry timestamp. The protocol constants are pinned in advance in the manner of Section 3.2, so a post-hoc edit would be visible.

**Cohort.** The unit is the first ICU stay of each hospital admission, the outcome in-hospital mortality from a single documented column, the site the hospital. Table 1 runs 200,859 raw stays down to 164,322 over 207 hospitals at 8.89% prevalence, recording deaths at every step so an outcome-correlated exclusion surfaces as a prevalence movement. Three standard eICU cohort filters — a length-of-stay floor, a minimum-stays cut, an APACHE-completeness requirement — are deliberately absent: each would move the site population the estimand averages over, and all three make certification harder (arguments in Supplementary Information A.3). One hospital contributes no stay with a usable outcome, which is why the primary cohort spans 207 sites rather than 208. Twenty-four hospitals are held out as the target population and the remaining 183 split 73 / 36 / 74 into train, auxiliary and calibration; the procedure repeats over 20 by-site re-splits.

**The certificate.** At $\alpha = 0.10$ the gate certifies on all 20 re-splits (Table 2, Figure 2), at mean deployed threshold $\tau^* = 0.793$ and mean coverage 0.890 over held-out target pools ranging from 13,667 to 27,021 records. At $\alpha = 0.05$ it certifies on none: both assumption modes return failsafe on every re-split. That pattern is what Section 4.5's frontier anticipates at 74 calibration clusters — a predicted refusal, not a malfunction. The certified quantity is the one Section 3.3 defines and no other — a site-population average; it is not a guarantee about any one of the 24 target hospitals. On the *published split* — the seeded replicate whose artifacts are released — the deployed threshold is $\tau^* = 0.850$, answering 12,964 of 15,169 target records for coverage 0.855 and answered error 0.0419; the estimated answered-set risk on the 74 calibration hospitals is 0.0387 (95% site bootstrap 0.0356–0.0422). The label-shift mode fitted without declining — 24 target sites, $\hat{\rho} = 0.668$ inside a box $[0.068, 1.986]$ — but no threshold certified at both $\rho$ endpoints, so it returned failsafe, and the exchangeable mode carried every deployed certificate in the study.

**What twenty re-splits can and cannot establish.** Scored against its own held-out 24-hospital pool, each certified re-split's $R_M$ averages 0.0478 (range 0.0348–0.0593) and exceeds $\alpha$ on none of the 20 — the pre-declared validity criterion, met subject to two limits that compound: 20 re-splits cannot resolve a 0.05 rate, and they partition one hospital population rather than draw fresh ones. The run bounds the absence of gross violation at the available power; it does not estimate the rate, and no amount of re-splitting one extract would.

**Per-hospital dispersion.** The per-hospital arm deploys the same calibration-side certificate to each hospital's pool separately — creating no per-hospital guarantee — reaching 477 of 480 pools (the three refusals sit under the 10-record floor). Across the 477, answered error has median 0.0448, interdecile range 0.0188–0.0776, maximum 0.2857; 7 of 477 are hard-violated on the Wilson criterion, a 0.0147 rate with no $\delta$ target. This arm measures the dispersion the certificate declines to bound: a hospital at 0.29 answered error under a certificate whose pools average 0.048 is what the dispersion clause says can happen (Supplementary Figure S2).

**Comparator.** APACHE-IVa [@zimmerman2006apache] is a purpose-built day-1 severity score and eICU ships its predictions, so the answered set can be scored against an external clinical reference. On the answered records carrying a comparator value (share 0.822, itself site-correlated), APACHE-IVa attains AUC [@hanley1982roc] 0.820 and Brier [@brier1950] 0.0437 over the 20 re-splits; CertGate's answered 0–1 error on that subset is 0.0471 — different currencies, side by side for orientation, not contest. The like-for-like reading, both models scored by Brier on the identical denominator inside one shared resample, is the post-hoc panel's on the published split: 0.038079 for the reference against 0.037104 for the head, a paired difference of 0.000975 (0.000136–0.001684) in the head's favour (Supplementary Information A.4, Figure S1). Both readings are on the subset the gate itself chose to answer — the head's preferred ground — and APACHE-IVa's discrimination there sits below its published full-cohort figures: the composition effect in a third form.

**Leak screens, and a pre-registered alarm that fired backwards.** The protocol pre-declared three leak screens, two independent of both $\alpha$ and coverage. The head's out-of-sample AUC peaks at 0.867 against a pre-registered ceiling of 0.90; ablating the 49 missingness and presence columns costs at most 0.0063 of AUC against a cap of 0.05, so that block is not carrying the model; and a univariate screen of all 161 allowlisted features flags none past the 0.75 review band, settling from data the nine treatment-related columns whose measurement timing could not be established in advance.

The most informative screen fired in the wrong direction. The registered fear: because the day-1 APACHE window does not close for a stay that ends in death, the presence of an APACHE row partly proxies surviving the first day — a leak with no column name, invisible to a denylist that matches names. The measured absent-to-present outcome prevalence ratio is 0.506 against a registered one-sided cap of 2.0 — an association at the cap's own magnitude ($0.506 \approx 1/2.0$), in the opposite direction. APACHE-absent stays here have *lower* mortality — shorter (median 27.8 hours against 40.7) but less deadly, so early discharge rather than early death dominates the absence. A caution, not a clearance: whole-row absence still encodes stay length, a prediction-time exposure here pointing toward survival; the registered mechanism remains sound in principle, just not what governs this extract. Two column-level missingness indicators did exceed the cap; the pre-registered abort covered only the two whole-row presence flags (which passed), so the indicators remain in the model, bounded by the ablation screen above.

**The seven predictions.** Four were confirmed, three were not. Confirmed: $\alpha = 0.10$ certifies while $\alpha = 0.05$ does not, 20 of 20 against a registered 15-of-20 threshold (P1); the label-shift mode contributes no certificate (P3); coverage lands inside the registered $[0.60, 0.95]$ at 0.890 (P6); cohort size and APACHE-result site count land inside their intervals (P7). Falsified: the per-site dispersion diagnostic came in at 0.027 against a registered $[0.05, 0.30]$ — real hospitals were *less* heterogeneous on it than the synthetic generator's 0.055, not more (P2); the per-hospital arm refused three of 480 pool-instances against a registered one to six of the 24 hospitals (P5); and P4 — the APACHE-absence features among the top three abstention drivers — fails on 18 of the 20 re-splits. For P4, failure is the desired outcome: an amendment logged *before* the extract was read (distinct from the post-hoc amendment under Scope below) re-read P4 as a leak alarm, so abstention drivers being physiology rather than APACHE-absence is the healthy reading. On the two exceptions `aps_heartrate__missing` ranks third, and the ablation screen's 0.0063 ceiling bounds that channel. The predictions with teeth were the certification and validity ones (P1, P6, and the exceedance criterion above); all three falsifications landed benign, and the count should be weighed accordingly.

**What the gate is trading away.** The answered set's observed mortality is 0.0458 against 0.0898 in its pool: the gate reaches its budget partly by abstaining where deaths concentrate — read the two numbers together. Stated baldly, a rule predicting survival for everyone already meets the $\alpha = 0.10$ budget here (erring at 0.0898); the certified rung's value is the guarantee and its disclosures, not headroom over the base rate. A post-hoc reliability panel (Supplementary Information A.4; descriptive, settling no registered prediction) makes the point quantitatively: the answered-set skill margin over the constant rule is 0.003934 and the answered-minus-all contrast $-0.006021$ — the answered set is *easier* for the trivial rule than its pool.

**Operating characteristics and subgroups (post-hoc, derived).** Table S7 reports the answered- and declined-set confusion quantities for all 20 re-splits, derived from the released panel artifact: answered-set sensitivity at the head's inherited 0.5 threshold has median 0.126 — the certified budget coexists with calling few answered deaths. Table S8 is the fairness reading of the same gate: subgroup coverage and answered error, one marginal dimension at a time. Coverage falls 0.96 to 0.82 and answered error rises 0.019 to 0.075 across age bands into 75+, and the same gradient runs from operating-theatre to floor and step-down admissions; every unsuppressed cell stays under $\alpha$, so the certificate's budget is met in every subgroup it can resolve, but it is met by abstaining more on the oldest and sickest. An abstaining classifier can be *fair in its errors and unequal in its service* — the concern the interpretable-and-fair abstention literature raises [@ifac2025abstainexplain] — and this is that pattern measured: the cost of the certificate is paid disproportionately in deferrals by the patients most at risk. The deployment consequence is plain and human-centred rather than statistical: the deferred workload concentrates on exactly these patients, and an adopting site should staff the deferral pathway accordingly and monitor subgroup coverage as a service-equity metric, not only answered error as a safety one.

**Scope.** One amendment followed the extract read, the only post-hoc change: one malformed negative value among roughly 4.1 million cells would, read literally, have aborted the run, so an already-frozen unparseable-data tolerance governs it instead. No new constant, no computed number moved; every figure from this extract carries the post-hoc label. Two exposures this cohort carries — unfiltered cross-hospital patients, and the population the estimand actually averages over — are stated in Section 6.1.

## 4.11 Explainable abstention on real clinical data

The margin-to-answer decomposition of Section 3.8 was run on every re-split's pooled target arm, and the answered-to-declined attribution gap — how much harder each feature pulls on the stays the gate withheld than on the stays it answered — was ranked at the operative rung (Figure 3). The result replicates where the synthetic attribution study could not. The largest gap falls on the **Glasgow Coma Scale motor score on all 20 re-splits** (mean gap $-0.31$ logit units), with the day-1 inspired oxygen fraction in the top three on 13 re-splits and the day-1 intubation flag on 10; arterial pH, temperature, the eye-opening score and the APS intubation indicator sit in the top ten on at least 19 of 20. Where the symmetric synthetic generator admits no privileged feature by construction — which is why Section 4.6 reports a null — the real cohort yields a stable, clinically legible driver: the gate abstains most where consciousness, oxygenation and airway status leave the answer contested. That is the same severity axis along which the composition disclosure of Section 4.10 shows deaths concentrating in the declined set; the legible driver and the composition cost are two views of one behavior, and a reader should hold them together.

The contrast with Section 4.6 is the point, not an embarrassment: an instrument that found a driver on every cohort would be suspect. The same code returns a null on a generator built to have none and a replicated driver on a cohort that has one, which is what licenses reading the real-data result as a property of the cohort rather than of the instrument.

One choice underneath every attribution a reader sees is named here because clinical explainability reports rarely name it. The reported values are interventional Shapley values with the training-mean baseline (Section 3.8); under correlated features the *conditional* Shapley values differ [@aas2021conditional; @chen2020truetomodel], and ICU variables are correlated — among the ten drivers the largest absolute pairwise correlation on the training split is 0.85. Recomputing the ten drivers' attributions under the Gaussian conditional value function on the published split, exactly over all $2^{10}$ coalitions, separates what survives the choice from what does not (Supplementary Table S10). The headline survives: the Glasgow motor score is the top driver under both value functions, with its gap unchanged to three decimals ($-0.246$ against $-0.246$). The ordering beneath it does not: the Spearman correlation between the two driver orderings is 0.58, the APS intubation indicator falls from fourth to tenth (its gap moves from $-0.109$ to $-0.022$, the largest change of the ten) and the eye-opening score rises from sixth to second. Both are members of correlated groups on the training split — the APS indicator with the out-of-theatre intubation and ventilation flags ($r = 0.72$ and $0.61$), the eye-opening score with the motor score ($r = 0.80$) — and redistributing credit within such groups is what the conditional value function is defined to do [@aas2021conditional]. The lesson is specific: the *leading* driver may be read as a property of the cohort, the *ranking* below it may not, because it depends on a value-function choice that a report which does not name it has left unfixed. This check is post-hoc and descriptive (Supplementary Information A.3), settles no registered prediction, and the Gaussian conditional is an approximation for the binary indicators among the drivers.

What a clinician reviewing a deferred stay receives is the case's margin-to-answer, the signed attributions of its confidence deficit, and the smallest change in score space that would have made it answerable — a statement about the gate, never a recommendation about the patient — beside the certificate's scope clauses (Section 3.7). The released code renders this as a two-register page, a plain-language reading for ward staff and governance reviewers and an arithmetic register for auditors, over the committed synthetic demonstration; the same page over the real extract is local-only because it embeds record-level data. Whether these explanations help a clinician decide is not tested here (Section 6.1).

## 4.12 Alternative certifiers on the same atoms

Four alternative one-sided bounds walk the identical per-site atoms, walk order, and stop-at-first-failure rule: finite-sample Hoeffding [@hoeffding1963] and Maurer–Pontil empirical-Bernstein [@maurerpontil2009] bounds, and the two asymptotic defaults a practitioner would reach for, a Student-$t$ upper bound [@student1908] and a percentile bootstrap over sites [@efron1979; @fieldwelsh2007] (Figure 4, left). Among the valid certifiers the betting test dominates everywhere — empirical-Bernstein reaches $\alpha = 0.10$ only at 400 sites, against the betting walk's 150. The asymptotic defaults grant every rung at every site count, at a measured fresh-pool exceedance equal to their nominal level with no margin, against 0.0 for the betting test. A stricter budget is bought with hospitals or with validity, never with a different bound.

## 4.13 Stressing the certificate: aleatoric risk and alternative heads

**Aleatoric stress.** The validity grid leaves the certifier untested near its boundary, and tilting the generator cannot reach it — the walk simply deploys a stricter threshold. The stress arm instead raises an aleatoric floor no threshold can screen: symmetric label flips at rate $\eta$ in every cohort, preserving exchangeability. As $\eta$ rises from 0.01 to 0.04 the walk responds in order (Figure 4, centre): coverage is spent first, then certification is withheld — certify rate 1.0, then 0.63, then 0.02 — while the exceedance rate stays at zero throughout. Near its boundary, the certifier declines before it violates.

**Alternative heads.** A gradient-boosted head and a feature-denied linear head run through the identical atoms and walk (Figure 4, right): every head certifies every draw with zero exceedances — validity belongs to the certificate, not the model — and the denied head pays for its lost signal as coverage, 0.88 against 0.98. The exact attributions of Section 3.8 do not survive the boosted head; the explainability contribution is scoped to transparent heads.

## 4.14 Power frontiers: label shift and the false-negative budget

**Label shift.** Section 4.3 showed the corrected mode refusing at 208 sites; the power frontier prices that refusal in capacity (Figure 5, left). With a 40-site declared target the mode certifies 0.38 of draws at 900 sites and 0.94 at 1,200, with zero exceedances: under genuine shift it certifies once source capacity suffices and declines below it. The single-declared-site path plateaus and is the one configuration in the whole study that exceeded its budget on fresh pools — under shift, certify against a multi-site declared target or not at all.

**The false-negative budget.** The symmetric loss prices most answered errors as false negatives, so whether the FN rate among answered positives can itself be certified is the clinical question. It can, on the same atoms (Section 3.3), and the frontier is the honest news (Figure 5, right): against a measured truth near 0.45, no budget below 0.55 issues at realistic site counts, and every issued certificate held on fresh pools. On the real cohort the derived answered-set FNR is 0.80–0.90 (Table S7).

# 5 Discussion

CertGate issues a specific promise: a bound on the influence-weighted answered-set error *parameter*, averaged over the site population the calibration hospitals were drawn from, at confidence $1-\delta$, under one tagged assumption. It is not a per-site bound — the heterogeneity arm shows aggregate conformance holding while the per-site diagnostic rises five-fold — nor a bound on a batch's realized count, nor protection against concept shift. Capacity is counted in sites, not records — 74 calibration *hospitals* on the real cohort, not their $\sim\!10^5$ records; Sections 4.5 and 4.12 price the stricter rung in hospitals, under every valid bound. A black-box head can replace the linear one, its selective quality priced visibly as certified coverage rather than smuggled in as an unearned guarantee.

For cross-institutional deployment the mapping is direct: a new hospital that differs only in ways the between-site variation already samples takes the exchangeable tag — subject to the dispersion clause, since it may sit in the tail the average does not bound. One that differs mainly in outcome prevalence takes the label-shift tag, with Sections 4.3 and 4.14 the relevant evidence. One that differs in how features relate to outcome — documentation practice, treatment thresholds — takes neither, and the certificate is void. The machinery is relevant wherever institutions are natural clusters, though CertGate assumes centrally available site-disjoint calibration data; a certified gate under genuine federation [@fedcrc2026] is future work.

The two evidence bases answer different questions: the synthetic grid supplies oracle access — certificates checked against a known truth, negative controls verified falsifiable, comparator bounds and stress arms on identical draws — while the eICU study shows the pipeline surviving contact with real multi-site data and disclosing what it trades away (Section 4.10). The synthetic arm tests the method; the real arm tests the implementation; the claim needs both.

# 6 Conclusion

CertGate sits at the intersection of certified selective risk [@chow1970reject; @geifman2017selective], cluster-as-unit distribution-free inference [@dunn2023hierarchical; @lee2025hierarchical], label-shift guarantees propagating weight uncertainty [@si2024pac], and explained rejection [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain]. The result is a certificate whose disclosures travel with it: the tagged assumption, the named scope, the unbounded dispersion beside it, a demonstrated concept-shift boundary. On eICU-CRD v2.0, under a protocol fixed before the extract was read, the $\alpha = 0.10$ rung certifies on all 20 re-splits at 0.890 coverage, the stricter rung refused, three pre-declared leak screens clear; the abstentions point at a stable, clinically legible driver — consciousness, oxygenation, airway — and the gate's cost is disclosed: part of the budget is met by abstaining where deaths concentrate, and disproportionately among the oldest and sickest.

## 6.1 Limitations and future directions

*The guarantee is a site-population average* — individual sites are not bounded; a per-site guarantee would need a tolerance bound on an upper risk quantile, costing substantially more clusters. *Concept shift is undetectable from unlabeled data* — the negative control demonstrates the boundary, and the caveat lives in the guarantee text. *Covariate-shift importance weighting is deliberately excluded*: its effective-sample-size loss at practical clip caps — a design-time estimate — puts certification out of reach below roughly 400 clusters, so we ship the two modes the data can certify. *There is no out-of-support screen or positivity diagnostic*; missingness is the caller's responsibility, validated loudly at entry, never imputed.

*Temporal common-shock correlation is unmodeled and is the assumption most at risk*: sites sharing a collection window may correlate through practice changes or seasonal case mix — a direct threat to Assumption 1, unquantified here and compounded on the real cohort, whose re-splits partition one fixed hospital list within a single 2014–2015 window. *The loss is symmetric and the operating point inherited*: $\alpha$ budgets the sum of false negatives and false positives — on the synthetic validity grid roughly three quarters of answered errors are false negatives — and the certificate says nothing about whether the head's $\hat{p} = 1/2$ boundary suits a given clinical decision; the class-conditional budget of Section 4.14 prices that choice — no FNR budget below roughly 0.55 is issuable at realistic site counts — and a full cost-weighted or net-benefit atom remains future work. *Beyond the influence cap and the stress and comparator arms of Sections 4.12–4.13, the frozen constants are not swept*; pinning attests they were fixed in advance, not that they are optimal.

*The eICU cohort carries disclosed exposures*: 3,996 patient identifiers appear at more than one hospital (share 0.029, a lower bound), deliberately unfiltered because the filter would itself be site-correlated; the certified population is, per Assumption 1, the hospital population the cohort samples — not the finite eICU list, not US hospitals generally; a without-replacement betting variant is future work. *The explanations are a read-out whose clinical utility is untested*: the attributions are exact for the deployed linear head and their leading driver is stable on eICU, but no clinician has evaluated whether the abstention report changes a decision, the ranking beneath the leading driver depends on the value-function choice (Section 4.11), and none of it survives a non-transparent head (Section 4.13); a human evaluation of the deferral report is the explanation-side increment this work does not supply. *The real-data evidence is one cohort*: a second independent multi-site cohort, not more re-splits, is the next increment of evidence. *The label-shift mode certifies only at source capacities beyond this cohort*: on eICU it fitted but certified nothing, and the measured frontier of Section 4.14 puts positive certification near 900–1,200 source sites under a multi-site declared target — the refusal is demonstrated on real data, and the price of more is now stated in sites. *The panel's calibration error is a biased plug-in estimate* whose interval covers the biased estimand, and *three of the four BBSE intervals are asymptotic*, with the Section 3.6 design-time probe measuring joint miscoverage 0.038 against a nominal $\le 0.019$; a finite-sample cluster bound is future work.

# Supplementary Information A: Deferred proofs and additional material

## A.1 Validity of the betting test

We show the crossing rule $\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta$ (Section 3.4) is a finite-sample level-$\delta$ test of $H_0: \mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$.

*(i) Boundedness.* Each term of the inner sum in $Z_c$ lies in $[-\alpha, 1-\alpha]$, so the prefactor $g_c/(M n_c)$ scales the sum into $[-\alpha\, g_c/M,\ (1-\alpha)\, g_c/M] \subseteq [-\alpha, 1-\alpha]$ because $g_c \le M$; adding $\alpha$ places $Z_c \in [0,1]$.

*(ii) Null equivalence.* Both the atom mean and the estimand are taken over the same index set: $c$ ranges over the population of sites from which the calibration sites are drawn, so $R_M = \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c e_c] / \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c]$ is a site-population parameter and $\mathbb{E}[Z]$ is an expectation over that same site draw. By the identity $Z_c - \alpha = g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)/M$ of Section 3.3, $\mathbb{E}[Z] - \alpha = \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)]/M$, whose sign is the sign of $\mathbb{E}[g_c a_c e_c] - \alpha\,\mathbb{E}[g_c a_c]$ and hence of $R_M - \alpha$. Testing $\mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ is therefore exactly testing $R_M \ge \alpha$ — for the population average, and for no individual site: for a single site $g_c$ and $a_c$ cancel and the quantity collapses to that site's own error rate, which a population mean does not bound (Section 3.7, clause 2).

*(iii) Supermartingale.* This is the step that consumes Assumption 1, and the assumption is not optional. Without it the atoms may carry site-specific means $\mu_t$, the per-step conditional expectation evaluates to $1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - \mu_t)$, and that exceeds 1 at any site with $\mu_t < \alpha$ even when the population average satisfies $H_0$ — the supermartingale property would fail atom-wise, before any product is formed. Under Assumption 1 it holds. The bet $\lambda_t$ is predictable, a function only of atoms already processed, so it is $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable and factors out:

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - Z_t) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr] = 1 + \lambda_t\bigl(\alpha - \mathbb{E}[Z_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\bigr) = 1 + \lambda_t\bigl(\alpha - \mathbb{E}[Z]\bigr) \le 1,$$

the last inequality because $\lambda_t > 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ under $H_0$. The cap at $0.9/(1-\alpha)$ additionally keeps every realized factor $1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - Z_t) \ge 0.1 > 0$, so $K_t$ is a *nonnegative* supermartingale with $K_0 = 1$. The processing order enters only through predictability: any order fixed without reference to the calibration atoms preserves it, which is why the permutation of Section 3.4 is seeded from the protocol constants and carries no target identifier.

*(iv) Ville.* For such a process, $P(\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta) \le \delta$ [@ville1939], so the crossing rule has finite-sample level $\delta$ — no asymptotics, and no distributional assumption beyond the boundedness in (i) together with the sampling condition of Assumption 1 consumed in (iii). The test's boundary behavior (type-I error at $\mathbb{E}[Z] = \alpha$) is additionally pinned by the unit test suite.

## A.2 Soundness of the dual-endpoint BBSE certification

The uncertainty box (Section 3.6) is a Bonferroni box over four estimated parameters $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}}, q_{\text{target}})$, each interval taken at level $\delta_{\text{conf}}/4$. The first three come from a cluster bootstrap of the $S_{\text{aux}}$ sites that requires 2,000 valid resamples within at most 4,000 attempts (a degenerate pool triggers a decline); the fourth is an exact two-sided Clopper–Pearson interval on the target pool's predicted-positive count for a single-site pool, and a cluster bootstrap over target sites for a pool declaring ten or more (a pool declaring two to nine declines). The box is propagated to $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ by *corner-interval coverage*: the clip on $\pi_t$ is monotone non-decreasing, so the clipped $\rho(c_0, c_1, \pi_s; q)$ remains coordinate-wise monotone in each of the four box parameters on the gated region, and the minimum and maximum of the clipped $\rho$ over the sixteen box corners therefore bound $\rho$ at every interior parameter combination — the corners bound every $\rho$ the box implies. Certification therefore reduces to the two endpoints, which is sound for the following reason.

The certified statistic is scale-invariant in the record weights, so the sign that decides certification factors as $\operatorname{sign}(\mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] - \alpha) = \operatorname{sign}(A + \rho B)$ with $(A, B)$ free of $\rho$ — affine in $\rho$. The certifiable set $\{\rho : \mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] \le \alpha\}$ is therefore convex, so certifying at both endpoints of $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ covers every interior $\rho$, and any $\rho$ inside the box with $\mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] > \alpha$ forces one endpoint whose level-$\delta_{\text{bet}}$ test bounds the false-certification probability. This holds even though the two endpoint atom sets use different per-endpoint normalizations $w_{\max} = \max(1, \rho)$, under which the *raw* atom mean is piecewise with a kink at $\rho = 1$: the affine object is the sign-determining product $(\mathbb{E}[Z]-\alpha)\cdot\max(1,\rho)$, not the kinked atom mean itself. The argument is pinned numerically in the test suite on the deployed normalization, including an interval that straddles $\rho = 1$.

Three full-scale arms are skipped unless explicitly enabled. The anti-conservativity of naive realized-contribution truncation (Section 3.3) is pinned by a dedicated regression test that exhibits the construction: 140 clean sites of 20 records with zero errors beside 10 heavy sites of 2,000 records at 20% error, everything answered, for a record-weighted selective risk of 17.5%. Truncating each site's *realized* signed contribution $T_c = e^{\text{cnt}}_c(1-\alpha) - (n_c - e^{\text{cnt}}_c)\,\alpha$ at 1 caps the ten heavy sites' adverse evidence at $+1$ apiece while every clean site keeps its full $-1$, and a Maurer–Pontil empirical-Bernstein upper bound [@maurerpontil2009] on the truncated contributions certifies $\alpha = 5\%$ at $\delta = 0.05$ — a false certificate at 3.5 times the true risk. The influence-weighting scheme refuses the same configuration: the influence-weighted atoms have mean above $\alpha$ and the betting test does not reject.

*Cohort filters deliberately omitted (Section 4.10).* There is no length-of-stay floor, because conditioning on survival to a horizon removes the patients who die fastest, does so differentially by site, and is computed from discharge offsets that are themselves on the feature denylist. There is no minimum-stays-per-hospital filter: the protocol projected before the extract was read that a 500-stay threshold would leave roughly 46 hospitals, and on the released extract it leaves 99 of the 207 — about 30 calibration clusters under Section 4.10's split, below the 50 the minimum-cluster gate requires — so that filter would be a direct threat to feasibility, not a modelling preference. And the primary arm applies no APACHE-availability filter, because 17 of the 207 primary-cohort hospitals contribute no rows to that table, so restricting to APACHE-covered stays deletes whole sites and changes the population the estimand averages over without changing a word of the guarantee.

# Data availability

This study uses two data sources. The synthetic cohorts of Sections 4.2–4.8 and 4.12–4.14 are generated deterministically by the included code, publicly available at https://github.com/Toepatella/certgate; the experiment grid regenerates Figures 4–5 and the synthetic Supplementary figures and tables from a single command (`python -m experiments.run_synthetic`), and Supplementary Tables S4–S5 from a second read-only command (`python -m experiments.panel_s2_tables`). The real-data results of Section 4.10 use the eICU Collaborative Research Database v2.0 [@pollard2019eicudb; @pollard2018eicu; @pollard2026physionet; @goldberger2000physionet], which the author accessed under the PhysioNet Credentialed Health Data License 1.5.0 and a signed Data Use Agreement 1.5.0. That dataset is **not redistributable by us**; as a matter of our own release policy under the agreement's disclosure-avoidance clauses, we treat derived record-level artifacts the same way, so every artifact we release from it is aggregate. eICU-CRD is available from PhysioNet (https://physionet.org/content/eicu-crd/2.0/) to any researcher who completes the required human-subjects training (CITI "Data or Specimens Only Research") and signs the same agreement, and the released code contains the complete extract-to-certificate pipeline, so Section 4.10 is reproducible by such a researcher from their own copy.
