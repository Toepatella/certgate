# CertGate: finite-sample certified selective prediction for multi-site clinical risk models, with label-shift robustness and explainable abstention

Tony Zhang$^{1}$

$^{1}$ Department of Physiology and Pharmacology, Western University, London, Ontario, Canada

**Corresponding author:** Tony Zhang, tzhan659@uwo.ca

---

**Abstract**

**Background** Records cluster by hospital, so record-level confidence guarantees can be silently overconfident. **Methods** CertGate is a selective-prediction gate: answer when confident, defer the rest. With the hospital as the unit of independence it certifies that the hospital-averaged answered error rate is within a 5% or 10% budget (rung) at confidence 0.95 per rung, 0.90 jointly for the reported rung, finite-sample in the exchangeable mode; no single hospital is bounded. A label-shift mode budgets its correction's uncertainty; a linear head attributes every answer and abstention exactly. **Results** On a 208-site synthetic cohort the 10% rung certifies all 200 replications, exceeding its budget on 0 of 200 fresh pools; under prevalence shift the uncorrected gate exceeds it on 97.5% (39.5% on a stricter per-site criterion) while the corrected mode declines; concept shift defeats it by design. On the eICU Collaborative Research Database (eICU-CRD) v2.0 (164,322 stays, 207 hospitals) the 10% rung certifies on all 20 by-site re-splits of one cohort, answering on average 89.0% of held-out stays; the 5% rung is refused; the label-shift mode fitted but issued no certificate. The Glasgow motor score is the leading abstention driver on every re-split, oxygenation and airway status next in varying order. **Conclusions** A hospital-level certificate is issuable at realistic scale at the 10% rung, with disclosures: on the published split answered mortality is 0.0458 against the pool's 0.0898, and the answered error, 0.0419, nearly equals that answered mortality; part of the budget is met by abstaining where deaths concentrate.

**Keywords** Explainable AI · Trustworthy AI · Selective prediction · Distribution-free uncertainty quantification · Label shift · Intensive care

# 1 Introduction

A clinical risk model rarely stays where it was built: a score calibrated on a handful of academic centers is deployed at a community hospital that contributed no training records, and it emits a probability for every patient regardless. A safer design wraps the model in a *selective gate* that answers the cases it is confident about and routes the rest to a clinician [@chow1970reject; @elyaniv2010selective]. The promise attached to such a gate is a high-confidence bound on the error rate among the answered cases — what a clinician weighs before trusting automated triage, and what an auditor asks to see documented. Under the way multi-site clinical data is actually distributed, that promise is often false, invisibly to the deployed system.

The object to certify is an *answered-set error rate* whose unit of independence is the **site** (hospital), not the record: records within a site share case mix, coding, and instrumentation, and a new site's prevalence is generally not the one calibration saw [@tripodcluster2023; @internalexternal2021]. A certificate treating records as exchangeable overruns its stated confidence once deployment is grouped by site [@zhou2026falsesense; @triage2026audit]. Selective prediction, cluster-as-unit distribution-free inference, and label-shift correction each have mature literatures (Section 2); multi-site deployment needs all three at once.

We present CertGate, a selective-prediction gate for this setting. The certificate treats the site as the atom of inference: each hospital's calibration data is compressed into one bounded summary, and a sequential "betting" test — the Waudby-Smith–Ramdas (WSR) martingale, which accumulates evidence hospital by hospital and certifies only when it crosses a preset bar [@waudbysmith2024betting; @ville1939] — decides whether the site-level mean error is within budget. The operating threshold comes from walking an ordered grid of candidates in a fixed sequence, which spends no extra confidence on the multiple looks [@angelopoulos2021ltt; @westfall2001fixedsequence], in the risk-control tradition [@bates2021rcps; @angelopoulos2024crc]. The gate ships in two assumption modes, and the mode is part of the guarantee text: an exchangeable baseline, and a label-shift mode that drops the shared-prevalence assumption through black-box shift estimation (BBSE) [@lipton2018bbse; @garg2020unified], whose four estimated parameters carry their own uncertainty inside the certificate [@si2024pac]. Because the deployed head is linear by design, every answer and every abstention carries exact linear attributions [@lundberg2017shap], attaching explanation to a certified decision rather than to an ungoverned reject option [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain; @fernandes2026abductivereject]. One assumption stays deliberately outside the guarantee: concept shift — a change in $P(y\mid x)$ — is undetectable from unlabeled data, so we do not claim to survive it. The parts are known; wiring them the obvious way goes wrong in three places the paper documents: capping each hospital's *observed* error contribution, the natural fix for unequal hospital sizes, can certify a rule whose true risk is 3.5 times the budget (Supplementary Information A.3); treating records rather than hospitals as the unit grants stricter budgets that the hospital unit refuses, and then breaks them (Section 4.8); and the label-shift correction must budget the uncertainty in the new hospital's own positive-call rate, a fourth estimated quantity, or it certifies on noise (Section 3.6).

Section 4 evaluates the gate twice over: nine synthetic experiment families — a validity grid, a label-shift stress test, a concept-shift negative control, a site-count frontier, an attribution study, a coverage-and-composition study, a record-as-unit comparator, a certificate stress and comparator suite, and two power frontiers — on a 208-site cohort where ground truth is available by construction, then the same pipeline end to end on eICU-CRD v2.0 under a protocol frozen before the extract was read (Section 4.10), with the explanation layer read on the real cohort (Section 4.11). Which rung is reachable turns out to be priced in hospitals and in how the estimand weights hospitals against records — the *site/record continuum*, from every hospital counting equally to every record counting equally, set by the influence cap of Section 3.3 — and, for a class-conditional budget, in whether a hospital's weight counts its records or its positives (Section 4.14); not in the choice of head. A reader who wants the clinical content first needs Section 3.7 (what the certificate promises, in seven clauses), Section 4.10 (the real-data certificate and its disclosures) and Section 4.11 (what the abstentions point at); the comparator, stress and capacity arms (Sections 4.8, 4.12–4.14) are for the reader checking the statistics.

The primary contributions are:

1. **A site-as-unit certified selective-prediction gate.** A finite-sample $(\alpha, 1-\delta)$ certificate bounding the influence-weighted error rate among answered cases, averaged over the site population, with the site rather than the record as the unit of independence. Its necessity is demonstrated, not assumed: on the same draws the record-as-unit certifier grants the $\alpha = 0.05$ budget the site-unit walk refuses, then violates its aggregate budget at nearly twice $\delta$ under high heterogeneity (Section 4.8); the site-count frontier prices the stricter budget in hospitals (Sections 4.2, 4.5).
2. **A label-shift mode that carries its own uncertainty.** A correction based on black-box shift estimation folds the estimation uncertainty of all four corrected parameters into the $1-\delta$ budget through a box of cluster-robust confidence intervals — three of them asymptotic, with the measured shortfall disclosed (Section 3.6) — with the assumption mode named in the guarantee text; it certifies or declines rather than issue a certificate it cannot support — replacing a baseline whose certificates exceed the budget on fresh pools in 97.5% of replications (hard-violating, on the stricter per-site criterion of Section 3.9, 39.5%) with a rule that declined at this scale (Section 4.3).
3. **Explained abstention on a certified gate, and what it shows on real data.** Each answer and abstention carries exact additive attributions under a named value function, including which features pushed a declined case below the confidence bar [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain; @fernandes2026abductivereject]. The attribution method is not new; what is measured is its behavior on a certified gate: on eICU the leading abstention driver is stable and, on face validity, physiologically coherent — the Glasgow motor score on all 20 re-splits, with oxygenation, airway status and eye opening behind it in an order that varies across re-splits — where the symmetric synthetic generator correctly yields none (Sections 4.6, 4.11), and the leading driver, unlike the ranking beneath it, survives the interventional-to-conditional value-function change.
4. **A validation design that can fail.** A concept-shift negative control verified capable of failing before it is run, and a protocol scoring the certificate against the estimand it actually claims (Section 4.4), answering a reported diagnosis of overconfident selective certificates [@zhou2026falsesense].

# 2 Related work

CertGate draws on four literatures that each solve part of the problem and stop one axis short.

*Certified selective prediction.* The reject option dates to Chow [@chow1970reject]; El-Yaniv and Wiener [@elyaniv2010selective] formalized the risk–coverage tradeoff, and Geifman and El-Yaniv [@geifman2017selective] turned it into a finite-sample selective-risk certificate on i.i.d. records, since sharpened by the distribution-free risk-control tradition [@bates2021rcps; @angelopoulos2021ltt; @angelopoulos2024crc], a 2025–26 wave of selective-risk work [@scrc2025; @score2026; @yu2026joint; @wang2026lec; @liu2026certifyrefuse], and betting-based tracking of a deployed model's risk — sequential monitoring rather than a one-shot certificate [@podkopaev2022tracking]; group-conditional selective risk control [@salem2026hgcrc] offers a per-group guarantee CertGate does not, bought with a Bonferroni split over a fixed group hierarchy and, again, i.i.d. records within groups. Yu and Liu [@yu2026joint] are closest — the same certificate shape, with an acceptance floor and a decline option — but, like the rest of the wave, over i.i.d. records; CertGate makes the site the unit of independence and carries a label-shift correction inside the budget.

*Conformal prediction and cluster exchangeability.* Conformal prediction [@vovk2005algorithmic] gives distribution-free conformal coverage — a set-validity guarantee, distinct from the answered fraction this paper calls coverage — under an exchangeability that multi-site clustering breaks. Dunn, Wasserman and Ramdas [@dunn2023hierarchical] and Lee, Barber and Willett [@lee2025hierarchical] supply the cluster-as-unit machinery CertGate reuses; conformal e-values over hierarchical data reach selection with false-discovery control — a different error criterion, and no gate certificate [@lee2025hierarchicalevalues]; Farinhas et al. [@farinhas2024nonexchangeable] relax record exchangeability inside conformal risk control itself, though controlling risk in expectation; a hierarchical conformal framework has since reached multi-hospital length-of-stay intervals [@shahbazi2026hierarchical]; and Shahid [@fedcrc2026] makes sites first-class in a federated risk control. These deliver conformal coverage or in-expectation risk, not a high-probability budget on the answered subset.

*Label shift.* Black-box shift estimation [@lipton2018bbse] corrects a moving prevalence by confusion-matrix inversion; regularized [@azizzadenesheli2019rlls], maximum-likelihood [@alexandari2020labelshift] and unified [@garg2020unified] variants refine the weights, and Podkopaev and Ramdas [@podkopaev2021labelshift] give reweighted-conformal coverage — all record-level point corrections. Abstention has been adapted to label shift without a guarantee [@alexandari2018abstention], and federated conformal prediction carries the correction across clients for conformal coverage rather than answered-set risk [@plassier2023federatedlabelshift]. Si et al. [@si2024pac] are nearest, propagating confusion-matrix uncertainty into the guarantee rather than treating the correction as exact; CertGate adopts that pattern for a bound on answered-set risk, with the uncertainty made cluster-robust by a bootstrap over sites.

*Multi-site clinical validation and explainable abstention.* Clinical practice already treats the site as the unit — TRIPOD-Cluster [@tripodcluster2023] and internal–external cross-validation [@internalexternal2021] — but reports point estimates, not certificates. Recent audits sharpen the gap: record-level selective-risk rules overrun their budget by 9–30% under grouped deployment [@zhou2026falsesense], the same overrun appears when documents rather than fields are the cluster [@gurram2026perfield], and marginal conformal coverage cannot certify a grouped release under shift [@triage2026audit]; conformal selective prediction with cost-aware deferral has meanwhile been proposed for clinical triage under distribution shift [@scireports2026deferral], and conformal prediction with SHAP explanations has been validated on eICU itself, descriptively [@cpmors2024jmir]. Reject-option explanation methods — up to provably minimal explanations of a linear model's rejections [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain; @l2lore2025; @fernandes2026abductivereject] — attach no statistical guarantee to the decision; CertGate's difference is exact attributions on a certified gate. The counterfactual-explanation and recourse literatures [@wachter2017counterfactual; @ustun2019recourse; @karimi2021survey] supply the plausibility and actionability models that the gate's smallest-change read-out deliberately does not carry (Section 4.11).

# 3 Methods

## 3.1 Problem setting: the site as the unit of independence

We consider multi-site clinical data: on the order of two hundred sites contributing 20 to 5,000 records each, outcome prevalence near 9–10%, sites differing in both prevalence and case mix. A deployed risk model produces a bounded score $s(x) \in [0.5, 1]$ — the model's confidence in its own call for the binary outcome $y \in \{0,1\}$, made precise below — and answers a case only when that score clears an operating threshold $\tau$; otherwise it abstains and defers to human judgment. The *coverage* of a deployment is its answered fraction — the share of cases the gate answers — and the word is used in that sense throughout, not in the sense of a conformal set's coverage. The object we construct is a *certificate*: with probability at least $1-\delta$ over the draw of calibration sites, the influence-weighted answered-set risk — the parameter $R_M$ of Section 3.3 — *averaged over the population of sites from which the calibration sites were drawn* is at most $\alpha$, under an explicitly tagged assumption about how deployment differs from calibration. This is a site-population average, not a per-site promise: under between-site heterogeneity an individual site's answered risk can exceed $\alpha$ while the average stays inside budget, at a rate the certificate neither measures nor bounds (clauses in Section 3.7; dispersion measured in Section 4.2). Figure 1 lays out the pipeline.

The certified quantity is an *error* rate, so its meaning is fixed by what the deployment supplies: an index event, a prediction time at which the features freeze, and an ascertainment window resolving $y$; CertGate certifies whatever binary label the caller supplies and does not itself resolve censoring, competing risks, or a time horizon. The head's hard label is $\hat{y}_i = \mathbb{1}[\hat{p}(x_i) \ge 1/2]$, the per-record error is $\text{err}_i = \mathbb{1}[\hat{y}_i \neq y_i]$, and the selective score $s(x) = \max(\hat{p}(x), 1 - \hat{p}(x))$ withholds cases near the decision boundary in either direction. The loss is symmetric 0–1 — a modeling choice, and not a neutral one: at single-digit prevalence most answered errors are false negatives (77% on the validity grid of Section 4.2), and an error budget near the outcome prevalence is close to what a constant always-negative rule achieves — the operating characteristics of Section 4.2 and the composition instrument of Section 3.8 exist to make that visible. A cost-weighted loss would require a different atom (Section 6.1).

## 3.2 Data discipline and lightweight pre-registration

Sites are partitioned disjointly — the fractions count sites, not records — into $S_{\text{train}}$ (40%, fits the risk model), $S_{\text{aux}}$ (20%, orders the threshold walk and fits the label-shift machinery), and $S_{\text{cal}}$ (40%, certification only); every fitted object is a function of $(S_{\text{train}}, S_{\text{aux}}, \text{target features})$ alone, and a site appearing in two pools aborts the run — internal–external cross-validation discipline [@internalexternal2021]. All governing constants are fixed a priori and pinned literally by an automated test — machine-verifiable evidence that no constant has drifted since the freeze commit, which does not time-stamp the freeze; pinning is not justifying, so Supplementary Table S1 lists every frozen constant with its role and basis. The one constant that materially moves the certificate, the influence cap $M$, is swept in Section 4.9.

## 3.3 The certified estimand: influence-weighted answered-set risk

Site sizes are heavy-tailed, so an unweighted record mean would let a single large site dominate the certified quantity. The natural fix — capping each site's *realized* contribution — is demonstrably anti-conservative (a construction with 17.5% true risk certifies at $\alpha = 5\%$; Supplementary Information A.3). Instead, each site receives an *outcome-independent* influence weight $g_c = \min(n_c, M)$ with $M = 100$ — a function of site size only, never of errors — and the certified quantity is

$$R_M = \frac{\sum_c g_c\, a_c\, e_c}{\sum_c g_c\, a_c},$$

where $a_c$ is the answered fraction and $e_c$ the answered-set error rate at site $c$. The cap acts on influence only — it scales each site's whole signed contribution, never censoring the error itself. On this size distribution $89.5\%$ of sites exceed 100 records, so $R_M$ is close to an unweighted site average: a 5,000-record hospital and a 100-record one carry identical influence — a substantive choice about whose errors count, measured at other $M$ in Section 4.9.

Certification compresses each calibration hospital into one bounded number — its *atom* — carrying how far its answered error runs above or below budget:

$$Z_c = \frac{g_c}{M\, n_c}\sum_{i \in c} \text{ans}_i\,(\text{err}_i - \alpha) + \alpha \;\in\; [0, 1],$$

constructed so that $\mathbb{E}[Z] \le \alpha \iff R_M \le \alpha$: the inner sum collapses to $n_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)$, so $Z_c - \alpha = g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)/M$, tying each atom to the estimand term by term. The construction extends to class-conditional budgets: label-weighting the contributions ($\text{weights} = y$, cap unchanged) makes the certified quantity the influence-weighted false-negative rate among answered positives, boundedness and weight outcome-independence preserved; Section 4.14 measures where such budgets become issuable. Sites that answer nothing enter as *neutral* atoms $Z_c = \alpha$ — exactly on budget, so such a site neither helps nor hurts the bet of Section 3.4 (its wealth factor $1 + \lambda(\alpha - Z_c)$ is 1) — rather than being dropped, which would narrow the population the guarantee refers to; separately, the *minimum-cluster gate* requires at least 50 record-carrying calibration hospitals. The expectation defining the estimand is over the *site draw*: $R_M$ is a population aggregate, and what follows from that — the scope of the claim, the dispersion it does not bound, the difference between a parameter and a realized count, and the event every rung shares — is stated once in Section 3.7.

## 3.4 The certification test: betting on the calibration sites

Once each calibration hospital has been reduced to one bounded atom $Z_c$, certification becomes a finite-sample test of whether the mean of those atoms exceeds the budget. We test the null $H_0: \mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ with the Waudby-Smith–Ramdas betting martingale for means of bounded random variables [@waudbysmith2024betting]. The test needs one assumption:

> **Assumption 1 (calibration sampling).** The calibration sites are independent and identically distributed draws from the site population over which the estimand $R_M$ of Section 3.3 is defined. In particular it implies the weaker condition the argument actually uses: given the sites already processed (the history $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$), the next site's atom has the population mean, $\mathbb{E}[Z_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = \mathbb{E}[Z]$.

It does not follow from boundedness; sites violating it are outside the argument (Section 6.1 records the unmodeled temporal dependence). Processing the calibration sites in a fixed order, the wealth process is

$$K_t = \prod_{s \le t}\bigl(1 + \lambda_s(\alpha - Z_s)\bigr), \qquad \lambda_t = \min\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)}{\hat{\sigma}^2_{t-1}\, n}},\; \frac{0.9}{1-\alpha}\right),$$

with $n$ the number of calibration clusters and $\hat{\sigma}^2_{t-1}$ the running variance of the atoms processed so far (floored at $10^{-8}$), its running mean and variance initialized at $(0.5, 0.25)$ — the widest a $[0,1]$ variable can have, and data-independent, which is what keeps the bet predictable; we certify if and only if $\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta$. In betting terms, the test starts with unit capital, stakes a fraction $\lambda_t$ on each successive hospital's atom landing below budget, and certifies only if the capital $K_t$ ever grows by $1/\delta$. Under $H_0$ the bet is *predictable* — computed only from hospitals already processed — and capped so every wealth factor stays positive, making $K_t$ a nonnegative supermartingale (it cannot grow in expectation), and Ville's inequality bounds the probability of ever crossing the bar by $\delta$ [@ville1939] — finite-sample, with no distributional assumption beyond boundedness and the sampling condition of Assumption 1 (Supplementary Information A.1 gives the four steps). The processing order is a deterministic permutation seeded from the protocol constants, carrying no target identifier — predictable, and unchoosable to flatter the outcome. The *information floor* $\ln(1/\delta)(1-\alpha)/n$ — the smallest margin any valid level-$\delta$ test of a $[0,1]$-bounded mean can certify at $n$ clusters, so a rung whose margin (the budget minus the answered error, in atom units) sits below it cannot certify at that site count; a feasibility diagnostic, never a gate — sets the frontier the site-count sweep of Section 4.5 maps.

## 3.5 Threshold selection and the budget ladder

The operating threshold is chosen from a grid of 23 values in $[0.55, 0.99]$, walked in a fixed sequence ordered by estimated certification margin on $S_{\text{aux}}$ — how far each candidate's estimated answered error sits below $\alpha$ — most conservative first; each is tested at the mode's full betting budget, stopping at the first failure, so the multiple looks cost no confidence [@westfall2001fixedsequence; @angelopoulos2021ltt; @bates2021rcps]. Selection happens at three levels: *within a mode*, the maximum-coverage threshold in the certified prefix (the lowest certified threshold maximizes coverage); *across modes*, the most conservative (highest) certified threshold; *across rungs*, the strictest certified $\alpha$. Each candidate budget is a *rung* of the two-rung ladder $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$, certified by separate walks and reported rung by rung (Section 4.5, Supplementary Table S6) — each valid at $1-\delta$, the selected claim jointly at $1-2\delta = 0.90$, as the guarantee states. The asymmetry with the threshold walk is deliberate: within one rung the walk is a fixed sequence that stops at its first failure, so the thresholds are not competing hypotheses and the level is spent once, whereas the two rungs are two separate tests of which the stricter passing one is reported, and selecting the better of two level-$\delta$ tests is what costs the union bound. At 208 sites (about 83 calibration clusters) the expected *operative* rung — the strictest that certifies — is $\alpha = 0.10$.

## 3.6 Assumption modes

**Baseline (exchangeable).** In plain terms: the new hospital is assumed to be the kind of hospital the calibration hospitals were drawn from. The target site is assumed exchangeable with the calibration sites and all record weights are one. Tag: "if exchangeability holds."

**Label shift (black-box shift estimation, BBSE).** In plain terms: the new hospital may see more or fewer deaths than the calibration hospitals, but a patient who dies or survives looks the same there; the gate estimates how much the death rate has moved from the model's own positive-call rate, and treats that estimate as uncertain rather than exact. Only the outcome prevalence may differ between target and calibration: the target's $P(x \mid y)$ must equal the pooled source's, a condition a single new hospital cannot be assumed to satisfy under site-varying case mix — the tag names that condition, it does not check it. We estimate the head's confusion rates on $S_{\text{aux}}$ — its false- and true-positive rates $c_0, c_1$ — observe the target predicted-positive rate $q$, and invert $q = c_0(1-\pi_t) + c_1\pi_t$ for the target prevalence [@lipton2018bbse; @garg2020unified]; certification reweights calibration records by class with the target-to-source odds ratio $\rho$. One approximation is disclosed rather than modeled: the correction estimates the record-pooled source prevalence, while the estimand's effective source mix is the influence-weighted site average — exact where the two coincide, approximate otherwise. Estimation uncertainty for all four parameters rides inside the guarantee, subject to the disclosure at the end of this paragraph [@si2024pac]: one interval each — cluster bootstraps over $S_{\text{aux}}$ sites for $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}})$ [@efron1979; @fieldwelsh2007]; exact Clopper–Pearson [@clopperpearson1934] (single-site pools) or cluster bootstrap (ten-plus declared sites) for $q$ — held as a box at Bonferroni [@dunn1961] $\delta_{\text{conf}}/4$ and propagated to $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ over the sixteen corners. Certification tests both endpoints at $\delta_{\text{bet}}$; this suffices because the sign deciding certification is affine in $\rho$, so the certifiable set is convex (Supplementary Information A.2). The budgets combine as $\delta_{\text{conf}} + \delta_{\text{bet}} = \delta$ with $\delta_{\text{conf}} = \delta_{\text{bet}} = 0.025$. One disclosure travels with every BBSE statement: the three percentile bootstraps are asymptotic, and their finite-cluster error is material at this scale [@cameron2008cluster] — a design-time probe measured the three-bootstrap box's joint miscoverage at $0.038$ against a nominal $\le 0.019$, and a post-hoc probe with the head held fixed (400 fits at each of 36, 42 and 74 auxiliary sites; Supplementary Information A.2) measures the four-parameter box's joint miscoverage at 0.042–0.056 against a nominal $\le 0.025$, so the mode's printed 0.95 reads as about 0.93 (0.914 at the conservative end of the probe's interval) — read this mode's printed confidence with that shortfall in mind; it touches no real-data certificate, because the label-shift mode issued none on eICU (Section 4.10), and the finite-sample claim holds in full only for the exchangeable mode. The mode *declines* whenever its inputs cannot support a correction — an empty target pool, two to nine declared target sites, a degenerate bootstrap, a confusion gap $(c_1 - c_0) < 0.10$, or a $q$ interval outside the box's confusion range $[c_{0,\text{lo}}, c_{1,\text{hi}}]$ (box corners that still imply a prevalence outside $(0,1)$ are clipped, and a fit whose clipped box certifies at neither endpoint resolves as failsafe); no shrinkage, no fallback to the uncorrected baseline. Declines are reported as *misspecified* (an input gate fired) or *failsafe* (the mode fit but no threshold certified at both endpoints).

**Combination, and what no mode covers.** The modes run as alternatives, each spending its own betting budget; we deploy the most conservative certified threshold, and a mode is listed only if its certified prefix contains it, so the combined guarantee reads "if *either* tagged assumption holds." Concept shift — a change in $P(y \mid x)$ — is invisible to unlabeled target data, and under it the certificate can be confidently wrong; the control of Section 4.4 injects a posterior shift *verified* capable of pushing answered error above $\alpha$ and shows the certificate fail — the tags mark a real boundary.

## 3.7 The certified guarantee

In plain terms, the certificate promises one thing and refuses four: it promises that, across the kind of hospitals calibration sampled and with each hospital weighted comparably, the share of answered cases the model gets wrong is at most $\alpha$, with probability $1-\delta$ over which hospitals happened to calibrate it and, because the reported rung is chosen from a two-rung ladder, at 0.90 for the rung reported; it does not promise this for any single hospital, for any single batch's realized count, for a hospital whose feature–outcome relationship has changed, or at a confidence stronger than the one printed. The clauses below are the deployed guarantee text, frozen as exact strings per mode combination; weakening or strengthening one is a specification change, not an editorial one.
(1) *Estimand.* Under exchangeability the certificate bounds the influence-weighted answered-set risk averaged over the population of sites from which the calibration sites were drawn, with the probability taken over the calibration draw; under label shift it bounds that same site-population risk reweighted to the target class prevalence the BBSE correction identifies, with the probability taken over the joint draw of the calibration sites, the auxiliary split, and the target pool. (2) *Dispersion.* This bounds a site-population average and not any individual site's answered error rate: under between-site heterogeneity individual sites can exceed $\alpha$ while the average stays within budget, at a rate the certificate does not measure or bound. (3) *Parameter, not count.* It does not bound any batch's realized error count, which exceeds $\alpha$ at binomial-dispersion rates even under a valid certificate — at the $\alpha$ boundary approaching one batch in two as batches grow. (4) *Shared event (exchangeable mode).* In the exchangeable mode the certified thresholds are a function of the calibration draw alone, so one $1-\delta$ event is shared by every target pool the certificate is applied to; the clause is omitted from certificates carrying the label-shift mode, because the BBSE fit depends on the target pool through $q$ and no shared event exists there. (5) *Operative rung.* The operative rung is the strictest certified $\alpha$, a data-driven selection over the $\{0.05, 0.10\}$ ladder, so the selected claim holds jointly at $1 - 2\delta = 0.90$. (6) *Concept shift.* Concept shift and combined shift are out of scope and undetectable from unlabeled data; the certificate is void there. (7) *Label-shift additions.* The $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ box covers four estimated parameters $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}}, q_{\text{target}})$ at Bonferroni $\delta_{\text{conf}} = 0.025$, spent over the auxiliary split and the target pool rather than the calibration draw; the $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}})$ intervals are percentile cluster bootstraps — asymptotic, with realized joint coverage measurably below nominal at small cluster counts (Section 3.6) — while the $q_{\text{target}}$ interval is finite-sample Clopper–Pearson for single-site pools and a cluster bootstrap for multi-site pools, with $\delta_{\text{bet}} = 0.025$ spent on the betting test.

## 3.8 Explainability: answers and abstentions

The deployed head is L2-regularized logistic regression ($C = 1.0$), chosen so that the answer/abstain decision is a transparent linear function of the features rather than a black box; the gate itself is model-agnostic — the score only *ranks* cases, and the certificate's validity never depends on the model's quality — so a different head can be substituted: validity is unaffected and selective quality is priced as coverage, measured rather than asserted in Section 4.13.

Three artifacts make the gate explainable. *Globally*, standardized coefficients give each feature's direction and strength. *Locally*, an answered case carries exact additive attributions $\phi_j(x) = w_j(x_j - \mu_j)$ with $\text{logit}(\hat{p}(x)) = \text{base} + \sum_j \phi_j$, where $w_j$ is the raw-feature-space coefficient; for a linear model these attributions coincide, in closed form and with no sampling, with the Shapley values of the interventional, independent-baseline game [@lundberg2017shap]. *For declined cases*, answering requires $|\text{logit}\,\hat{p}(x)| \ge \text{logit}\,\tau^*$, so the gate reports the margin-to-answer $m(x) = \text{logit}\,\tau^* - |\text{logit}\,\hat{p}(x)| > 0$ together with each feature's signed contribution to the logit read against the bar — which features pulled toward each class, leaving confidence below the certified bar; the margin is what remains after the intercept and the contributions net out. Explaining reject options is itself established [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain]; what the gate adds is that the same exact attributions accompany a *certified* decision. The released code renders these artifacts as one self-contained page in two registers — a plain-language reading written for non-specialist readers, which carries the what-if controls and the smallest-change flip under a fixed caveat, and an arithmetic register (logits, margins, attributions, the logit waterfall, full-precision tables and per-hospital panels) for auditors; the threshold explorer appears in both registers and is fenced on the page as intuition-only, outside the certified path — with the certificate's scope clauses and the page's own caveat — that every interactive control asks a score-space question of the gate, not a causal claim, a treatment suggestion or a clinically achievable action — fixed on the page rather than left to the reader; it is a transparency surface, and its effect on clinical decisions is not evaluated here (Section 6.1).

A fourth artifact guards a subtler failure: the answered-set class composition is reported three ways — predicted-class, BBSE-implied true-class (tagged), and oracle true-class (a retrospective diagnostic) — together revealing whether a certificate was earned by answering only easy negatives.

## 3.9 Validation protocol

Validation scores the certificate against the estimand it actually claims — scoring a population-average guarantee against single-site draws would test a claim it does not make. One conformance metric carries the $\delta$ target: **aggregate conformance**, the fraction of certified draws whose $R_M$ on a fresh 200-site evaluation pool exceeds $\alpha$, required to stay at or below $\delta$ over 200 independent calibration draws. Two diagnostics deliberately carry none. The first is the single-fresh-site **hard-violation rate** (a site counts as violated only when the one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound [@wilson1927] on its answered error exceeds $\alpha$), reported beside the mean fraction of evaluation-pool sites exceeding $\alpha$ — the between-site dispersion the certificate declines to bound. The second is the **realized exceedance rate** against its binomial reference, since small answered sets exceed $\alpha$ by chance at rates approaching 50% at the boundary. The Wilson criterion is deliberately conservative, so meeting the target shows the *absence of gross violations at the power this harness has*; the guarantee itself rests on the finite-sample level of the betting test, not on this harness. Every rate reported over the replication draws — the conformance metric and both diagnostics — carries an exact Clopper–Pearson 95% interval [@clopperpearson1934] on its numerator and denominator, emitted beside the rate by the experiment runner, so a rate near zero is read with the width the replication count actually supports rather than as a point.

## 3.10 Reproducibility

Every result regenerates deterministically from the released code: one protocol seed drives the whole grid through a fixed hashing rule, and a single command reproduces the synthetic grid's figures and tables (Figures 4 and 5 in the main text; Supplementary Figures S3–S9 and Tables S2–S3, S6 and S9), with identical inputs yielding byte-identical certificates. The real-data results of Section 4.10 come from a separate runner over the eICU extract, which we cannot redistribute; the command, protocol, and aggregate artifacts are released, so any credentialed holder of the data can reproduce the analysis. Figure 3 and Supplementary Table S10 are likewise derived: the figure is rendered by a read-only command from the released aggregate diagnostics, and the table by the eICU runner's post-hoc block on a separate output directory. Supplementary Information A.3 details these commands, package versions, the seeding rule, and provenance.

# 4 Results

We evaluate CertGate on a synthetic multi-site cohort whose every generative mechanism is known, then on eICU-CRD v2.0. The synthetic harness exists for *oracle access*: the true answered-set risk is computable, so a certificate can be checked rather than assumed — a real cohort can measure a violation but cannot separate a sound method from a benign cohort. Because record-level selective certificates have been shown to overrun under grouped deployment [@zhou2026falsesense], the design includes a verified-falsifiable negative control and a record-as-unit comparator on the same draws.

## 4.1 Experimental setup

The generator follows a frozen specification (released as `data.py`): 208 sites with clipped-lognormal sizes spanning roughly 20 to 5,000 records; $d = 8$ features with the class signal on one normalized direction supported by features 0–3; base prevalence 0.095 with a site random effect $u_c \sim \mathcal{N}(0, s_u^2)$, $s_u = 0.5$, on the outcome log-odds, so per-site prevalence varies and no two sites share a prevalence, while $P(x \mid y)$ is the same at every site — pure label shift by construction, exactly the condition the BBSE mode assumes (Section 3.6). The profile resembles eICU-CRD's hospital count, size range and prevalence (208 hospitals at 8.9% prevalence, Section 4.10; the synthetic 208 sites and the database's 208 hospitals are distinct cohorts that happen to share a count), with between-site heterogeneity of the kind clustered-validation guidance expects [@tripodcluster2023; @internalexternal2021]; the random-effect scale is a design choice, swept in Section 4.2. Labels are fully ascertained and carry no time axis; the clinical-target specification of Section 3.1 binds real deployments, not these experiments. Splits are site-disjoint 40/20/40; the head is L2 logistic regression fit on $S_{\text{train}}$. Everything runs under protocol seed 20260721 over the $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$ ladder at $\delta = 0.05$; statistical experiments replicate over $R = 200$ calibration draws unless stated — the label-shift power frontier uses 50 per point, the label-shift magnitude sweep 100 and the aleatoric frontier 300, and the explanation case study and the coverage-and-composition arm are single draws — the draw being the unit of replication, scored per Section 3.9 with exact Clopper–Pearson 95% intervals.

## 4.2 Validity in distribution

With target sites exchangeable with calibration, the $\alpha = 0.10$ rung certifies all 200 draws at mean coverage 0.9828 (minimum 0.871) and conforms on every one: 0 of 200 exceed $\alpha$ (exact 95% CI $[0, 0.018]$) at mean fresh-pool $R_M$ 0.0567, half the budget (Supplementary Figure S3). The per-site dispersion diagnostic records what the certificate declines to bound: the single-fresh-site hard-violation rate is 0.02, and raising the site random effect $s_u$ from 0.5 to 1.0 and 2.0 leaves aggregate conformance untouched (0.0 exceedances in all three arms) while the diagnostic detaches, as the design intends rather than as a failure: the hard rate moves from 0.02 to 0.10 at $s_u = 1.0$ and 0.095 at $s_u = 2.0$, and the mean per-site exceedance fraction climbs from 0.055 to 0.1285 and 0.1634 across the three arms — individual sites exceeding the budget while the population average holds, the signature the guarantee's dispersion clause (Section 3.7) names, and the reason the $\delta$ target attaches to the aggregate, not the diagnostic. The realized exceedance rate, 0.08 overall, sits far below its binomial reference in every answered-set size bin (Supplementary Table S2) — the parameter-versus-realized-count distinction in action. The stricter $\alpha = 0.05$ rung certifies nothing at 208 sites; Section 4.5 maps what that budget would require.

**Operating characteristics.** A budget on an error rate can be met by a degenerate rule, so Supplementary Table S5 reports what the answered and declined sets contain: sensitivity 0.550 with $76.9\%$ of answered errors false negatives — the asymmetry the symmetric loss does not price — while the declined 1.8% of records carries positive fraction 0.492, roughly $4.7\times$ the pools' prevalence, routing $8.6\%$ of all positives to a clinician. The gate is not meeting its budget by answering only easy negatives (answered positive fraction 0.0968 against the pools' 0.1039); a constant always-negative rule errs at 0.1039 here, so the error budget alone is a weak test — the sensitivity and composition columns carry the evidence.

## 4.3 Label-shift stress test

We shift site-level prevalence from 0.095 to 0.22 — the kind of increase a changed referral pattern produces — and compare the exchangeable baseline against the BBSE mode [@lipton2018bbse]; the target pool is declared as a single site, as a deployment batch would be. The uncorrected baseline is the motivating failure: at $\alpha = 0.10$ it certifies all 200 draws and hard-violates 39.5% of them (exact 95% CI $[0.327, 0.466]$, far above $\delta = 0.05$) — and against the certified estimand itself, the influence-weighted answered risk on a fresh label-shifted pool exceeds $\alpha$ for 97.5% of its certificates (Figure S9, left). BBSE issues no certificate on this configuration — it declines all 200 draws (194 failsafe, 6 misspecified), the designed behavior: once the target predicted-positive rate carries its own confidence share, a single-site target pool of this size cannot support a certified rung under this shift. A magnitude sweep through target base rates $\{0.095, 0.13, 0.16, 0.19, 0.22\}$ separates a working correction from a reflexive decliner (Figure S9, right): the baseline's failure is dose-responsive while BBSE's certify-and-violate rate — the share of *all* draws on which it both certifies and hard-violates on its single fresh target site, the Wilson criterion of Section 3.9, a joint rate with no conditioning on certification (the mode mostly declines, so "certified and wrong" is rare partly because "certified" is rare) — is 0.0 at *every* magnitude — it certifies 9% of draws at the null point, never above 5% under genuine shift at this scale. The mode certifies when the pool supports it and refuses in proportion to what the shift destroys; at this scale its demonstrated value under a genuine shift is the refusal itself — Section 4.14 measures the source capacity at which that changes.

## 4.4 Concept-shift negative control

The harness first *verifies the poison*: the injected posterior shift (a tilt of 2.0 added to the outcome log-odds given the features, changing $P(y \mid x)$ while leaving the features untouched) is confirmed to raise mean realized answered error to 0.161, above budget; a shift failing the check aborts the run. Given a verified poison the certificate certifies all 200 draws and hard-violates 70.0% (exact 95% CI $[0.631, 0.763]$); against the certified estimand the failure is total — 100% exceed $\alpha$ on a fresh tilted pool (Supplementary Figure S4). This is the intended result: concept shift is the failure mode the guarantee explicitly excludes, and the exclusion marks a real boundary rather than a rhetorical one [@zhou2026falsesense]. The control runs at one verified magnitude — it demonstrates that the harness can register a violation, not how the certificate degrades across concept-shift severities.

## 4.5 Site-count frontier

This is the capacity-planning study: it sweeps the site count over $\{60, 100, 150, 208, 300, 400\}$ at both rungs (Supplementary Table S6, Figure S7), as an independent experiment with its own seed, so its 208-site coverage differs from Section 4.2's 0.9828 only in the third decimal (the two other readings of that operating point are recorded in Supplementary Table S4's caption). At $\alpha = 0.10$ certification switches on at 150 sites and holds; the 60- and 100-site declines come from the minimum-cluster gate of Section 3.3, not the information floor — different failure modes with different remedies. At $\alpha = 0.05$ the budget first appears at 300 (certify rate 0.285) and is reliable only at 400. The frontier is set by the information floor scaling as $1/n_{\text{cal}}$: a stricter answered-set budget is a claim about how many hospitals one has and about where the estimand sits on the site/record continuum (Section 4.9) — and, for a class-conditional budget, about the atom's normalisation (Section 4.14) — not about the head.

## 4.6 Attribution study

Because the head is linear, the answer/abstain decision carries the exact attributions of Section 3.8. In a single-deployment case study ($\tau^* = 0.55$; 200 answered, 2 declined) the global importances recover the generator — features 0–3 dominate, features 4–7 are negligible — and each declined case's margin-to-answer localizes its deficit to named features (Supplementary Figure S5). Replicated over $R = 200$ draws (186 with declines; 2,644 declined cases pooled; pooled decline rate 2.15%), the single-draw reading does not survive: no stable single abstention driver exists (modal top-feature share 0.274 against a 0.25 chance level) — exactly what the generator's symmetric construction predicts, and we report the null as a null. What does replicate is the *shape*: declined cases carry systematically larger attribution magnitudes on every informative feature (mean gaps $-0.21$ to $-0.26$) — contested cases whose large pulls cancel. The instrument correctly finds no privileged feature here; the real cohort (Section 4.10) yields a stable one.

## 4.7 Per-site coverage and composition

The coverage-and-composition study deploys a single draw ($\tau^* = 0.55$; 40 target sites) and asks whether coverage is uniform across site sizes — do small hospitals receive worse selective service? — and how the gate earns its low answered error. Coverage is essentially flat across size bins (0.9896, 0.9800, 0.9856; Supplementary Table S3) with mean answered error below $\alpha$ in every bin (0.0523, 0.0670, 0.0595; Supplementary Figure S8), and no site answers nothing. Here, unlike on the validity grid, the certified quantity and the record-level average separate: $R_M$ is 0.0619 against an unweighted answered error of 0.0555 — the influence weighting counting the 15 mid-sized sites (answered error 0.0670) as heavily as the 21 largest (0.0595) — which is what it exists to do.

The composition analysis (Supplementary Table S9) is the instrument that tells us *how* a 9.5%-prevalence task yields a sub-$\alpha$ answered error. Over 25,314 answered records the predicted-class positive fraction is 0.0630, the BBSE-implied true-class fraction 0.0858, and the oracle fraction 0.0915 — just under the cohort prevalence, so the sub-$\alpha$ error is not bought by answering only easy negatives; the predicted-class column alone would have understated the true positive load by about a third. A post-hoc reliability panel over the same partition — a descriptive diagnostic added after the synthetic grid was published; it moves no certified quantity — adds that the answered set is close to calibrated at the deployed threshold (expected calibration error 0.002914), that its skill margin over the constant rule is 0.035948, and that the answered-minus-all contrast straddles zero — the composition reading by a second instrument.

## 4.8 Record-as-unit comparator

The design's premise — record-as-unit certificates are overconfident on clustered data — entered as a citation [@zhou2026falsesense]; the comparator tests it on the same draws: identical betting machinery with each *record* as one atom, on a 2,000-record subsample of $S_{\text{cal}}$ — still roughly 24 times the site count. At $\alpha = 0.05$ — the rung the site-unit walk refuses on every one of 400 draws across both heterogeneity arms — the record-unit certifier *grants* the budget almost always (certify rate 1.0 at $s_u = 0.5$, 0.99 at $s_u = 2.0$) and then violates the aggregate budget in 3.5% and 9.6% of those draws respectively — the latter nearly twice $\delta$ (Supplementary Figure S6).

Two readings temper the demonstration. At the default heterogeneity the 3.5% violation rate sits within $\delta$ — the outright failure that contribution 1 cites belongs to the high-heterogeneity arm ($s_u = 2.0$) alone — and at $\alpha = 0.10$ both units certify on all but one of 400 draws, the difference showing in conduct rather than validity: the record unit deploys laxer thresholds under heterogeneity (mean $\tau$ 0.587 against 0.747) and runs at fresh-pool risk 0.081 against the site unit's 0.052, most of the way to the boundary. The record-as-unit certifier buys its extra rungs and coverage by spending confidence clustered data may not have; the price surfaces as heterogeneity grows, and the real cohort's dispersion (Section 4.10) sits below even the default arm.

## 4.9 Sensitivity to the frozen influence cap

Of the frozen constants, $M$ is the one that visibly moves the certificate, so the validity grid's exchangeable arm is re-run at $M \in \{25, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 5000\}$, re-deriving the walk and rescoring against $R_M$ at each value (Supplementary Table S4): a plateau across $M \le 100$, then certification erodes toward the record-proportional end, where nothing certifies at 208 sites. No $M$ certifies *more* than the frozen value and the stricter rung is unreachable at every $M$, so the stricter rung's frontier of Section 4.5 is not an artifact of this constant, while the operative rung's reachability at 208 sites does depend on it: at this cluster count only the site-equal end of the site/record continuum is certifiable at all.

## 4.10 eICU-CRD v2.0 — the certificate on real multi-site data

This section reports what the same pipeline did on a cohort nobody constructed. The eICU Collaborative Research Database v2.0 [@pollard2019eicudb; @pollard2018eicu; @pollard2026physionet; @goldberger2000physionet] contains 200,859 ICU stays from 139,367 patients at 208 US hospitals in 2014–2015, under credentialed access and a data use agreement forbidding sharing access. We extend that obligation, as our own release policy, to any record-level derived artifact: every artifact behind this section is aggregate, and the release code refuses to write anything record-level.

The cohort predicates, a 161-column feature allowlist (everything not explicitly allowed is excluded), a 36-entry leak denylist, the split arithmetic, seven numbered predictions and five failure criteria were written from the database's published schema and column documentation and committed before the extract was downloaded (commit `9f25b491b2554d0a4bd7aaaf44081c185d01715f`, 2026-07-30 — the repository's first commit, which already carries the five pre-download amendments A1–A5, each made on mock corpora); their outcomes are reported where they arise, and no failure criterion fired. The certified run was committed four and a half hours after the freeze, with one post-hoc protocol amendment logged between the two (Scope, below); the chronology and the limits of a self-hosted freeze are set out in Supplementary Information A.3.

**Cohort.** The unit is the first ICU stay of each hospital admission, the outcome in-hospital mortality from a single documented column, the site the hospital. Table 1 runs 200,859 raw stays down to 164,322 over 207 hospitals at 8.89% prevalence, recording deaths at every step so an outcome-correlated exclusion surfaces as a prevalence movement. Features are the APACHE day-1 worst values, so the prediction time is the close of ICU day 1, or the end of the stay if earlier: the head is a retrospective day-1 instrument, not an hour-N bedside one. The distinction matters here because 30.0% of the 164,322 stays, and 30.6% of the stays that ended in in-hospital death, left the ICU before hour 24 (post-hoc); in the target pools sub-24 h stays are 0.302 of the pool, 0.313 of the answered set and 0.217 of the declined set, only 7.8% of them are declined, and 52.1% of the deaths among sub-24 h stays are declined — those stays are scored retrospectively, and no real-time answer would have existed for them. A missing numeric is filled with the $S_{\text{train}}$ column mean beside a per-column missingness indicator, so the certified path sees only finite inputs (Section 6.1); ages over 89, masked by the distributor, are mapped to 90 with an explicit `age_masked` indicator rather than dropped, so the 75+ band of Table S8 contains that stratum. Three standard eICU cohort filters — a length-of-stay floor, a minimum-stays cut, an APACHE-completeness requirement — are deliberately absent: each would move the site population the estimand averages over, and all three make certification harder (arguments in Supplementary Information A.3). One hospital contributes no stay with a usable outcome, which is why the primary cohort spans 207 sites rather than 208. Twenty-four hospitals are held out as the target population and the remaining 183 split 73 / 36 / 74 into train, auxiliary and calibration; the procedure repeats over 20 by-site re-splits.

**The certificate.** At $\alpha = 0.10$ the gate certifies on all 20 re-splits (Table 2, Figure 2), at mean deployed threshold $\tau^* = 0.793$ and mean coverage 0.890 over held-out target pools ranging from 13,667 to 27,021 records. At $\alpha = 0.05$ it certifies on none: both assumption modes return failsafe on every re-split. That pattern is what Section 4.5's frontier anticipates at 74 calibration clusters — a predicted refusal, not a malfunction — and the arithmetic is visible: at 74 record-carrying calibration clusters the information floor $\ln(1/\delta)(1-\alpha)/n$ is 0.0364 at $\alpha = 0.10$ and 0.0385 at $\alpha = 0.05$, while the estimated certification margin on $S_{\text{aux}}$ — the budget minus the estimated answered error, in the atom units of Section 3.3 — is 0.0462 at the first rung (1.27 times its floor) and 0.0166 at the second (0.43 times), so the stricter rung sits below its floor before any calibration hospital is read. Each rung is certified at $1-\delta = 0.95$, and the $\alpha = 0.10$ rung, being the operative one selected from the ladder, holds jointly at 0.90 (Section 3.7, clause 5). The certified quantity is the one Section 3.3 defines and no other — a site-population average; it is not a guarantee about any one of the 24 target hospitals. On the *published split* — the seeded replicate whose artifacts are released — the deployed threshold is $\tau^* = 0.850$, answering 12,964 of 15,169 target records for coverage 0.855 and answered error 0.0419; the estimated answered-set risk on the 74 calibration hospitals is 0.0387 (95% site bootstrap 0.0356–0.0422). The label-shift mode fitted without declining — 24 target sites, $\hat{\rho} = 0.668$ inside a box $[0.068, 1.986]$, wide as expected when 24 hospitals with their own prevalences are declared as one target, so that the target-side interval is a cluster bootstrap over 24 sites and the auxiliary side rests on 36 (Sections 4.14 and 6.1) — but no threshold certified at both $\rho$ endpoints, so it returned failsafe, and the exchangeable mode carried every deployed certificate in the study.

**The sensitivity arm.** The protocol's declared APACHE-complete arm — the 190 hospitals and 135,127 stays that carry a complete APACHE record, a population the primary arm deliberately does not restrict to — certifies $\alpha = 0.10$ on 19 of 20 re-splits (exact 95% CI [0.751, 0.999]) at mean coverage 0.838 and mean threshold 0.854, with no fresh-pool exceedance; the one refusal (replicate 7) returned failsafe on both modes at 67 calibration clusters, and its $\alpha = 0.05$ rung certifies on none; 25 of its 480 per-hospital pools fall under the 10-record floor, against 3 in the primary arm (Table 2). It is reported beside the primary arm, as the protocol requires, and never as the headline: its site population is the APACHE-contributing hospitals, a different estimand.

**What twenty re-splits can and cannot establish.** Scored against its own held-out 24-hospital pool, each certified re-split's $R_M$ averages 0.0478 (range 0.0348–0.0593) and exceeds $\alpha$ on none of the 20 (exact 95% CI [0, 0.168], nominal: the re-splits are not independent draws) — the pre-declared validity criterion, met subject to two limits that compound: 20 re-splits cannot resolve a 0.05 rate, and they partition one hospital population rather than draw fresh ones. The limit is the margin as much as the count: with realized $R_M$ at 35–59% of the budget the criterion could not have failed short of a gross implementation error, which is what it screens. The run bounds the absence of gross violation at the available power; it does not estimate the rate, and no amount of re-splitting one extract would.

**Per-hospital dispersion.** The per-hospital arm deploys the same calibration-side certificate to each hospital's pool separately — creating no per-hospital guarantee — reaching 477 of 480 pools (the three refusals sit under the 10-record floor). Across the 477, answered error has median 0.0448, interdecile range 0.0188–0.0776, maximum 0.2857; 7 of 477 are hard-violated on the Wilson criterion of Section 3.9 — the hospital's one-sided 95% lower bound on its own answered error exceeds $\alpha = 0.10$ — a 0.0147 rate (exact 95% CI [0.0059, 0.030]) with no $\delta$ target. The seven fall on two hospitals: one, with 112 target records and under 100 beds, is hard-violated on all four of its held-out appearances (coverage 0.81–0.90, answered error 0.18–0.21), the other, with 132 records, on three of four (coverage 0.36–0.58, answered error 0.13–0.29) — persistent and hospital-specific rather than sporadic, so the per-site diagnostic identifies it rather than averaging it away. Age mix is not what marks them: across the 477 pools the rank correlation between a hospital's share of patients aged 75 or older (mean 0.30, range 0.005–0.62 across the pools) and its answered error is 0.019 (post-hoc). This arm measures the dispersion the certificate declines to bound: a hospital at 0.29 answered error under a certificate whose pools average 0.048 is what the dispersion clause says can happen (Supplementary Figure S2).

**Comparator.** APACHE-IVa [@zimmerman2006apache] is a purpose-built day-1 severity score and eICU ships its predictions, so the answered set can be scored against an external clinical reference. On the answered records carrying a comparator value (share 0.822 of answered records, itself site-correlated), APACHE-IVa attains AUC [@hanley1982roc] 0.820 and Brier [@brier1950] 0.0437 over the 20 re-splits; CertGate's answered 0–1 error on that subset is 0.0471 — different currencies, side by side for orientation, not contest. The denominator-matched reading — both models scored by Brier on the identical denominator inside one shared resample, the reference used as shipped — is the post-hoc reliability panel's (Supplementary Information A.4) on the published split: 0.038079 for the reference against 0.037104 for the head, a paired difference of 0.000975 (0.000136–0.001684), too small to carry clinical meaning (Supplementary Information A.4, Figure S1). Both readings are on the subset the gate itself chose to answer — the head's preferred ground. On the full held-out pool, post-hoc, APACHE-IVa attains AUC 0.860 on the 82.2% of records that carry it and the head 0.859 on all of them, while the head's answered-set AUC is 0.810 (Table 2; Supplementary Table S11): both instruments discriminate less well on the answered subset than on the pool, the composition effect in a third form — the answered set is the easy end of the pool for either.

**Leak screens.** Three pre-declared screens passed: the head's out-of-sample AUC on the calibration hospitals averages 0.862 (range 0.854–0.867, the maximum on replicate 4; Supplementary Table S11) against a ceiling of 0.90; ablating the 49 missingness and presence columns costs at most 0.0063 of AUC against a cap of 0.05, so that block is not carrying the model; and a univariate screen of all 161 allowlisted features flags none past the 0.75 review band, settling from data the nine treatment-related columns whose measurement timing could not be established in advance. The fourth screen — for the fear that the presence of an APACHE row partly proxies surviving the first day, since the day-1 window does not close for a stay that ends in death, a leak with no column name — fired in the opposite direction: APACHE-absent stays are about half as deadly as the rest (prevalence ratio 0.506 against a cap of 2.0 on that ratio), shorter (median 27.8 hours against 40.7; 44.2% ended before hour 24 against 29.4%, post-hoc) and answered almost entirely (coverage 0.991, answered error 0.051; Table S8), so APACHE-ineligible admission types and stays that ended before the day-1 window closed, rather than early death, dominate the absence — a caution rather than a clearance, since whole-row absence still encodes stay length, a prediction-time exposure here pointing toward survival. Two column-level missingness indicators did exceed the cap — missing ejection fraction (ratio 4.333) and missing elective-surgery flag (2.6552), both fields recorded by the APACHE definitions mainly for operative and cardiac admissions, so their absence plausibly marks non-operative case mix rather than early death — and remain in the model, bounded by the ablation screen; neither is among the ten most frequent abstention drivers of Section 4.11 (the elective-surgery indicator enters a single re-split's top ten, at rank 10 with a positive gap, so it pulls on answered rather than declined stays). The screens' registration mechanics are in Supplementary Information A.3.

**The seven predictions.** Of the seven pre-declared predictions three held ($\alpha = 0.10$ certifies while $\alpha = 0.05$ does not, 20 of 20 against a registered 15-of-20 threshold; coverage inside the registered $[0.60, 0.95]$ at 0.890; the label-shift mode contributes no certificate), one partly (207 hospitals against a registered 208) and three failed in the benign direction (real hospitals *less* heterogeneous on the per-site diagnostic than the synthetic generator, 0.027 against a registered $[0.05, 0.30]$; fewer per-hospital refusals than registered; no APACHE-absence leak signature among the abstention drivers); the clause-by-clause settlement, made after the fact and changing no certified quantity, is in Supplementary Information A.3.

**What the gate is trading away.** On the published split the answered set's observed mortality is 0.0458 against 0.0898 in its pool (over the 20 re-splits, post-hoc, 0.0524 (range 0.0388–0.0643) against 0.0874 (range 0.0788–0.1038)): the gate reaches its budget partly by abstaining where deaths concentrate — read the two numbers together. The declined 14.5% of stays hold 768 of the pool's 1,362 deaths — a deferred share of 0.56 and a declined-set mortality of 0.35, both on this split's denominators; the answered set predicts death for 63 stays; and the answered error, 0.0419, is nearly the answered mortality. Stated baldly, a rule predicting survival for everyone already meets the $\alpha = 0.10$ budget here — erring at 0.0898 on the pool and at 0.0458 on the answered set itself — so an $\alpha = 0.10$ certificate cannot by itself distinguish the head, at answered error 0.0419, from a rule twice as bad as always-survive there (always-survive errs at 0.0458 on the answered set, and $2 \times 0.0458 < 0.10$); the certificate attests to the answer/abstain rule as a whole, head and gate together, and the budget was set at 0.10 by the site count (Section 4.5), not by the base rate. The certified rung's value is the guarantee and its disclosures, not headroom over the base rate. The reliability panel (Supplementary Information A.4; descriptive, settling no registered prediction) makes the point quantitatively: the answered-set skill margin over the constant rule is 0.003934 on the published split and 0.003–0.008 across the re-splits, and the answered-minus-all contrast in that margin is $-0.006021$ — negative because the head's advantage over the constant rule is smaller on the answered set (0.0039) than on the whole pool (0.0100): the answered set is *easier* for the trivial rule than its pool. The composition is reported three ways, as Section 3.8 prescribes, and on the published split reads 0.0049 (predicted-class), 0.0307 (BBSE-implied, $\hat{\rho} = 0.668$ against a true pool-to-source odds ratio of 0.98) and 0.0458 (oracle): without labels the disclosure would have understated the answered positive load by a third, because the auxiliary-split confusion rates do not transfer to the held-out hospitals — with false- and true-positive rates $c_0 = 0.014$ and $c_1 = 0.261$ and an auxiliary-split source-prevalence estimate of 0.091 (the cohort's is 0.0889), the observed pool prevalence of 0.0898 predicts a positive-call rate of 0.0365 against the observed 0.0299, and inverting the observed rate instead gives an implied target prevalence of 0.063. The confusion rates fail to transfer because the invariance assumption of Section 3.6 fails on this cohort — the case-mix heterogeneity Section 1 describes is itself a departure from it — which is why the fitted $\hat{\rho} = 0.668$ is not to be trusted and why the mode's failsafe return above is the right outcome.

**Operating characteristics and subgroups (post-hoc, derived).** Table S7 reports the answered- and declined-set confusion quantities for all 20 re-splits, derived from the released panel artifact: answered-set sensitivity at the head's inherited 0.5 threshold has median 0.126 — the certified budget coexists with calling few answered deaths. Table S8 is the fairness reading of the same gate: subgroup coverage and answered error, one marginal dimension at a time, over sex, ethnicity, age band, admission source and whether a day-1 APACHE row exists (the unit-type dimension is in the released file, not in the table). Coverage falls 0.96 to 0.82 and answered error rises 0.019 to 0.075 across age bands into 75+ — a 3.9-fold gradient in answered error and a 4.9-fold one in deferral (3.7% to 17.9%) — and the same gradient runs from operating-theatre to floor and step-down admissions, while both quantities are flat across sex (coverage 0.890 and 0.891) and ethnicity (0.875–0.898). Every row mean stays under $\alpha$; at the per-re-split level 8 of 570 resolvable cells exceed it, six of them step-down-unit admissions (maximum 0.1203) — the subgroup-level face of the dispersion clause — and the declined-set error is unresolvable on most re-splits for every ethnicity other than Caucasian and African American (Native American 20 of 20 cells suppressed, Asian 19, Hispanic 15, Other or unknown 14), so the audit is silent for the smallest minority strata, where it is most wanted. The gate is thus within budget in every resolvable subgroup on average and unequal in both answered error and service — the concern the interpretable-and-fair abstention literature raises [@ifac2025abstainexplain], measured — and the gradient tracks severity rather than age as such: pooled over the 20 re-splits, the deferred share of each band's deaths is 0.48, 0.44, 0.46 and 0.49 from the youngest band to 75+ (the youngest band on the 9 re-splits where its declined cell resolves) and declined-set mortality is 0.38–0.39 at every age — a different denominator from the published split's 0.56 and 0.35 above, not a contradiction of them. The stays with no day-1 APACHE row (6,919 in the cohort; mean 728 per held-out pool) — APACHE-ineligible admission types, stays that ended before the day-1 window closed, hospitals that filed no APACHE rows — are answered almost entirely (coverage 0.991) at answered error 0.051 (the pinned-specification addition noted under Scope below). The deployment consequence is an observation rather than a recommendation: the deferred workload would concentrate on exactly the oldest and sickest patients; whether that workload is acceptable is a site decision the certificate does not make, and subgroup coverage belongs beside answered error in whatever a site reports.

**Scope.** One protocol amendment followed the extract read (A6 in the protocol's log): one malformed negative value among roughly 4.1 million cells would, read literally, have aborted the run, so an already-frozen unparseable-data tolerance governs it instead — no new constant, no computed number moved. The descriptive additions made after the read — the reliability panel, the subgroup reading and its day-1-APACHE-row dimension, the value-function contrast — entered the pinned specification as dated, append-only amendments (2026-08-01 to 2026-09-04) rather than the protocol, enter no certificate, and every figure from this extract carries the post-hoc label. Two exposures this cohort carries — unfiltered cross-hospital patients, and the population the estimand actually averages over — are stated in Section 6.1.

## 4.11 Explainable abstention on real clinical data

The margin-to-answer decomposition of Section 3.8 was run on every re-split's pooled target arm, and the answered-to-declined attribution gap — how much harder each feature pulls on the stays the gate withheld than on the stays it answered — was ranked by absolute value at the operative rung (Figure 3). The result replicates where the synthetic attribution study could not. The largest gap falls on the **Glasgow Coma Scale motor score on all 20 re-splits** (mean gap $-0.31$ logit units; a negative gap means the feature pulls harder on declined stays than on answered ones), with the day-1 inspired oxygen fraction in the top three on 13 re-splits and the out-of-theatre day-1 intubation flag on 10 (the Acute Physiology Score (APS) intubation indicator on 5); arterial pH, temperature, the eye-opening score and the APS intubation indicator sit in the top ten on at least 19 of 20. The ranking is unsigned, so the sign is reported with it: three of the sixty top-three cells carry a positive gap — the feature pulling harder on answered stays — and Figure 3 marks them; every other cell is negative. Where the symmetric synthetic generator admits no privileged feature by construction — which is why Section 4.6 reports a null — the real cohort yields one stable driver along an axis that reads, on face validity, as severity: the gate abstains most where the documented neurological status (which sedation can depress — an interpretation, not a measurement made here), oxygenation and airway status leave the answer contested. That is the same severity axis along which the composition disclosure of Section 4.10 shows deaths concentrating in the declined set; the driver and the composition cost are two views of one behavior, and a reader should hold them together.

The contrast with Section 4.6 is the point, not an embarrassment: an instrument that found a driver on every cohort would be suspect. The same code returns a null on a generator built to have none and a replicated driver on a cohort that has one, which shows that the instrument does not fabricate a driver where none exists — and no more than that. The head's strongest coefficient sits on a feature whose distribution also differs between answered and declined stays, and the equal-loading synthetic null cannot separate those two contributions, so the cohort reading is a hypothesis the next paragraph prices rather than a licensed inference.

Two things underneath every attribution a reader sees are named here because clinical explainability reports rarely name them. The first is arithmetic. The gap statistic factorises exactly as $|w_j|$ times the answered-minus-declined difference in mean standardized magnitude, so the attribution machinery contributes only the coefficient, and the motor score leads partly because it carries the largest coefficient among the ten (1.64 times the next); among all 161 features its coefficient ranks second on 16 re-splits, third on 3 and fourth on 1, so at least one feature outside the ten carries a larger coefficient without producing a comparable gap — a large coefficient on a feature whose distribution barely differs between answered and declined stays produces none, which is the factorisation's point. The coefficient-free factor tells a related but not identical story — on it arterial pH leads and the motor score is third (Supplementary Table S10). The second is the value function. The reported values are interventional Shapley values with the training-mean baseline (Section 3.8); under correlated features the *conditional* Shapley values differ [@aas2021conditional; @chen2020truetomodel], and ICU variables are correlated — among the ten drivers the largest absolute pairwise correlation on the training split is 0.85. Recomputing the ten drivers' attributions under the Gaussian conditional value function on the published split, exactly over all $2^{10}$ coalitions, separates what survives the choice from what does not (Supplementary Table S10). The headline survives: the Glasgow motor score is the top driver under both value functions, with its gap unchanged to three decimals, and it stays the top driver under both on all 20 re-splits (Supplementary Table S10). The ordering beneath it does not: the Spearman correlation between the two orderings is 0.58, the APS intubation indicator falls from fourth to tenth and the eye-opening score rises from sixth to second — each a member of a correlated group on the training split, and redistributing credit within such groups is what the conditional value function is defined to do [@aas2021conditional]; the airway flags also split in sign, the signature of collinear indicators, which is why the released page's global register is a transparency surface, not a set of physiological lessons. The lesson is specific: the *leading* driver is robust to the value-function choice, the *ranking* below it is not, because it depends on a choice that a report which does not name it has left unfixed. This check is post-hoc and descriptive, settles no registered prediction, and the Gaussian conditional is an approximation for the binary indicators among the drivers.

What a reader reviewing a deferred stay receives is the case's margin-to-answer, each feature's signed contribution to the logit read against the bar, and the smallest change in standardized feature space — the minimal L2 move — that would have made it answerable, beside the certificate's scope clauses (Section 3.7). The move is a statement about the gate, never a recommendation about the patient: it is unconstrained, may land on an unrealisable input (fractional flags, mixed one-hot levels), and carries no plausibility or actionability model of the kind the counterfactual-explanation and recourse literatures require [@wachter2017counterfactual; @ustun2019recourse; @karimi2021survey]. The released code renders this as a two-register page, a plain-language reading for non-specialist readers and an arithmetic register for auditors, over the committed synthetic demonstration and over a MOCK eICU-shaped corpus that carries the real column names (`examples/explain_dashboard_mock.html`, labelled as a mock on the page itself); the same page over the real extract is local-only because it embeds record-level data. Whether these explanations help a clinician decide is not tested here (Section 6.1).

## 4.12 Alternative certifiers on the same atoms

Four alternative one-sided bounds walk the identical per-site atoms, walk order, and stop-at-first-failure rule: finite-sample Hoeffding [@hoeffding1963] and Maurer–Pontil empirical-Bernstein [@maurerpontil2009] bounds, and the two asymptotic defaults a practitioner would reach for, a Student-$t$ upper bound [@student1908] and a percentile bootstrap over sites [@efron1979; @fieldwelsh2007] (Figure 4, left). Among the valid certifiers the betting test dominates at every site count tested — empirical-Bernstein reaches $\alpha = 0.10$ only at 400 sites, against the betting walk's 150. The asymptotic defaults grant every rung at every site count and attain their nominal exceedance rather than undershoot it — 0.049 for the site bootstrap and 0.051 for the Student-$t$ bound at $\alpha = 0.05$, pooled over the six site counts: they carry no finite-sample guarantee, and the betting test's guarantee is paid for in a realized level well below $\delta$ (no exceedance at either rung). A stricter budget is bought with hospitals, never with a different valid bound.

## 4.13 Stressing the certificate: aleatoric risk and alternative heads

**Aleatoric stress.** The validity grid leaves the certifier untested near its boundary, and tilting the generator cannot reach it — the walk simply deploys a stricter threshold. The stress arm instead raises an aleatoric floor — irreducible label noise — that no threshold can screen: symmetric label flips at rate $\eta$ in every cohort, preserving exchangeability. As $\eta$ rises from 0.01 to 0.04 the walk responds in order (Figure 4, centre): coverage is spent first, then certification is withheld — certify rate 1.0, then 0.63, then 0.02 — while the exceedance rate stays at zero throughout. Near its boundary, the certifier declines before it violates.

**Alternative heads.** A gradient-boosted head and a feature-denied linear head run through the identical atoms and walk (Figure 4, right): every head certifies every draw with zero exceedances — validity belongs to the certificate, not the model — and the denied head pays for its lost signal as coverage, 0.88 against 0.98. The exact attributions of Section 3.8 do not survive the boosted head; the explainability contribution is scoped to transparent heads. Exactness, not availability, is what is lost — sampling approximations in the Shapley family attach to any head, without the closed-form guarantee — and the linear head is the model class clinical scores actually deploy: on eICU it matches APACHE-IVa on the denominator-matched Brier comparison (Supplementary Information A.4), and any head's quality difference is priced as coverage — here the boosted head buys none over the linear head (0.98 against 0.98) while the feature-denied head pays 0.88 — so a site can choose with the price visible.

## 4.14 Power frontiers: label shift and the false-negative budget

**Label shift.** Section 4.3 showed the corrected mode refusing at 208 sites; the power frontier prices that refusal in capacity (Figure 5, left; $R = 50$ draws per point). With a 40-site declared target the mode certifies 0.38 of draws at 900 sites and 0.94 at 1,200, with zero exceedances: under genuine shift it certifies once source capacity suffices and declines below it. The single-declared-site path certifies 0.14, 0.26 and 0.40 of draws at 600, 900 and 1,200 sites and is the only arm of the shipped certificate whose certificates exceeded their budget on fresh pools (the positives-normalised atom's $b = 0.4$ control in Supplementary Information A.5 is the other case): at 1,200 sites 2 of its 20 $\alpha = 0.10$ certificates did (0.10; exact 95% CI [0.012, 0.317]), and 1 of its 3 at $\alpha = 0.05$. A post-hoc rescoring of the arm (Supplementary Information A.3) shows what the exceedance is. The fresh pool is drawn at the population shift, whereas the box was fitted to the declared site's own prevalence — it covered that site's odds ratio in 20 of 20 certificates and the population's in 15 — and rescored against the declared site's prevalence, the estimand the certificate named, 0 of 20 exceed, the three exceeding rows falling from 0.058, 0.107 and 0.109 to 0.018, 0.041 and 0.059. The exceedances are a scoring-estimand mismatch, not over-certification: what a single declared site cannot do is stand in for a population, which is the identification condition of Section 3.6, not a failure of the certificate it issues.

**The false-negative budget.** The symmetric loss prices most answered errors as false negatives, so whether the false-negative rate among answered positives can itself be certified at some budget $b$ — $b$ the false-negative-rate budget, the class-conditional counterpart of $\alpha$ — is the clinical question. It can, on the same atoms (Section 3.3), and the frontier is the honest news (Figure 5, right): with the site-count normalisation inherited from $R_M$ — sites weighted by their records, capped at 100 — and against a measured truth near 0.45 (the true answered-set false-negative rate on the fresh pool), no budget below 0.55 issues at realistic site counts (0, 0 and 0.005 of draws at $b = 0.5$ for 208, 400 and 600 sites), and every issued certificate held on fresh pools. That frontier is a property of the estimand's normalisation as much as of the site count: a positives-normalised atom, which weights sites by their positive count capped at ten — a different and equally valid estimand, argued in Supplementary Information A.5 — certifies $b = 0.5$ in 0.79 of draws at 208 sites and in 0.995 and 1.0 at 400 and 600, with no fresh-pool exceedance at any budget $\ge 0.5$ under either estimand (Supplementary Figure S10; the $b = 0.4$ control, which the positives atom does not always refuse, is reported there). On the real cohort the derived answered-set FNR is 0.80–0.90 (Table S7), so a class-conditional certificate there would need a budget near 0.9 — a budget with no clinical content.

# 5 Discussion

CertGate issues a specific promise: a bound on the influence-weighted answered-set error *parameter*, averaged over the site population the calibration hospitals were drawn from, at confidence $1-\delta$, under one tagged assumption. It is not a per-site bound — the heterogeneity arm shows aggregate conformance holding while the per-site diagnostic rises five-fold — nor a bound on a batch's realized count, nor protection against concept shift. Capacity is counted in sites, not records — 74 calibration *hospitals* on the real cohort, not their $\sim\!10^5$ records; Sections 4.5 and 4.12 price the stricter rung in hospitals, under every valid bound. A black-box head can replace the linear one, its selective quality priced visibly as certified coverage rather than smuggled in as an unearned guarantee. In plain words, what a clinician receives on eICU is an answer/abstain rule at the close of ICU day 1 whose average answered error is bounded — at a budget that, at this prevalence, an always-survive rule also meets (Section 4.10) — with deferrals concentrated where deaths are and nothing certified about sensitivity: on the published split the answered set carries 63 positive calls in 12,964 answers (0.49%), and of the pool's 1,362 deaths 0.56 are deferred, 0.39 answered as survivors and 0.04 flagged (post-hoc; Section 4.10).

For cross-institutional deployment the mapping is direct: a new hospital that differs only in ways the between-site variation already samples takes the exchangeable tag — subject to the dispersion clause, since it may sit in the tail the average does not bound. One that differs mainly in outcome prevalence takes the label-shift tag, with Sections 4.3 and 4.14 the relevant evidence. One that differs in how features relate to outcome — documentation practice, treatment thresholds, how the Glasgow motor score is recorded under sedation — takes neither, and the certificate is void. The machinery is relevant wherever institutions are natural clusters, though CertGate assumes centrally available site-disjoint calibration data; a certified gate under genuine federation [@fedcrc2026] is future work.

The mapping presumes a fixed prediction time — on eICU the close of ICU day 1, or the end of the stay if earlier (Section 4.10) — and it is era-bound: the eICU certificate is a statement about 2014–2015 eICU hospitals, and a present-day deployment must calibrate on contemporary sites, because a decade of practice change is a feature–outcome shift the exchangeable tag does not cover. A refusal is a governance event, not a runtime fallback: the head may run uncertified only under a visible banner (the released page carries one), and the remedy is more calibration hospitals (Section 4.5), never a widened budget. The certificate needs a validity window and a re-audit trigger. Concept shift is undetectable from unlabeled data at prediction time but detectable retrospectively once discharge labels accrue, so a labeled spot-check of roughly the first 200 answered stays is the natural operational check, after which the same betting walk over the new site atoms recertifies; who signs the certificate, and under which tag, is a decision the deploying institution records, not one the software makes.

The two evidence bases answer different questions: the synthetic grid supplies oracle access — certificates checked against a known truth, a negative control verified falsifiable, comparator bounds and stress arms on identical draws — while the eICU study shows the pipeline surviving contact with real multi-site data and disclosing what it trades away (Section 4.10). The synthetic arm tests the method; the real arm tests the implementation; the claim needs both.

# 6 Conclusion

CertGate sits at the intersection of certified selective risk [@chow1970reject; @geifman2017selective], cluster-as-unit distribution-free inference [@dunn2023hierarchical; @lee2025hierarchical], label-shift guarantees propagating weight uncertainty [@si2024pac], and explained rejection [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain]. The result is a certificate whose disclosures travel with it: the tagged assumption, the named scope, the unbounded dispersion beside it, a demonstrated concept-shift boundary. On eICU-CRD v2.0, under a protocol fixed before the extract was read, the $\alpha = 0.10$ rung certifies on all 20 by-site re-splits of one cohort at 0.890 coverage, the stricter rung refused, three pre-declared leak screens clear and a fourth returning a caution the ablation screen bounds; the abstentions point at one stable driver — the Glasgow motor score on every re-split, with oxygenation and airway variables behind it on most — along a severity axis that is coherent on its face, unrated by any clinician (Section 6.1), and the gate's cost is disclosed: on the published split part of the budget is met by abstaining where deaths concentrate, and disproportionately among the oldest and sickest.

## 6.1 Limitations and future directions

*The guarantee is a site-population average* — individual sites are not bounded; a per-site guarantee would need a tolerance bound on an upper risk quantile, costing substantially more clusters. *Concept shift is undetectable from unlabeled data* — the negative control demonstrates the boundary, and the caveat lives in the guarantee text. *Covariate-shift importance weighting is deliberately excluded*: its effective-sample-size loss at practical clip caps — a design-time estimate — puts certification out of reach below roughly 400 clusters, so we ship the two modes the data can certify. *There is no out-of-support screen or positivity diagnostic*, and the certification package never imputes: features must arrive finite, and a missing value is rejected at entry; for eICU the extract-to-feature step fills a missing numeric with the $S_{\text{train}}$ column mean beside a per-column missingness indicator (Section 4.10), so the head can read missingness as a signal while the certified path sees only finite inputs.

*Assumption 1 is the assumption most at risk, and the threat is cross-site dependence rather than a shared shock*: a practice change or seasonal case mix common to every site in the collection window leaves the betting argument intact conditionally on that shock and restricts the certified population to that window — the eICU certificate is a statement about 2014–2015 eICU hospitals (Section 5); the dependences that would break the argument are the ones not shared by all sites — multi-hospital patients (the 3,996 identifiers disclosed below), regional transfer networks, shared data pipelines — which are unquantified, and compounded on the real cohort, whose re-splits partition one fixed hospital list. *The loss is symmetric and the operating point inherited*: $\alpha$ budgets the sum of false negatives and false positives — on the synthetic validity grid roughly three quarters of answered errors are false negatives — and the certificate says nothing about whether the head's $\hat{p} = 1/2$ boundary suits a given clinical decision; the class-conditional budget of Section 4.14 prices that choice — with the site-count normalisation inherited from $R_M$ no false-negative budget below 0.55 issues at realistic site counts, while a positives-normalised atom, a different and equally valid estimand, certifies 0.5 in 0.79 of draws at 208 sites (Supplementary Information A.5) — and a full cost-weighted or net-benefit atom, or a budget expressed relative to the answered set's own base rate, remains future work. *Beyond the influence cap and the stress and comparator arms of Sections 4.12–4.13, the frozen constants are not swept*; pinning attests they were fixed in advance, not that they are optimal.

*The eICU cohort carries disclosed exposures*: 3,996 patient identifiers appear at more than one hospital (share 0.029, a lower bound), deliberately unfiltered because the filter would itself be site-correlated; the certified population is, per Assumption 1, the hospital population the cohort samples — not the finite eICU list, not US hospitals generally; a without-replacement betting variant is future work. *The explanations are a read-out whose clinical utility is untested*: the attributions are exact for the deployed linear head and their leading driver is stable on eICU, but its physiological coherence is asserted on face validity — no clinician reader has rated the driver list or the deferral report, and whether the report changes a decision is unmeasured; the ranking beneath the leading driver depends on the value-function choice (Section 4.11), and none of it survives a non-transparent head (Section 4.13). A clinician walkthrough of the deferral report over the mock-rendered page is the explanation-side next increment. *The composition guard is only as good as the label-shift correction's transfer*: on the published split the BBSE-implied answered positive fraction understated the oracle one by a third (Section 4.10), and the oracle column is a retrospective luxury a deployment does not have. *The real-data evidence is one cohort*: a second independent multi-site cohort, not more re-splits, is the next increment of evidence, and the site-count frontier of Section 4.5 is why none is offered here — the $\alpha = 0.10$ rung needs on the order of 150 hospitals, eICU is the only public multi-hospital ICU database at that scale, and a single-centre database cannot calibrate a site-as-unit certificate at all. *The label-shift mode certifies only at source capacities beyond this cohort*: on eICU it fitted but certified nothing, and the measured frontier of Section 4.14 puts positive certification near 900–1,200 source sites under a multi-site declared target — the refusal is demonstrated on real data, and the price of more is now stated in sites. *The panel's calibration error is a biased plug-in estimate* whose interval covers the biased estimand, and *three of the four BBSE intervals are asymptotic*: the post-hoc probe of Supplementary Information A.2 measures the four-parameter box's joint miscoverage at 0.042–0.056 against a nominal $\le 0.025$, so a label-shift certificate printed at 0.95 is supported at about 0.93 in the probe's regime; a finite-sample cluster bound is future work.

# Supplementary Information A: Deferred proofs and additional material

## A.1 Validity of the betting test

We show the crossing rule $\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta$ (Section 3.4) is a finite-sample level-$\delta$ test of $H_0: \mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$.

*(i) Boundedness.* Each term of the inner sum in $Z_c$ lies in $[-\alpha, 1-\alpha]$, so the prefactor $g_c/(M n_c)$ scales the sum into $[-\alpha\, g_c/M,\ (1-\alpha)\, g_c/M] \subseteq [-\alpha, 1-\alpha]$ because $g_c \le M$; adding $\alpha$ places $Z_c \in [0,1]$.

*(ii) Null equivalence.* Both the atom mean and the estimand are taken over the same index set: $c$ ranges over the population of sites from which the calibration sites are drawn, so $R_M = \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c e_c] / \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c]$ is a site-population parameter and $\mathbb{E}[Z]$ is an expectation over that same site draw. By the identity $Z_c - \alpha = g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)/M$ of Section 3.3, $\mathbb{E}[Z] - \alpha = \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)]/M$, whose sign is the sign of $\mathbb{E}[g_c a_c e_c] - \alpha\,\mathbb{E}[g_c a_c]$ and hence of $R_M - \alpha$. Testing $\mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ is therefore exactly testing $R_M \ge \alpha$ — for the population average, and for no individual site: for a single site $g_c$ and $a_c$ cancel and the quantity collapses to that site's own error rate, which a population mean does not bound (Section 3.7, clause 2).

*(iii) Supermartingale.* This is the step that consumes Assumption 1, and the assumption is not optional. Without it the atoms may carry site-specific means $\mu_t$, the per-step conditional expectation evaluates to $1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - \mu_t)$, and that exceeds 1 at any site with $\mu_t < \alpha$ even when the population average satisfies $H_0$ — the supermartingale property would fail atom-wise, before any product is formed. Under Assumption 1 it holds. The bet $\lambda_t$ is predictable, a function only of atoms already processed, so it is $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable and factors out:

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - Z_t) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr] = 1 + \lambda_t\bigl(\alpha - \mathbb{E}[Z_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\bigr) = 1 + \lambda_t\bigl(\alpha - \mathbb{E}[Z]\bigr) \le 1,$$

the last inequality because $\lambda_t > 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ under $H_0$. The cap at $0.9/(1-\alpha)$ additionally keeps every realized factor $1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - Z_t) \ge 0.1 > 0$, so $K_t$ is a *nonnegative* supermartingale with $K_0 = 1$. The processing order enters only through predictability: any order fixed without reference to the calibration atoms preserves it, which is why the permutation of Section 3.4 is seeded from the protocol constants and carries no target identifier.

*(iv) Ville.* For such a process, $P(\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta) \le \delta$ [@ville1939], so the crossing rule has finite-sample level $\delta$ — no asymptotics, and no distributional assumption beyond the boundedness in (i) together with the sampling condition of Assumption 1 consumed in (iii). The test's boundary behavior (type-I error at $\mathbb{E}[Z] = \alpha$) is additionally checked by the unit test suite, which asserts that the realized rejection rate over 800 Bernoulli($\alpha$) atom sets of 80 clusters stays at or below a tolerance of 0.08 — a tolerance on a Monte-Carlo estimate, not a pin of the level, and coarser than the $\delta = 0.05$ the argument above delivers.

## A.2 Soundness of the dual-endpoint BBSE certification

The uncertainty box (Section 3.6) is a Bonferroni box over four estimated parameters $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}}, q_{\text{target}})$, each interval taken at level $\delta_{\text{conf}}/4$. The first three come from a cluster bootstrap of the $S_{\text{aux}}$ sites that requires 2,000 valid resamples within at most 4,000 attempts (a degenerate pool triggers a decline); the fourth is an exact two-sided Clopper–Pearson interval on the target pool's predicted-positive count for a single-site pool, and a cluster bootstrap over target sites for a pool declaring ten or more (a pool declaring two to nine declines). The box is propagated to $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ by *corner-interval coverage*: the clip on $\pi_t$ is monotone non-decreasing, so the clipped $\rho(c_0, c_1, \pi_s; q)$ remains coordinate-wise monotone in each of the four box parameters on the gated region, and the minimum and maximum of the clipped $\rho$ over the sixteen box corners therefore bound $\rho$ at every interior parameter combination — the corners bound every $\rho$ the box implies. Certification therefore reduces to the two endpoints, which is sound for the following reason.

The certified statistic is scale-invariant in the record weights, so the sign that decides certification factors as $\operatorname{sign}(\mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] - \alpha) = \operatorname{sign}(A + \rho B)$ with $(A, B)$ free of $\rho$ — affine in $\rho$. The certifiable set $\{\rho : \mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] \le \alpha\}$ is therefore convex, so certifying at both endpoints of $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ covers every interior $\rho$, and any $\rho$ inside the box with $\mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] > \alpha$ forces one endpoint whose level-$\delta_{\text{bet}}$ test bounds the false-certification probability. This holds even though the two endpoint atom sets use different per-endpoint normalizations $w_{\max} = \max(1, \rho)$, under which the *raw* atom mean is piecewise with a kink at $\rho = 1$: the affine object is the sign-determining product $(\mathbb{E}[Z]-\alpha)\cdot\max(1,\rho)$, not the kinked atom mean itself. The argument is pinned numerically in the test suite on the deployed normalization, including an interval that straddles $\rho = 1$.

*Measured coverage of the box (post-hoc probe).* The three bootstrap intervals are asymptotic, so the box's realized joint coverage was measured after the fact by a probe that holds the head fixed (`experiments/probe_bbse_coverage.py`, output in `experiments/out-bbse-probe/`; post-hoc, certifying nothing, and leaving the resample count `BBSE_BOOT` unchanged). One 208-site cohort on the generator's stream $(9, 2)$ fixes the head, whose population confusion rates are closed-form under the class-conditional Gaussian generator and checked against Monte Carlo; each of 400 fits per auxiliary-site count $n_{\text{aux}} \in \{36, 42, 74\}$ then draws a fresh auxiliary cohort and one declared target site at the registered shift (base rate 0.22), its random effect drawn in the open so that its prevalence is known exactly, and runs the pipeline's own BBSE fit at the Bonferroni-4 level of 0.00625 per parameter with 2,000 resamples. The mode declined 0.04, 0.0225 and 0.03 of fits, every one as misspecified. Per parameter, over all 400 fits with a box, the coverage sits at or near its nominal 0.99375: $c_0$ 0.985–0.99, $c_1$ 0.98–0.985, $\pi_{\text{source}}$ 0.975–0.9925 and the Clopper–Pearson $q$ 0.9925–0.9975. Jointly it does not: the four-parameter box missed on 0.042, 0.056 and 0.044 of certifiable fits (exact 95% intervals 0.024–0.067, 0.036–0.084 and 0.026–0.069) against a nominal $\le 0.025$, and the three-bootstrap sub-box on 0.042, 0.054 and 0.036 against $\le 0.019$ — the design-time 0.038 of Section 3.6, whose configuration is not recorded, reproduced in magnitude at auxiliary-site counts bracketing the design's (36, 42 and 74). The propagated $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ covered the declared site's own odds ratio in every certifiable fit and the population's in 0.90, 0.90 and 0.85 of them, at median width 5.96, 5.16 and 4.54 (the mean is dominated by a handful of clip-bound fits and is not quoted). Read as a certificate level, $1 - (\text{box miss} + \delta_{\text{bet}})$ gives 0.933, 0.919 and 0.931 — pooled over the 1,200 fits, 0.928, or 0.914 at the conservative upper end of the pooled interval — which is the number to hold beside a label-shift certificate that prints 0.95. Part of the shortfall is Monte-Carlo resolution rather than asymptotics, and that part has a closed form: each endpoint is the linearly interpolated (type-7) sample quantile at $p = 0.003125$ of $B = 2{,}000$ resamples, resolved from about six tail draws, and the expected mass beyond such a sample quantile is approximately $((B-1)p + 1)/(B+1) = 0.00362$, 1.16 times nominal; over the six endpoints of the three bootstrap intervals that adds roughly 0.003 of joint miss — 8–17% (a twelfth to a sixth) of the measured three-bootstrap shortfalls of 0.017–0.035 above the nominal 0.019, the rest being the asymptotic error of the percentile intervals themselves. Raising `BBSE_BOOT` is a frozen-constant change that re-derives every label-shift artifact, and is deferred to a later release. The probe covers the single-declared-site target mode only; the multi-site bootstrap mode would need per-site effects drawn in the open and is left for a later release.

The test suite checks the load-bearing claims rather than merely exercising the code: the betting test's type-I rate at the $\mathbb{E}[Z] = \alpha$ boundary against a 0.08 tolerance on Bernoulli($\alpha$) atoms (A.1); the confidence actually spent at each call site, recorded by a wrapper around the test itself and asserted to be exactly $\delta$ on the baseline path and $\delta_{\text{bet}}$ on the label-shift path; the $S_{\text{aux}}$ provenance of the threshold ordering, recomputed independently and compared; the emitted guarantee text, frozen as an exact string per mode combination so that silently weakening a clause fails continuous integration; every frozen constant, compared to its literal value; and the closed-form attributions of Section 3.8 against exhaustive enumeration of the $2^8$ coalitions on the synthetic head, with the conditional-value matrix of Section 4.11 checked the same way on a correlated block. Three full-scale arms are skipped unless explicitly enabled. The anti-conservativity of naive realized-contribution truncation (Section 3.3) is pinned by a dedicated regression test that exhibits the construction: 140 clean sites of 20 records with zero errors beside 10 heavy sites of 2,000 records at 20% error, everything answered, for a record-weighted selective risk of 17.5%. Truncating each site's *realized* signed contribution $T_c = e^{\text{cnt}}_c(1-\alpha) - (n_c - e^{\text{cnt}}_c)\,\alpha$ at 1 caps the ten heavy sites' adverse evidence at $+1$ apiece while every clean site keeps its full $-1$, and a Maurer–Pontil empirical-Bernstein upper bound [@maurerpontil2009] on the truncated contributions certifies $\alpha = 5\%$ at $\delta = 0.05$ — a false certificate at 3.5 times the true risk. The influence-weighting scheme refuses the same configuration: the influence-weighted atoms have mean above $\alpha$ and the betting test does not reject.

*Cohort filters deliberately omitted (Section 4.10).* There is no length-of-stay floor, because conditioning on survival to a horizon removes the patients who die fastest, does so differentially by site, and is computed from discharge offsets that are themselves on the feature denylist. There is no minimum-stays-per-hospital filter: the protocol projected before the extract was read that a 500-stay threshold would leave roughly 46 hospitals, and on the released extract it leaves 99 of the 207 — about 30 calibration clusters under Section 4.10's split, below the 50 the minimum-cluster gate requires — so that filter would be a direct threat to feasibility, not a modelling preference. And the primary arm applies no APACHE-availability filter, because 17 of the 207 primary-cohort hospitals contribute no rows to that table, so restricting to APACHE-covered stays deletes whole sites and changes the population the estimand averages over without changing a word of the guarantee.

*Pre-registration chronology (Section 4.10).* The repository was initialized at the freeze commit (`9f25b49`, 2026-07-30 22:59, UTC-4), so it has no history before the freeze; `EICU-PROTOCOL.md` at that commit already carries amendments A1–A5, each made on mock corpora before any eICU byte was read, and no A6. The extract was downloaded after the freeze (the download instant itself is not recorded); the one post-hoc protocol amendment, A6, was committed 4 h 17 min after the freeze and the certified 20-re-split run 4 h 31 min after it (the preserved preflight stamp sits 4 h 15 min after), all under the author's own clock — a dated commit in a repository the author controls is weaker evidence than a third-party registry timestamp. The protocol constants are pinned in the manner of Section 3.2, so a post-hoc edit to a constant would be visible; later documentation edits to the protocol file (2026-08-10) are in the repository history.

*Leak-screen registration (Section 4.10).* The protocol pre-declared three screens, two of them independent of both $\alpha$ and coverage: a 0.90 ceiling on the head's out-of-sample calibration-hospital AUC, a 0.05 cap on the AUC cost of ablating the missingness and presence columns, and a 0.75 review band on a univariate screen of every allowlisted feature. The APACHE-absence screen was registered one-sided — an abort if APACHE-absent stays were at least twice as deadly as the rest, a cap of 2.0 on the absent-to-present prevalence ratio — and covered only the two whole-row presence flags, which passed at 0.506; the two column-level indicators that exceeded the cap in the feared direction (ejection fraction 4.333, elective-surgery flag 2.6552) were outside the registered abort and remain in the model, bounded by the ablation screen's 0.0063. The length-of-stay contrast quoted with that screen is post-hoc, from the preflight re-run (`experiments/out-timing/EICU_preflight.json`, `apache_absent_los`).

*The seven registered predictions, settled (Section 4.10).* The clause-by-clause settlement is `experiments/out-settled/EICU-PREDICTIONS-SETTLED.md` (post-hoc; it changes no certified quantity). Confirmed: $\alpha = 0.10$ certifies while $\alpha = 0.05$ does not, 20 of 20 against a registered 15-of-20 threshold (P1); the label-shift mode contributes no certificate — its registered falsification rule, a BBSE-certified threshold the baseline walk refuses, fired on 0 pools, though the registered modal decline reason held on only 7 of 20 re-splits, failsafe being modal on the other 13 (P3); coverage lands inside the registered $[0.60, 0.95]$ at 0.890 (P6). The panel is the same instrument applied in Section 4.7, run over the eICU answered/declined partition (Figure S1). Its own statement of scope follows the numbers.

*The false-negative budget under a second estimand (post-hoc run; Section 4.14).* The shipped class-conditional atom of Section 3.3 keeps the influence weight $g_c = \min(n_c, M)$ on the full site size, and the frozen specification objects to the obvious alternative: renormalising by per-site *answered* positives would make the weight outcome-dependent and void the outcome-independence requirement on $g_c$ (`SPEC.md`, "Outcome-weighted atoms", paragraph "Validity"). The post-hoc runner `experiments/run_e9b_positives.py` (`experiments/out-e9b-positives/`; its shipped arm must reproduce the released frontier cell for cell before anything is written, and did on all 2,400 rows) runs a second arm on the same draws with $g_c^+ = \min(n_c^+, M^+)$, $M^+ = 10$, on the site's positive *count*. That atom does not meet the objection's target: $n_c^+$ is fixed by the site's labels before any threshold is chosen, identical at every rung of the $\tau$ grid, and never a function of the head's errors or of which records answer. The requirement exists so that the certified quantity is one fixed functional of the site distribution — a threshold-dependent weight would make the estimand move with $\tau$ — and $n_c^+$ is $\tau$-independent, so $\text{FNR}_M^+ = \mathbb{E}[g_c^+\, \text{fn}_c / n_c^+] \,/\, \mathbb{E}[g_c^+\, \text{ap}_c / n_c^+]$ is fixed, a ratio of expectations over the site draw exactly parallel to $R_M$. Under Assumption 1 the atoms $Z_c = b + \bigl(g_c^+ / (M^+ n_c^+)\bigr) \sum_{i \in c,\, y_i = 1} \text{ans}_i\,(\text{err}_i - b)$ are i.i.d. and bounded — the inner sum lies in $[-b\, n_c^+, (1-b)\, n_c^+]$, the prefactor scales it into $[-b, 1-b]$ and the shift lands it in $[0, 1]$ — a site with no positives, or none answered, enters as the neutral atom $Z_c = b$, and the sign identity $\mathbb{E}[Z] \le b \iff \text{FNR}_M^+ \le b$ holds exactly as for the shipped atom (pinned in `tests/test_e9b_positives.py`). What changes is the estimand: sites count by their positives, capped at ten, rather than by their records capped at a hundred, and since a certificate is a statement about one named estimand both are reported. The result (Figure S10): at $b = 0.5$ the shipped atom certifies 0, 0 and 0.005 of draws at 208, 400 and 600 sites and the positives atom 0.79, 0.995 and 1.0; at $b = 0.55$ the shipped atom certifies 0, 0.285 and 0.995 against 1.0 at every site count for the positives atom; the measured truth is 0.450 under either estimand, and at every budget $\ge 0.5$ no certificate under either atom exceeded its budget on the fresh pool, scored under its own estimand and under the other's. The $b = 0.4$ control, which the shipped normalisation never certifies, is not a built-in refusal for the positives atom: it issued 6 certificates over the 600 draws at that budget (2 at 400 sites, 4 at 600, at $\tau$ between 0.85 and 0.95), one of the four 600-site certificates exceeded 0.4 on the fresh pool by $10^{-4}$: 0.25 on the conformance metric of Section 3.9 (exact 95% CI [0.006, 0.806] — four certificates resolve nothing), and a joint certify-and-exceed rate of 1 in 600 draws, the quantity Ville's inequality bounds at $\delta$.
