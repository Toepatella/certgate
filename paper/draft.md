**Abstract**

**Background** Clinical risk models are deployed across many sites, yet the records they score cluster by site, so a confidence guarantee written at the record level is silently overconfident once deployment spans institutions. **Methods** We present CertGate, a selective-prediction gate treating the site, not the record, as the unit of independence, certifying with finite-sample confidence $1-\delta$ that the error rate among answered cases — averaged over the site population calibration sampled — stays at or below $\alpha$. That average does not bound individual sites. The certificate rests on an influence-weighted answered-set risk certified by a betting martingale over sites, and a label-shift mode carrying its four parameters' uncertainty inside the guarantee; a linear head gives every answer and abstention an exact attribution. **Results** On a 208-site synthetic cohort the $\alpha=0.10$ rung certifies all 200 draws at 0.983 mean coverage with no exceedance of the certified quantity, inside $\delta=0.05$; under a prevalence shift to 0.22 the uncorrected baseline certifies and violates in 39.5% of draws while the corrected mode declines all 200, a concept-shift control hard-violates 70% as its exclusion predicts, and $\alpha=0.05$ needs about 300 sites. On eICU-CRD v2.0 (164,322 stays, 207 hospitals), under a protocol fixed before the extract was read, $\alpha=0.10$ certifies on all 20 by-site re-splits of one hospital population at 0.890 coverage and the stricter rung refused — bounding gross violation, not estimating the rate. **Conclusions** The certificate's disclosures travel with it: the published split's answered set shows mortality 0.046 against 0.090 in its pool.

**Keywords** Selective prediction · Distribution-free uncertainty quantification · Cluster-robust inference · Label shift · Explainable abstention · Clinical risk prediction

# 1 Introduction

A clinical risk model rarely stays where it was built. A deterioration or readmission score calibrated on patients from a handful of academic centers is deployed at a community hospital that contributed no training records, and the model does not hesitate: it emits a probability for every patient, and downstream software turns it into an action. A safer design wraps the model in a *selective gate* that answers the cases it is confident about and abstains on the rest, routing the hard ones to a clinician [@chow1970reject; @elyaniv2010selective]. The promise attached to such a gate is a bound on the error rate among the cases it chose to answer, holding with high confidence — what a clinician weighs before trusting an automated triage, and what an auditor asks to see documented. Under the way multi-site clinical data is actually distributed, that promise is often false, in a way the deployed system cannot see.

The object to certify is an *answered-set error rate* whose unit of independence is the **site** (hospital), not the record: records within a site share case mix, coding conventions, and instrumentation, so they are not independent draws, and the prevalence a new site presents is generally not the one calibration saw [@tripodcluster2023; @internalexternal2021]. A certificate that treats records as exchangeable overruns its stated confidence once deployment is grouped by site — a failure recently documented for record-level selective-risk rules under grouped deployment [@zhou2026falsesense] and prevalence shift [@triage2026audit]. Selective prediction, cluster-as-unit distribution-free inference, and label-shift correction each have mature literatures (Section 2); multi-site clinical deployment needs all three at once.

We present CertGate, a selective-prediction gate for this setting. The certificate treats the site as the atom of inference: influence-capped per-site statistics feed a betting-martingale (WSR) sequential test [@waudbysmith2024betting; @ville1939], and a fixed-sequence threshold walk selects the operating point without spending confidence on multiplicity [@angelopoulos2021ltt; @westfall2001fixedsequence], in the risk-control tradition [@bates2021rcps; @angelopoulos2024crc]. It ships in two assumption modes, and the mode is part of the guarantee text: an exchangeable baseline, and a label-shift mode that drops the shared-prevalence assumption through a black-box correction [@lipton2018bbse; @garg2020unified] whose four estimated parameters carry their own uncertainty inside the certificate, following the weight-uncertainty-in-the-guarantee pattern of [@si2024pac]. Because the deployed head is linear by design, every answer and every abstention carries exact linear attributions [@lundberg2017shap], attaching explanation to a certified decision rather than to an ungoverned reject option [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain]. One assumption stays deliberately outside the guarantee: concept shift — a change in $P(y\mid x)$ — is undetectable from unlabeled data, so we do not claim to survive it.

On a 208-site synthetic cohort matched to the size, prevalence, and heterogeneity profile reported for large multi-site clinical cohorts, the certificate is valid but not vacuous: at $\alpha = 0.10$ every one of 200 calibration draws certifies at $0.9828$ mean coverage and the certified quantity exceeds $\alpha$ on none of them, inside the $\delta = 0.05$ budget; under a site-level prevalence shift from $0.095$ to $0.22$ the uncorrected baseline certifies and hard-violates on $39.5\%$ of draws while the label-shift mode declines all 200 rather than overclaim; and $\alpha = 0.05$ first becomes certifiable near 300 sites, so the operative rung is a property of how many hospitals contribute, not of the method (Section 4). The same pipeline then runs end to end on eICU-CRD v2.0 under a protocol frozen before the extract was read (Section 4.10).

The primary contributions are:

1. **A site-as-unit certified selective-prediction gate.** A finite-sample $(\alpha, 1-\delta)$ certificate bounding the influence-weighted error rate among answered cases, averaged over the site population, with the site rather than the record as the unit of independence. This is the primary contribution, and its necessity is demonstrated rather than assumed: on the same draws, the equivalent record-as-unit certifier grants the $\alpha = 0.05$ budget the site-unit walk refuses and then violates it at up to twice $\delta$ under between-site heterogeneity (E7), while the site-count frontier prices what honest certification costs (E1, E4).
2. **A label-shift mode that carries its own uncertainty.** A BBSE-based correction folds the estimation uncertainty of all four corrected parameters into the $1-\delta$ budget through a cluster-robust box, with the assumption mode named in the guarantee text — replacing a $39.5\%$-violation baseline with a gate that certifies or declines rather than issue a certificate it cannot support — a secondary extension of the site-as-unit certificate (E2).
3. **Exact explanations attached to every certified decision.** Each answer and each abstention carries exact additive attributions, including which features pushed a declined case below the confidence bar — explanation attached to a *certified* decision, extending reject-option explanation work that carries no statistical guarantee [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain]; it supports the certificate rather than standing as an independent method (E5).
4. **A validation design that can fail.** A concept-shift negative control verified capable of failing before it is run, and a protocol that scores the certificate against the estimand it actually claims (E3) — the constructive answer to a reported diagnosis of overconfident selective certificates [@zhou2026falsesense].

# 2 Related work

CertGate draws on four literatures that each solve part of the problem and stop one axis short.

*Certified selective prediction.* The reject option dates to Chow [@chow1970reject]; El-Yaniv and Wiener [@elyaniv2010selective] formalized the risk–coverage tradeoff, and Geifman and El-Yaniv [@geifman2017selective] turned it into a finite-sample selective-risk certificate on i.i.d. records, since sharpened by the distribution-free risk-control tradition [@bates2021rcps; @angelopoulos2021ltt; @angelopoulos2024crc] and a 2025–26 wave of selective-risk work [@scrc2025; @score2026; @yu2026joint]. Yu and Liu [@yu2026joint] are closest — the same certificate shape, with an acceptance floor and a decline option — but over i.i.d. records, whereas CertGate makes the site the unit of independence and carries a label-shift correction inside the budget.

*Conformal prediction and cluster exchangeability.* Conformal prediction [@vovk2005algorithmic] gives distribution-free coverage under an exchangeability that multi-site clustering breaks. Dunn, Wasserman and Ramdas [@dunn2023hierarchical] and Lee, Barber and Willett [@lee2025hierarchical] supply the cluster-as-unit machinery CertGate reuses; Farinhas et al. [@farinhas2024nonexchangeable] relax record exchangeability inside conformal risk control itself, though controlling risk in expectation; a hierarchical conformal framework has since reached multi-hospital length-of-stay intervals [@shahbazi2026hierarchical]; and Shahid [@fedcrc2026] makes sites first-class in a federated risk control. These deliver coverage or in-expectation risk, not a high-probability budget on the answered subset.

*Label shift.* Black-box shift estimation [@lipton2018bbse] corrects a moving prevalence by confusion-matrix inversion; regularized [@azizzadenesheli2019rlls], maximum-likelihood [@alexandari2020labelshift] and unified [@garg2020unified] variants refine the weights, and Podkopaev and Ramdas [@podkopaev2021labelshift] give reweighted-conformal coverage — all record-level point corrections. Si et al. [@si2024pac] are nearest, propagating confusion-matrix uncertainty into the guarantee rather than treating the correction as exact; CertGate adopts that pattern for a bound on answered-set risk, with the uncertainty made cluster-robust by a bootstrap over sites.

*Multi-site clinical validation and explainable abstention.* Clinical practice already treats the site as the unit — TRIPOD-Cluster [@tripodcluster2023] and internal–external cross-validation [@internalexternal2021] — but reports point estimates, not certificates. Two recent audits sharpen the gap: certified record-level selective-risk rules overrun their budget by 9–30% under grouped deployment [@zhou2026falsesense], and marginal coverage cannot certify a grouped release under prevalence shift [@triage2026audit]; conformal selective prediction with cost-aware deferral has meanwhile been proposed for clinical triage under distribution shift [@scireports2026deferral]. Reject-option explanation methods [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain; @l2lore2025] attach no statistical guarantee to the decision; CertGate's difference is exact attributions on a certified gate.

# 3 Methods

## 3.1 Problem setting: the site as the unit of independence

We consider multi-site clinical data in which on the order of two hundred collection sites each contribute between 20 and 5,000 records, outcome prevalence is roughly 9–10%, and sites differ in both prevalence and case mix. A deployed risk model produces a bounded score $s(x) \in [0.5, 1]$ — the max-softmax of a probabilistic classifier over the binary outcome $y \in \{0,1\}$ — and answers a case only when that score clears an operating threshold $\tau$; otherwise it abstains and defers to human judgment. The object we construct is a *certificate*: with probability at least $1-\delta$ over the draw of calibration sites, the influence-weighted answered-set risk — the parameter $R_M$ of Section 3.3 — *averaged over the population of sites from which the calibration sites were drawn* is at most $\alpha$, under a stated and explicitly tagged assumption about how deployment differs from calibration. This is a site-population average, not a per-site promise: under between-site heterogeneity an individual site's answered risk can exceed $\alpha$ while the average stays inside budget, at a rate the certificate neither measures nor bounds (Section 3.7 states the full clause set; Section 4.2 measures the dispersion). Figure 1 lays out the whole pipeline: the site-disjoint splits, the two assumption modes, the certify-or-decline walk, and the deployed gate with its explanation artifacts.

Because the certified quantity is an *error* rate, its meaning is fixed by what the deployment supplies. The binary label must be pinned by an index event, a prediction time at which the features are frozen, and an ascertainment window over which $y$ is resolved; CertGate certifies whatever binary label the caller supplies and does not itself resolve censoring, competing risks, or a time-to-event horizon. The head's hard label is $\hat{y}_i = \mathbb{1}[\hat{p}(x_i) \ge 1/2]$, the per-record error is $\text{err}_i = \mathbb{1}[\hat{y}_i \neq y_i]$, and the selective score $s(x) = \max(\hat{p}(x), 1 - \hat{p}(x))$ withholds cases near the decision boundary in either direction. The loss is symmetric 0–1 — a modeling choice, and not a neutral one: at single-digit prevalence most answered errors are false negatives (77% in E1), and an error budget near the outcome prevalence is close to what a constant always-negative rule achieves, so meeting the budget is not by itself evidence the gate is doing anything. The operating characteristics of Section 4.2 and the composition instrument of Section 3.8 exist to make that visible; a cost-weighted loss would require a different atom and is not offered here (Section 6.1).

## 3.2 Data discipline and lightweight pre-registration

Sites are partitioned disjointly by site count into $S_{\text{train}}$ (40%, fits the risk model), $S_{\text{aux}}$ (20%, orders the threshold walk and fits the label-shift machinery), and $S_{\text{cal}}$ (40%, reserved for certification); every fitted object is a function of $(S_{\text{train}}, S_{\text{aux}}, \text{target features})$ alone, and a hard assertion at pipeline entry aborts on any site appearing in two pools — the internal–external cross-validation discipline of multi-site prediction modeling [@internalexternal2021]. All governing constants are fixed a priori and pinned to their literal values by a unit test, so any drift fails continuous integration; this is a lightweight, machine-verifiable substitute for third-party pre-registration, and pinning a value is not the same as justifying it — Supplementary Table S1 lists every frozen constant with its role and the basis for its value. The one constant that materially moves the certificate is the influence cap $M$; Section 4.9 reports its sensitivity directly.

## 3.3 The certified estimand: influence-weighted answered-set risk

Because site sizes are heavy-tailed, an unweighted mean of the answered-set error over records would let a single large site dominate the certified quantity. The natural fix — capping each site's *realized* contribution — is demonstrably anti-conservative: a construction with 17.5% true risk certifies at $\alpha = 5\%$ under naive truncation (Supplementary Information A.3 exhibits it; retained as a regression test). Instead, each site receives a *data-independent* influence weight $g_c = \min(n_c, M)$ with $M = 100$, and the certified quantity is

$$R_M = \frac{\sum_c g_c\, a_c\, e_c}{\sum_c g_c\, a_c},$$

where $a_c$ is the answered fraction and $e_c$ the answered-set error rate at site $c$. The cap acts on influence only — it scales each site's whole signed contribution and never censors the error itself. On this cohort's size distribution $89.5\%$ of sites exceed 100 records, so $R_M$ is close to an unweighted average over sites: a 5,000-record hospital and a 100-record one enter with identical total influence. That is what makes the certified quantity cluster-robust rather than dominated by whichever sites happen to be largest, but it is a substantive choice about whose errors count, and Section 4.9 measures the certificate at other values of $M$.

Certification proceeds through the linearized per-site atom

$$Z_c = \frac{g_c}{M\, n_c}\sum_{i \in c} \text{ans}_i\,(\text{err}_i - \alpha) + \alpha \;\in\; [0, 1],$$

constructed so that $\mathbb{E}[Z] \le \alpha \iff R_M \le \alpha$: the inner sum collapses to $n_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)$, so $Z_c - \alpha = g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)/M$, tying each atom to the estimand term by term. Sites that answer nothing enter as *neutral* atoms $Z_c = \alpha$ rather than being dropped, which would narrow the population the guarantee refers to; the minimum-cluster gate counts only record-carrying sites. (No record-carrying calibration site answered nothing in these results; the path is exercised by the test suite.) The expectation defining the estimand is over the *site draw*: $R_M$ is a population aggregate, and what follows from that — scope, dispersion, parameter-not-count, and the shared $1-\delta$ event — is stated once in Section 3.7.

## 3.4 The certification test: betting on the calibration sites

Once each calibration hospital has been reduced to one bounded atom $Z_c$, certification becomes a finite-sample test of whether the mean of those site-level atoms exceeds the budget — and a betting martingale performs exactly that test, sequentially and with no asymptotics. We test the null $H_0: \mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ with the Waudby-Smith–Ramdas betting martingale for means of bounded random variables [@waudbysmith2024betting]. The test needs one assumption about how the calibration pool relates to the population the guarantee quantifies over:

> **Assumption 1 (calibration sampling).** The calibration sites are independent and identically distributed draws from the site population over which the estimand $R_M$ of Section 3.3 is defined. In particular it implies the weaker condition the proof consumes: conditional on the sites already processed, the next site's atom carries the population mean, $\mathbb{E}[Z_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = \mathbb{E}[Z]$.

It does not follow from boundedness, and a certificate computed on sites that violate it is not covered by the argument (Section 6.1 records the residual temporal dependence we do not model). Processing the calibration sites in a fixed order, the wealth process is

$$K_t = \prod_{s \le t}\bigl(1 + \lambda_s(\alpha - Z_s)\bigr), \qquad \lambda_t = \min\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)}{\hat{\sigma}^2_{t-1}\, n}},\; \frac{0.9}{1-\alpha}\right),$$

with a variance floor of $10^{-8}$ and running moments initialized at $(0.5, 0.25)$; we certify if and only if $\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta$. Under $H_0$ the bet $\lambda_t$ is predictable and capped so every wealth factor stays positive, making $K_t$ a nonnegative supermartingale, and Ville's inequality bounds the crossing probability by $\delta$ [@ville1939] — finite-sample, with no assumption beyond boundedness (Supplementary Information A.1 gives the four steps). The processing order is a deterministic permutation seeded from the protocol constants and deliberately carries no target identifier, so it preserves predictability and cannot be chosen to flatter the outcome. At $n$ clusters no test can certify with an arbitrarily small margin; the *information floor* $\ln(1/\delta)(1-\alpha)/n$ — a feasibility diagnostic, never a gate — scales as $1/n_{\text{cal}}$ and sets the capacity frontier E4 maps.

## 3.5 Threshold selection and the budget ladder

The operating threshold is chosen from a grid of 23 values evenly spaced in $[0.55, 0.99]$, walked in a fixed sequence ordered by estimated certification margin on $S_{\text{aux}}$, most conservative first; each candidate is tested at the mode's full betting budget and the walk stops at the first failure — a fixed-sequence, learn-then-test procedure that needs no $\delta$-splitting across the grid [@westfall2001fixedsequence; @angelopoulos2021ltt; @bates2021rcps]. Selection happens at three levels: *within a mode*, the maximum-coverage threshold in the certified prefix (the answered set is nested in $\tau$, so the lowest certified threshold maximizes coverage on every pool at once); *across modes*, the most conservative certified threshold; *across rungs*, the strictest certified $\alpha$. Both budgets $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$ are certified by separate walks and reported rung by rung (Section 4.5, Supplementary Table S6); each rung is individually valid at $1-\delta$, and the selected claim holds jointly at $1-2\delta = 0.90$, as the guarantee text states. At the 208-site scale (about 83 calibration clusters) we expect $\alpha = 0.10$ to be the operative rung; E4 maps the frontier.

## 3.6 Assumption modes

**Baseline (exchangeable).** The target site is assumed exchangeable with the calibration sites and all record weights are one. Tag: "if exchangeability holds."

**Label shift (BBSE).** Only the outcome prevalence may differ between target and calibration; $P(x \mid y)$ is held invariant. We estimate the confusion rates $c_0, c_1$ on $S_{\text{aux}}$, observe the target predicted-positive rate $q$, and invert $q = c_0(1-\pi_t) + c_1\pi_t$ for the target prevalence [@lipton2018bbse; @garg2020unified]; certification reweights calibration records by class with the target-to-source odds ratio $\rho$. Estimation uncertainty is carried inside the guarantee for all four estimated parameters, following [@si2024pac]: cluster bootstraps over $S_{\text{aux}}$ sites for $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}})$ and an exact Clopper–Pearson interval (single-site pools) or cluster bootstrap (ten or more declared sites) for $q$, held at Bonferroni level $\delta_{\text{conf}}/4$ and propagated to a weight interval $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ over the box's sixteen corners. Certification tests both endpoints at $\delta_{\text{bet}}$; this suffices because the sign deciding certification is affine in $\rho$, so the certifiable set is convex (Supplementary Information A.2, including the $\rho = 1$ kink). The budgets combine as $\delta_{\text{conf}} + \delta_{\text{bet}} = \delta$ with $\delta_{\text{conf}} = \delta_{\text{bet}} = 0.025$. One disclosure travels with every BBSE statement: the three percentile bootstrap intervals are asymptotic, and their finite-cluster error is material at this design's scale — with the head held fixed we measure the three-parameter box's realized joint miscoverage at $0.038$ against a nominal $\le 0.019$. The mode *declines* rather than falls back whenever its inputs cannot support a correction — an empty target pool, two to nine declared target sites, a degenerate bootstrap, an ill-conditioned confusion gap $(c_1 - c_0) < 0.10$, or a $q$ interval implying a prevalence outside $(0,1)$; there is no shrinkage and no fallback to the uncorrected baseline anywhere.

**Combination, and what no mode covers.** The modes run as alternatives, each spending its own betting budget, and we deploy the most conservative certified threshold; a mode is listed in the combined guarantee only if its own certified prefix contains the deployed threshold, so the OR-guarantee reads "if *either* tagged assumption holds." Concept shift — a change in $P(y \mid x)$ — is invisible to any method that sees only unlabeled target data, and under it the certificate can be confidently wrong; E3 injects a concept tilt *verified* capable of pushing answered error above $\alpha$ and shows the certificate fail, demonstrating that the assumption tags do real work.

## 3.7 The certified guarantee

The clauses below are the deployed guarantee text: the certificate emits them, and they are frozen as exact strings per mode combination, so weakening or strengthening one is a specification change rather than an editorial one.

(1) *Estimand.* Under exchangeability the certificate bounds the influence-weighted answered-set risk averaged over the population of sites from which the calibration sites were drawn, with the probability taken over the calibration draw; under label shift it bounds that same site-population risk reweighted to the target class prevalence the BBSE correction identifies, with the probability taken over the joint draw of the calibration sites, the auxiliary split, and the target pool. (2) *Dispersion.* This bounds a site-population average and not any individual site's answered error rate: under between-site heterogeneity individual sites can exceed $\alpha$ while the average stays within budget, at a rate the certificate does not measure or bound. (3) *Parameter, not count.* It does not bound any batch's realized error count, which exceeds $\alpha$ at binomial-dispersion rates even under a valid certificate — at the $\alpha$ boundary approaching one batch in two as batches grow. (4) *Shared event, baseline rows only.* In the exchangeable mode the certified thresholds are a function of the calibration draw alone, so one $1-\delta$ event is shared by every target pool the certificate is applied to; the clause is omitted wherever BBSE covers the row, because the BBSE fit depends on the target pool through $q$ and no shared event exists there. (5) *Operative rung.* The operative rung is the strictest certified $\alpha$, a data-driven selection over the $\{0.05, 0.10\}$ ladder, so the selected claim holds jointly at $1 - 2\delta = 0.90$. (6) *Concept shift.* Concept shift and combined shift are out of scope and undetectable from unlabeled data; the certificate is void there. (7) *BBSE rows add.* The $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ box covers four estimated parameters $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}}, q_{\text{target}})$ at Bonferroni $\delta_{\text{conf}} = 0.025$, spent over the auxiliary split and the target pool rather than the calibration draw; the $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}})$ intervals are percentile cluster bootstraps — asymptotic, with realized joint coverage measurably below nominal at small cluster counts (Section 3.6) — while the $q_{\text{target}}$ interval is finite-sample Clopper–Pearson for single-site pools and a cluster bootstrap for multi-site pools, with $\delta_{\text{bet}} = 0.025$ spent on the betting test.

## 3.8 Explainability: answers and abstentions

The deployed head is L2-regularized logistic regression ($C = 1.0$), chosen so the answer/abstain decision is intrinsically interpretable. The gate itself is model-agnostic — the score only *ranks* cases, and the certificate's validity never depends on the model's quality or calibration; a stronger black-box head can be substituted at a visible cost in coverage.

Three artifacts make the gate explainable. *Globally*, standardized coefficients give each feature's direction and strength. *Locally*, an answered case carries exact additive attributions $\phi_j(x) = w_j(x_j - \mu_j)$ with $\text{logit}(\hat{p}(x)) = \text{base} + \sum_j \phi_j$, where $w_j$ is the raw-feature-space coefficient; for a linear model these attributions coincide, in closed form and with no sampling, with the Shapley values of the interventional, independent-baseline game [@lundberg2017shap]. *For declined cases*, answering requires $|\text{logit}\,\hat{p}(x)| \ge \text{logit}\,\tau^*$, so the gate reports the margin-to-answer $m(x) = \text{logit}\,\tau^* - |\text{logit}\,\hat{p}(x)| > 0$ together with the signed attributions of the confidence deficit — "declined because feature A pulls toward positive while features B and C pull toward negative, leaving confidence below the certified bar." This is what a clinician reviewing a deferred case is shown, and what an auditor asking why a case was withheld is given; explaining reject options is itself established [@artelt2023rejectjournal; @ifac2025abstainexplain], and what the gate adds is that the same exact attributions accompany a *certified* decision.

A fourth artifact guards against a subtler failure: the answered-set class composition is reported three ways — predicted-class positive fraction (estimated), BBSE-implied true-class fraction (estimated, tagged), and oracle true-class fraction (a diagnostic available where outcomes are ascertained retrospectively) — which together reveal whether a certificate was earned by answering only easy negatives on a 9–10%-prevalence task rather than by controlling error on a representative mix.

## 3.9 Validation protocol

Validation on the synthetic harness scores the certificate against the estimand it actually claims — scoring a population-average guarantee against single-site draws would test a claim the certificate does not make. One conformance metric carries the $\delta$ target: **aggregate conformance**, the fraction of certified draws whose influence-weighted answered risk $R_M$ on a fresh 200-site evaluation pool exceeds $\alpha$, required to stay at or below $\delta$ over at least 200 independent calibration draws. Two diagnostics deliberately carry none: the single-fresh-site **hard-violation rate** (a site counts as violated only when the one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound [@wilson1927] on its answered error exceeds $\alpha$) beside the mean fraction of evaluation-pool sites exceeding $\alpha$ — the between-site dispersion the certificate declines to bound — and the **realized exceedance rate** against its binomial reference, since small answered sets exceed $\alpha$ by chance at rates approaching 50% at the boundary. The Wilson criterion is deliberately conservative — it protects a valid system from false condemnation — so meeting the target evidences the *absence of gross violations at the tested power*; the guarantee itself rests on the finite-sample level of the betting test, not on this harness.

## 3.10 Reproducibility

Every result regenerates deterministically from the released code: one protocol seed drives the whole grid through a fixed hashing rule, the frozen constants are pinned by a unit test, and a single command reproduces the synthetic grid's figures and tables (Figures 2–4 and Table 1 in the main text; Supplementary Figures S3–S6 and Tables S2–S3 and S6), with identical inputs yielding byte-identical certificates. The real-data results of Section 4.10 come from a separate runner over the eICU extract, which we cannot redistribute; the command, the frozen protocol, and the aggregate artifacts are released, so the analysis is reproducible by any credentialed holder of the data. Supplementary Tables S4–S5 come from a second read-only command that reseeds the same draws and verifies itself against the released E1 artifact before reporting. Package versions, the seeding rule, and provenance are detailed in Supplementary Information A.3.

# 4 Results

We evaluate CertGate on a synthetic multi-site cohort whose every generative mechanism is known by construction, then on eICU-CRD v2.0. The synthetic harness exists for *oracle access*: because the data-generating process is controlled, the true answered-set risk is computable and a certificate can be checked rather than assumed — a real cohort has labels, so it can measure a violation, but it cannot separate a certificate that holds because the method is sound from one that holds because the cohort was benign. Because record-level selective certificates have been shown to overrun their budgets under grouped deployment [@zhou2026falsesense], the design includes a verified-falsifiable negative control and a record-as-unit comparator run on the same draws.

## 4.1 Experimental setup

The generator follows the specification frozen in `data.py`: 208 sites with clipped-lognormal sizes spanning roughly 20 to 5,000 records; $d = 8$ features with the class signal on one normalized direction supported by features 0–3 ($\mathrm{sep} = 2.2$); base prevalence 0.095 with a site random effect $u_c \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.5^2)$ on the outcome log-odds, so per-site prevalence varies and no two sites share a case mix — the distributional profile reported for large multi-site clinical cohorts [@tripodcluster2023; @internalexternal2021]. Labels are fully ascertained and carry no time axis, so the clinical-target specification of Section 3.1 is a requirement on real deployments, not something these experiments instantiate. Splits are site-disjoint 40/20/40; the head is L2 logistic regression fit on $S_{\text{train}}$. Every experiment runs under protocol seed 20260721 over the $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$ ladder at $\delta = 0.05$; the statistical experiments replicate over $R = 200$ independent calibration draws — the calibration draw, not the target site, is the unit of replication — and results are scored with the protocol of Section 3.9, with exact Clopper–Pearson 95% intervals on primary rates.

## 4.2 E1 — Validity in distribution

With target sites exchangeable with calibration, the $\alpha = 0.10$ rung certifies every one of 200 draws at mean answered-set coverage 0.9828 (minimum 0.871 — no certificate is bought by abstaining). Scored against the certified estimand, the certificate conforms on every draw: 0 of 200 exceed $\alpha$ (exact 95% CI $[0, 0.018]$) against the $\delta = 0.05$ target, at a mean fresh-pool $R_M$ of 0.0567 — a little over half the budget (Supplementary Figure S3). The per-site dispersion diagnostic records what the certificate declines to bound: the single-fresh-site hard-violation rate is 0.02, and raising the site random effect $s_u$ from 0.5 to 1.0 and 2.0 leaves aggregate conformance untouched (0.0 exceedances in all three arms) while the diagnostic detaches — the hard rate moves to 0.10 and 0.095 and the mean per-site exceedance fraction climbs 0.055, 0.1285, 0.1634. This is the aggregate-versus-individual signature clause (2) of the guarantee names, and why the $\delta$ target attaches to the aggregate and not to the diagnostic. The realized exceedance rate, 0.08 overall, sits far below its binomial reference in every answered-set size bin (Supplementary Table S2) — the parameter-versus-realized-count distinction in action. The stricter $\alpha = 0.05$ rung certifies nothing at 208 sites: the system declines rather than issue a certificate it cannot support, and E4 maps what that budget would require.

**Operating characteristics.** A budget on an error rate can in principle be met by a degenerate rule, so Supplementary Table S5 reports what the answered and declined sets contain. Pooled over the 200 fresh evaluation pools at $\alpha = 0.10$: 27,972,430 answered records at prevalence 0.0968 and answered error 0.0567, sensitivity 0.550, specificity 0.986, PPV 0.803, NPV 0.953, with $76.9\%$ of answered errors false negatives — the asymmetry the symmetric loss does not price. The declined set holds 517,230 records (1.8% of the total) at positive fraction 0.492 — roughly $4.7\times$ the pools' own prevalence — so abstention concentrates on genuinely contested cases and routes $8.6\%$ of all positives to a clinician. The gate is not meeting its budget by answering only easy negatives (answered positive fraction 0.0968 against the pools' 0.1039); at the same time, a constant always-negative rule errs at 0.1039 on these pools, so at this prevalence the error budget alone is a weak test, and the sensitivity and composition columns are what show the gate doing work.

## 4.3 E2 — Label shift

We shift site-level prevalence from 0.095 to 0.22 — a 2.3-fold increase of the kind a changed referral pattern produces — and compare the exchangeable baseline against the BBSE mode [@lipton2018bbse]. The uncorrected baseline is the motivating failure: at $\alpha = 0.10$ it certifies all 200 draws and hard-violates 39.5% of them (exact 95% CI $[0.327, 0.466]$, far above $\delta = 0.05$) — and against the certified estimand itself, the influence-weighted answered risk on a fresh label-shifted pool exceeds $\alpha$ for 97.5% of its certificates (Figure 2, left). BBSE issues no certificate on this configuration: it declines all 200 draws (194 failsafe, 6 misspecified), so certify-and-violate is 0 of 200. That is the honest behavior, not a fallback — once the target predicted-positive rate carries its own confidence share, a single-site target pool of this size cannot support a certified rung under this shift, and the mode refuses rather than repeat the baseline's overclaim. A magnitude sweep through target base rates $\{0.095, 0.13, 0.16, 0.19, 0.22\}$ separates a working correction from a reflexive decliner (Figure 2, right): the baseline's failure is dose-responsive (hard-violation 0.0 to 0.395; aggregate-estimand violation 0.0 to 0.975) while BBSE's certify-and-violate rate is 0.0 at *every* magnitude — and at the null point it certifies 9% of draws with zero violations. The mode certifies when the pool supports it and refuses in proportion to what the shift destroys.

## 4.4 E3 — Concept-shift negative control

A negative control that cannot fail proves nothing, so the harness first *verifies the poison*: the injected posterior tilt (concept intercept 2.0) is confirmed to raise mean realized answered error to 0.161, above the $\alpha = 0.10$ budget, and a tilt failing that check aborts the run before any output is written. Given a genuinely poisonous shift, the certificate certifies all 200 draws and hard-violates 70.0% (exact 95% CI $[0.631, 0.763]$); against the certified estimand the failure is total — 100% of certificates exceed $\alpha$ on a fresh concept-tilted pool (Supplementary Figure S4). This is the intended result: concept shift is the failure mode the guarantee explicitly excludes, and the assumption tag is load-bearing rather than rhetorical [@zhou2026falsesense].

## 4.5 E4 — Cluster-count sweep

E4 is the capacity-planning study: it sweeps the site count over $\{60, 100, 150, 208, 300, 400\}$ at both rungs (Supplementary Table S6, Figure 3). At $\alpha = 0.10$ certification switches on at 150 sites and holds (certify rates $[0.0, 0.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0]$; coverage 0.9818 at the 208-site operating point). The 60- and 100-site declines come from the 50-record-carrying-cluster gate, not the betting test's information floor — the two failure modes have different remedies. At $\alpha = 0.05$ the certify rates are $[0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.285, 1.0]$: the stricter budget is unreachable through 208 sites, first appears at 300, and becomes reliable only at 400. The frontier is set by the information floor scaling as $1/n_{\text{cal}}$: a stricter answered-set budget is a claim about how many hospitals one has, not about the method.

## 4.6 E5 — Explainability case studies

Because the head is linear, the answer/abstain decision carries the exact attributions of Section 3.8. In a single-deployment case study ($\tau^* = 0.55$; 200 answered, 2 declined) the global importances recover the generator — features 0–3 dominate at 1.155–1.178, features 4–7 are negligible — and each declined case's margin-to-answer localizes its confidence deficit to named features (Supplementary Figure S5). Replicated over $R = 200$ draws (186 with declines; 2,644 declined cases pooled; pooled decline rate 2.15%), the single-draw reading does not survive: no stable single abstention driver exists (top-feature counts 47, 40, 51, 48 across features 0–3; modal share 0.274 against a 0.25 chance level) — exactly what the generator's symmetric construction predicts, and we report the null as a null. What does replicate is the *shape*: declined cases carry systematically larger attribution magnitudes on every informative feature (mean gaps $-0.21$ to $-0.26$), the signature of contested cases whose large pulls cancel. The cohort-level instrument works, and on this generator it correctly finds no privileged feature; the real cohort of Section 4.10 is where a stable, clinically legible driver appears.

## 4.7 E6 — Per-site coverage uniformity and composition

E6 deploys a single draw ($\tau^* = 0.55$; 40 target sites) and asks whether coverage is uniform across site sizes and how the gate earns its low answered error — the equity question scoped to site size: whether small hospitals receive systematically worse selective service. Coverage is essentially flat across size bins (0.9896, 0.9800, 0.9856; Supplementary Table S3) with mean answered error below $\alpha$ in every bin (0.0523, 0.0670, 0.0595; Figure 4), and no site answers nothing. Here, unlike E1, the certified quantity and the record-level average separate: $R_M$ is 0.0619 against an unweighted answered error of 0.0555 — the influence weighting counting the 15 mid-sized sites (answered error 0.0670) as heavily as the 21 largest (0.0595), which is what it exists to do, and visible only because both numbers are reported.

The composition analysis (Table 1) is the instrument that tells us *how* a 9.5%-prevalence task yields a sub-$\alpha$ answered error. Over 25,314 answered records, the predicted-class positive fraction is 0.0630, the BBSE-implied true-class fraction 0.0858, and the oracle fraction 0.0915 — just under the cohort prevalence, so at this operating point the sub-$\alpha$ error is not bought by answering only easy negatives. The predicted-class column alone would have understated the true positive load by about a third; without the oracle column one could not tell which reading was right, which is why the three-way report exists. A post-hoc reliability panel over the same partition (descriptive; added after E1–E7 were published; no certified quantity moves) adds that the answered set is close to calibrated at the deployed threshold (plug-in ECE 0.002914 against 0.033783 on the declined set), the answered-set skill margin over a constant always-negative rule is 0.035948, and the answered-minus-all contrast straddles zero — the composition reading in a second currency.

## 4.8 E7 — Record-as-unit comparator

The design's premise — record-as-unit certificates are overconfident on site-clustered data — entered as a citation [@zhou2026falsesense]; E7 demonstrates it on the same draws. The comparator is the identical betting machinery with each *record* as one atom, walked over the same grid, on a 2,000-record subsample of $S_{\text{cal}}$ — still roughly 24 times the site count, with an information floor smaller by the same factor. At $\alpha = 0.05$ — the rung the site-unit walk refuses on every one of 400 draws across both heterogeneity arms — the record-unit certifier *grants* the budget almost always (certify rate 1.0 at $s_u = 0.5$, 0.99 at $s_u = 2.0$) and then violates the aggregate budget in 3.5% and 9.6% of those draws respectively — the latter nearly twice $\delta$ (Supplementary Figure S6). At $\alpha = 0.10$ both units certify on all but one of 400 draws (the site unit declines once at $s_u = 2.0$), but the record unit deploys materially laxer thresholds under heterogeneity (mean $\tau$ 0.587 against 0.747) and runs deployment at fresh-pool risk 0.081 against the site unit's 0.052 — most of the way to the boundary, with no budget left for the cluster variance its independence assumption ignores. The record-as-unit certifier buys its extra rungs and coverage by spending confidence the clustered data does not have.

## 4.9 Sensitivity to the frozen influence cap

Of the frozen constants, $M$ is the one whose value visibly moves the certificate, so E1's exchangeable arm is re-run at $M \in \{25, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 5000\}$, re-deriving the walk at each value and rescoring against $R_M$ *at that same* $M$ (Supplementary Table S4). The result is a plateau and then a cliff: across $M \in \{25, 50, 100\}$ the procedure is effectively invariant (certify rate 1.0, coverage 0.984, zero exceedances); behavior moves at $M = 200$, coverage is visibly bought down by $M = 500$, the certify rate collapses to 0.03 at $M = 1000$, and at $M = 5000$ — where the estimand becomes the record-proportional answered error — nothing certifies at 208 sites. The stricter rung is unreachable at every $M$, so the frontier of Section 4.5 is not an artifact of this constant; and no $M$ above the frozen value certifies *more*, so $M = 100$ is not a setting that makes certification easy. $M$ selects a point on a continuum between a site-equal and a record-proportional estimand, and at this cluster count only the site-equal end is certifiable at all.

## 4.10 eICU-CRD v2.0 — the certificate on real multi-site data

Everything above is synthetic, for the reason Section 4 opens with. This section reports what the same pipeline did on a cohort nobody constructed. The eICU Collaborative Research Database v2.0 [@pollard2019eicudb; @pollard2018eicu; @pollard2026physionet; @goldberger2000physionet] contains 200,859 ICU stays from 139,367 patients admitted to 208 US hospitals in 2014–2015, under credentialed access and a data use agreement forbidding sharing access to the data. We extend that obligation, as a matter of our own release policy under the agreement's disclosure-avoidance clauses, to any record-level artifact derived from the extract: every artifact behind this section is consequently aggregate, and a gate on each write refuses payloads carrying a record-level key or an array longer than 512 elements.

The cohort predicates, the 161-column deny-by-default feature allowlist, a 36-entry leak denylist, the split arithmetic, seven numbered predictions and five failure criteria were written from public documentation — the schema DDL, the data descriptor, the published column documentation — and committed to version control before the extract was downloaded. A dated commit in a repository the authors control is weaker evidence than a third-party registry timestamp. Every protocol constant is pinned to its literal value by a unit test, so editing one after the data arrived turns continuous integration red and appears in the diff.

**Cohort.** The unit is the first ICU stay of each hospital admission, the outcome is in-hospital mortality as recorded in a single documented column, and the site is the hospital. Table 2 runs 200,859 raw stays down to 164,322 in the primary cohort over 207 hospitals at 8.89% prevalence, recording deaths at every step, so an outcome-correlated exclusion would surface as a prevalence movement rather than hide inside a stay count. Three filters standard in published eICU cohorts are absent, and Table 2's caption gives the argument for each; all three make certification harder. One hospital leaves of its own accord — it contributes no stay with a usable outcome — which is why the primary cohort spans 207 sites rather than 208. Twenty-four hospitals are then held out as the target population and the remaining 183 split 73 train / 36 auxiliary / 74 calibration, against the floor of 50 record-carrying calibration clusters, and the whole procedure is repeated over 20 by-site re-splits of that one hospital population.

**The certificate.** At $\alpha = 0.10$ the gate certifies on all 20 re-splits (Table 3, Figure 5), at mean deployed threshold $\tau^* = 0.793$ and mean coverage 0.890 over held-out target pools ranging from 13,667 to 27,021 records. At $\alpha = 0.05$ it certifies on none: both assumption modes return failsafe on every re-split. That pattern is what Section 4.5's frontier anticipates at 74 calibration clusters, and the refusal is the system working rather than failing. The certified quantity is the one Section 3.3 defines and no other — the influence-weighted answered-set risk averaged over the population of hospitals from which the calibration sites were drawn. It is not a guarantee about any one of the 24 target hospitals. On the published split the deployed threshold is $\tau^* = 0.850$, answering 12,964 of 15,169 target records for coverage 0.855 at an answered error of 0.0419 on that pool; the estimated answered-set risk on the 74 calibration hospitals is 0.0387, with a 95% bootstrap over calibration sites of 0.0356–0.0422. The label-shift mode fitted without declining — 24 target sites, $\hat{\rho} = 0.668$ inside a box $[0.068, 1.986]$ — but no threshold certified at both $\rho$ endpoints, so it returned failsafe, and the exchangeable mode carried every deployed certificate in the study.

**What twenty re-splits can and cannot establish.** Scored against its own held-out 24-hospital pool, each certified re-split's influence-weighted answered risk $R_M$ averages 0.0478 (range 0.0348–0.0593) and exceeds $\alpha$ on none of the 20. The pre-declared validity criterion asked for that exceedance rate to stay at or below $\delta = 0.05$, and it did, subject to two limits that compound: 20 re-splits cannot resolve a 0.05 rate, and they partition one hospital population, so they are not 20 draws of the site population the guarantee quantifies over. The run bounds the absence of gross violation at the available power. It does not estimate the rate, and no amount of re-splitting one extract would.

**Per-hospital dispersion.** The per-hospital arm certifies 477 of 480 single-hospital pools (24 hospitals $\times$ 20 re-splits), the three refusals being pools under the 10-record answerable floor; pool sizes run from 3 to 4,660 records with a median of 494. Across the 477, answered error has median 0.0448 and interdecile range 0.0188–0.0776 with a maximum of 0.2857, and 7 of 477 are hard-violated on the one-sided Wilson criterion, a rate of 0.0147 that carries no $\delta$ target. This arm exists to measure the between-site dispersion the certificate declines to bound, and a hospital running at 0.29 answered error underneath a certificate whose pools average 0.048 is what clause (2) of the guarantee says can happen (Supplementary Information, Figure S2).

**Comparator.** APACHE-IVa [@zimmerman2006apache] is a purpose-built day-1 severity score and eICU ships its predictions, so the answered set can be scored against an external clinical reference. On the answered records carrying a comparator value (share 0.822, itself site-correlated), APACHE-IVa attains AUC 0.820 and Brier 0.0437 averaged over the 20 re-splits; CertGate's answered 0–1 error is 0.0471 on that subset — different currencies, side by side for orientation rather than as a contest. The like-for-like reading, both models scored by Brier on the identical denominator inside one shared resample, is the post-hoc panel's on the published split: 0.038079 for the reference against 0.037104 for the head, a paired difference of 0.000975 (0.000136–0.001684) in the head's favour (Supplementary Information A.4, Figure S1). The answered set is a population on which a mature clinical score behaves about as it does in the literature.

**Leak screens, and a pre-registered alarm that fired backwards.** A good number on real data is the outcome nobody investigates, so the protocol pre-declared three screens, two of which depend on neither $\alpha$ nor coverage. The head's own out-of-sample AUC on the site-disjoint calibration split peaks at 0.867 across the 20 re-splits against a pre-registered ceiling of 0.90; ablating the 49 missingness and presence columns costs at most 0.0063 of AUC against a cap of 0.05, so that block is not carrying the model; and a univariate screen of all 161 allowlisted features flags none past the pre-registered 0.75 review band, which settles from data the nine treatment flags whose measurement timing could not be established from documentation.

The most informative screen fired in the wrong direction. The protocol registered a specific fear: because the day-1 APACHE window does not close for a stay that ends because the patient died, the presence of an APACHE row is partly a proxy for surviving the first day, and a feature encoding it would be a leak with no column name, invisible to a denylist that matches names. The measured absent-to-present outcome prevalence ratio is 0.506 against a cap of 2.0. APACHE-absent stays here have *lower* mortality: they are shorter (median 27.8 hours against 40.7) but less deadly, so early discharge rather than early death dominates the absence. The registered mechanism is sound in principle and is not what governs this extract. Two column-level missingness indicators did exceed the ratio cap; the abort gates the two whole-row presence flags, and the cell-level half of that channel is what the ablation screen above bounds.

**What the abstentions point at.** The margin-to-answer decomposition of Section 3.8, run on the pooled target arm, localizes each declined stay's confidence deficit to named clinical variables rather than to an opaque score, and on real data it replicates in the way E5's synthetic arm could not. The largest answered-to-declined attribution gap falls on the Glasgow motor score on all 20 re-splits; inspired oxygen fraction is in the top three on 13 and the day-1 out-of-bed intubation flag on 10. Where the symmetric synthetic generator admits no privileged feature by construction — which is why Section 4.6 reports a null — the real cohort yields a stable and clinically legible one: the gate abstains most where consciousness, oxygenation and airway status leave the answer contested, which is what a clinician reviewing a deferred case would be shown, and what an auditor asking why a case was withheld would be given.

**The seven predictions.** Four were confirmed, three were not. Confirmed: $\alpha = 0.10$ certifies while $\alpha = 0.05$ does not, at 20 of 20 and 0 of 20 against a registered threshold of 15 of 20 (P1); the label-shift mode contributes no certificate (P3); mean coverage lands inside the registered $[0.60, 0.95]$ at 0.890 (P6); and cohort size and APACHE-result site count land inside their intervals at 164,322 stays and 190 hospitals (P7). Falsified: the per-site dispersion diagnostic came in at 0.027 against a registered $[0.05, 0.30]$ — real hospitals were *less* heterogeneous on it than the synthetic generator's 0.055, not more (P2); the per-hospital arm refused three of 480 pool-instances against a registered one to six of the 24 hospitals (P5); and P4 — the APACHE-absence features among the top three abstention drivers — fails on 18 of the 20 re-splits, with `aps_heartrate__missing` ranking third on the other two. Those two exceptions are exactly the observations P4's leak logic exists to surface: under the amendment that identified APACHE absence as a potential leak channel, a *confirmed* P4 would have been the leak's signature rather than a success, and the ablation screen's 0.0063 ceiling bounds what that channel could carry.

**What the gate is trading away.** The answered set's observed mortality is 0.0458 against 0.0898 in the target pool: the gate reaches its budget partly by abstaining where deaths concentrate, and a deployer reading the certified number has to read this one beside it. A post-hoc reliability panel (Supplementary Information A.4) puts the same fact in a second currency — against a constant always-negative rule the answered-set skill margin is 0.003934, and the answered-minus-all contrast is $-0.006021$, so the answered set is *easier* for the trivial rule than the pool it came from. Both are descriptive, added after the extract was read, and settle none of the registered predictions.

**Scope.** One amendment was made after the extract was read, the only post-hoc change in the protocol: the abort on an unrecognised negative sentinel is now bounded by an already-frozen threshold, because the extract carries exactly one negative-but-not-sentinel cell in roughly 4.1 million against an otherwise contiguous non-negative support. It introduces no new constant and moves no computed number, and every figure derived from this extract carries the post-hoc label. Two exposures this cohort carries — unfiltered cross-hospital patients, and the population the estimand actually averages over — are stated in Section 6.1.

# 5 Discussion

CertGate issues a specific, bounded promise: a bound on the influence-weighted answered-set error *parameter* averaged over the population of sites calibration sampled, at confidence $1-\delta$, under one explicitly tagged assumption. It is not a per-site bound — E1's heterogeneity arm shows aggregate conformance holding at zero exceedances while the per-site diagnostic rises five-fold — nor a bound on any batch's realized error count, nor protection against concept shift, where E3 shows exactly what happens. Capacity is counted in sites, not records: statistical power comes from roughly 80 calibration *hospitals*, not the $\sim\!10^5$ records they contain, and for any reader tempted to call $\alpha = 0.10$ weak, E4 prices the stricter rung in units of sites. The gate is model-agnostic — a black-box head can replace the linear one, and its selective quality would be priced visibly as certified coverage rather than smuggling in an unearned guarantee.

For cross-institutional deployment the mapping is direct. If a new hospital differs from the calibration sites only in ways their between-site variation already samples, the exchangeable tag applies and the certificate holds as a site-population average — subject to clause (2), since that hospital may sit in the tail the average does not bound. If it differs mainly in outcome prevalence, the label-shift tag applies, and E2 is the relevant evidence: at the pool sizes tested, that mode declines rather than certifies. If it differs in how features relate to outcome — documentation practice, treatment thresholds — neither tag applies and the certificate is void (E3). The machinery is relevant wherever institutions are natural clusters, but we state this as relevance, not as a federated method: CertGate assumes site-disjoint calibration data centrally available, and a certified gate under genuine federation constraints [@fedcrc2026] is future work.

The two evidence bases answer different questions. The synthetic grid supplies oracle access — certificates checked against a known truth, a negative control verified falsifiable, a comparator run on identical draws. The eICU application shows the pipeline surviving contact with real multi-site data — real missingness, real coding heterogeneity, a pre-frozen protocol, clear leak screens, and an external clinical comparator — while disclosing what it trades: on the published split the answered set's observed mortality is 0.046 against 0.090 in its pool. The synthetic arm tests the method; the real arm tests the implementation against the world; the claim needs both.

# 6 Conclusion

CertGate occupies the intersection of certified selective risk [@chow1970reject; @geifman2017selective], cluster-as-unit distribution-free inference [@dunn2023hierarchical; @lee2025hierarchical], and label-shift guarantees that propagate weight uncertainty [@si2024pac]. The result is a certificate whose honesty features travel with it: the tagged assumption, the named site-population scope, the dispersion it does not bound reported beside it, and a demonstrated concept-shift boundary. On eICU-CRD v2.0, under a protocol fixed before the extract was read, the $\alpha = 0.10$ rung certifies at 0.890 mean coverage across all 20 re-splits with the stricter rung refused, three pre-declared leak screens come back clear, and the gate's cost is disclosed rather than hidden: it reaches its budget partly by abstaining where deaths concentrate.

## 6.1 Limitations and future directions

Each boundary below is stated rather than patched over. *The guarantee is a site-population average* — individual sites are not bounded; a genuine per-site guarantee would need a tolerance bound on an upper quantile of the per-site risk distribution, costing substantially more clusters. *Concept shift is undetectable from unlabeled data* — E3 demonstrates the boundary, and the caveat lives in the guarantee text. *Covariate-shift importance weighting is deliberately excluded*: at practical clip caps its effective-sample-size loss prevents certification below roughly 400 clusters, so we ship the two modes the data can certify rather than a third that would decline everywhere. *There is no out-of-support screen and no positivity diagnostic*; missingness handling is the caller's responsibility, validated loudly at entry rather than imputed. *Temporal common-shock correlation is not modeled, and it is the assumption most at risk*: sites sharing a collection window may be correlated through practice changes or seasonal case mix, a direct threat to Assumption 1 whose effect on the realized level we do not quantify. *The loss is symmetric and the operating point inherited*: $\alpha$ budgets the sum of false negatives and false positives — roughly three quarters of answered errors are false negatives at this prevalence — and the certificate says nothing about whether the head's $\hat{p} = 1/2$ boundary suits a given clinical decision; a cost-weighted or net-benefit atom is future work. *Only the influence cap is swept* among the frozen constants; pinning attests the others were fixed in advance, not that they are optimal. *The eICU cohort carries disclosed exposures*: 3,996 patient identifiers appear at more than one hospital (share 0.029, a lower bound) and are deliberately not filtered, because the filter would itself be site-correlated; and the certified population is the eICU hospitals surviving the cohort predicates — not US hospitals generally. *The real-data evidence is one cohort*: 20 re-splits of one hospital population bound the absence of gross violation but cannot estimate the violation rate; a second independent multi-site cohort, not more replicates, is the next increment of evidence. *The label-shift mode is evidenced synthetically*: on eICU it fitted but certified nothing, so the real-data section demonstrates that the correction refuses honestly, not that it certifies usefully. *The panel's calibration error is a biased plug-in estimate* whose interval covers the biased estimand, and *three of the four BBSE intervals are asymptotic* with measured joint miscoverage 0.038 against a nominal $\le 0.019$ — disclosed wherever the guarantee is stated; a finite-sample cluster bound is future work.

# Supplementary Information A: Deferred proofs and additional material

## A.1 Validity of the betting test

We show the crossing rule $\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta$ (Section 3.4) is a finite-sample level-$\delta$ test of $H_0: \mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$.

*(i) Boundedness.* Each term of the inner sum in $Z_c$ lies in $[-\alpha, 1-\alpha]$, so the prefactor $g_c/(M n_c)$ scales the sum into $[-\alpha\, g_c/M,\ (1-\alpha)\, g_c/M] \subseteq [-\alpha, 1-\alpha]$ because $g_c \le M$; adding $\alpha$ places $Z_c \in [0,1]$.

*(ii) Null equivalence.* Both the atom mean and the estimand are taken over the same index set: $c$ ranges over the population of sites from which the calibration sites are drawn, so $R_M = \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c e_c] / \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c]$ is a site-population parameter and $\mathbb{E}[Z]$ is an expectation over that same site draw. By the identity $Z_c - \alpha = g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)/M$ of Section 3.3, $\mathbb{E}[Z] - \alpha = \mathbb{E}[g_c a_c (e_c - \alpha)]/M$, whose sign is the sign of $\mathbb{E}[g_c a_c e_c] - \alpha\,\mathbb{E}[g_c a_c]$ and hence of $R_M - \alpha$. Testing $\mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ is therefore exactly testing $R_M \ge \alpha$ — for the population average, and for no individual site: for a single site $g_c$ and $a_c$ cancel and the quantity collapses to that site's own error rate, which a population mean does not bound (Section 3.7, clause 2).

*(iii) Supermartingale.* This is the step that consumes Assumption 1, and the assumption is not optional. Without it the atoms may carry site-specific means $\mu_t$, the per-step conditional expectation evaluates to $1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - \mu_t)$, and that exceeds 1 at any site with $\mu_t < \alpha$ even when the population average satisfies $H_0$ — the supermartingale property would fail atom-wise, before any product is formed. Under Assumption 1 it holds. The bet $\lambda_t$ is predictable, a function only of atoms already processed, so it is $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable and factors out:

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - Z_t) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr] = 1 + \lambda_t\bigl(\alpha - \mathbb{E}[Z_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\bigr) = 1 + \lambda_t\bigl(\alpha - \mathbb{E}[Z]\bigr) \le 1,$$

the last inequality because $\lambda_t > 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[Z] \ge \alpha$ under $H_0$. The cap at $0.9/(1-\alpha)$ additionally keeps every realized factor $1 + \lambda_t(\alpha - Z_t) \ge 0.1 > 0$, so $K_t$ is a *nonnegative* supermartingale with $K_0 = 1$. The processing order enters only through predictability: any order fixed without reference to the calibration atoms preserves it, which is why the permutation of Section 3.4 is seeded from the protocol constants and carries no target identifier.

*(iv) Ville.* For such a process, $P(\sup_t K_t \ge 1/\delta) \le \delta$ [@ville1939], so the crossing rule has finite-sample level $\delta$ — no asymptotics, and no distributional assumption beyond the boundedness in (i). The test's boundary behavior (type-I error at $\mathbb{E}[Z] = \alpha$) is additionally pinned by the unit test suite.

## A.2 Soundness of the dual-endpoint BBSE certification

The uncertainty box (Section 3.6) is a Bonferroni box over four estimated parameters $(c_0, c_1, \pi_{\text{source}}, q_{\text{target}})$, each interval taken at level $\delta_{\text{conf}}/4$. The first three come from a cluster bootstrap of the $S_{\text{aux}}$ sites that requires 2,000 valid resamples within at most 4,000 attempts (a degenerate pool triggers a decline); the fourth is an exact two-sided Clopper–Pearson interval on the target pool's predicted-positive count for a single-site pool, and a cluster bootstrap over target sites for a pool declaring ten or more (a pool declaring two to nine declines). The box is propagated to $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ by *corner-interval coverage*: the clipped $\rho(c_0, c_1, \pi_s; q)$ is coordinate-wise monotone in each of the four box parameters on the gated region, the minimum and maximum of $\rho$ over the sixteen box corners bound every interior parameter combination, and the clip on $\pi_t$ only widens the resulting interval, so the corners bound every $\rho$ the box implies. Certification therefore reduces to the two endpoints, which is sound for the following reason.

The certified statistic is scale-invariant in the record weights, so the sign that decides certification factors as $\operatorname{sign}(\mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] - \alpha) = \operatorname{sign}(A + \rho B)$ with $(A, B)$ free of $\rho$ — affine in $\rho$. The certifiable set $\{\rho : \mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] \le \alpha\}$ is therefore convex, so certifying at both endpoints of $[\rho_{\text{lo}}, \rho_{\text{hi}}]$ covers every interior $\rho$, and any $\rho$ inside the box with $\mathbb{E}[Z(\rho)] > \alpha$ forces one endpoint whose level-$\delta_{\text{bet}}$ test bounds the false-certification probability. This holds even though the two endpoint atom sets use different per-endpoint normalizations $w_{\max} = \max(1, \rho)$, under which the *raw* atom mean is piecewise with a kink at $\rho = 1$: the affine object is the sign-determining product $(\mathbb{E}[Z]-\alpha)\cdot\max(1,\rho)$, not the kinked atom mean itself. The argument is pinned numerically in the test suite on the deployed normalization, including an interval that straddles $\rho = 1$.

## A.3 Software and reproducibility details

Every result regenerates from the released code under a pinned environment (`requirements.txt`): Python 3.13.3 with `numpy==2.5.0`, `scipy==1.18.0`, `scikit-learn==1.9.0`, `joblib==1.5.3`, `threadpoolctl==3.6.0`, `matplotlib==3.11.0`, and `pytest==9.1.1`; no undeclared dependencies are used, and all third-party imports sit at module top level. All randomness derives from a single protocol seed (20260721): every per-experiment, per-$\alpha$, per-mode, and per-site seed is produced by a fixed SHA-256 rule from that one seed, so the whole grid is a deterministic function of it, and identical inputs produce byte-identical certificates. The frozen design constants — split fractions, budget ladder, influence cap $M$, threshold grid, betting-test parameters, and decline thresholds — are pinned to their literal values by a unit test, so any drift fails continuous integration. The full grid runs from a single command (`python -m experiments.run_synthetic`), which regenerates the synthetic figures and tables (Figures 2–4, Table 1, Supplementary Figures S3–S6 and Supplementary Tables S2–S3 and S6); the eICU results of Section 4.10 (Figure 5, Tables 2 and 3) come from `python -m experiments.run_eicu`, which requires a credentialed copy of the extract that cannot be redistributed with this paper, and whose every write passes a gate refusing record-level keys and arrays longer than 512 elements; each report artifact embeds a provenance block recording package versions, seeds, and input hashes. The influence-cap sweep and the operating-characteristic counts (Supplementary Tables S4 and S5) are produced by a second read-only command (`python -m experiments.panel_s2_tables`) that reseeds the same E1 and E6 draws from the same protocol seed and recertifies them at each candidate $M$; it asserts against the released E1 artifact that its replay reproduces every baseline-deploying draw's certified threshold exactly (194 of 194, no mismatches) before reporting anything. The test suite pins the load-bearing claims rather than merely exercising the code: the betting test's empirical type-I level at the $\mathbb{E}[Z] = \alpha$ boundary; the confidence actually spent at each call site, recorded by a wrapper around the test itself and asserted to be exactly $\delta$ on the baseline path and $\delta_{\text{bet}}$ on the label-shift path; the $S_{\text{aux}}$ provenance of the threshold ordering, recomputed independently and compared; the emitted guarantee text, frozen as an exact string per mode combination so that silently weakening a clause fails continuous integration; and every frozen constant, compared to its literal value. Three full-scale arms are skipped unless explicitly enabled. The anti-conservativity of naive realized-contribution truncation (Section 3.3) is pinned by a dedicated regression test that exhibits the construction: 140 clean sites of 20 records with zero errors beside 10 heavy sites of 2,000 records at 20% error, everything answered, for a record-weighted selective risk of 17.5%. Truncating each site's *realized* signed contribution $T_c = e^{\text{cnt}}_c(1-\alpha) - (n_c - e^{\text{cnt}}_c)\,\alpha$ at 1 caps the ten heavy sites' adverse evidence at $+1$ apiece while every clean site keeps its full $-1$, and a Maurer–Pontil empirical-Bernstein upper bound on the truncated contributions certifies $\alpha = 5\%$ at $\delta = 0.05$ — a false certificate at 3.5 times the true risk. The influence-weighting scheme refuses the same configuration: the influence-weighted atoms have mean above $\alpha$ and the betting test does not reject.

## A.4 The post-hoc selective reliability panel on eICU

The panel is the same instrument Section 4.7 applies to E6, run over the eICU answered/declined partition (Figure S1). Its own statement of scope follows the numbers.

On the pooled 24-hospital target arm (replicate 0, $\alpha = 0.10$, $\tau^* = 0.850$, coverage 0.854638; 15,169 records, 12,964 answered), the same panel returns an answered-set expected calibration error of 0.004757 (95% CI 0.002591–0.009664) against 0.022430 (0.009290–0.049626) on the declined set, a weak-calibration slope of 1.082649 (0.991115–1.233626) with intercept 0.305678, and an answered Brier score of 0.038438 (0.031158–0.044759). Against APACHE-IVa on the denominator-matched subset — the 10,404 answered records (share 0.80253, over 22 hospitals) that carry a reference probability — the reference scores 0.038079 (0.031912–0.043270) and the head 0.037104 (0.030803–0.042137), a paired difference of 0.000975 (0.000136–0.001684) in the head's favour, computed inside one shared resample rather than by differencing the two intervals. The skill margin on the answered set is 0.003934 (0.001796–0.005850) against a constant always-negative error rate of 0.045819, and the answered-minus-all contrast is $-0.006021$ ($-0.009365$ to $-0.003328$). This panel is post-hoc — added after the extract was read, outside the pre-extract freeze — and settles none of the pre-registered predictions. It is reported because the composition disclosure already established that the gate earns its low answered error partly by abstaining where deaths concentrate (answered oracle positive fraction 0.045819 against the pool's 0.089788); the skill margin is the number that says how much of what remains is accuracy rather than selection, and at 0.003934 on the answered set against 0.045351 on the declined set it is thin.

The plug-in calibration-error caveat of Section 6.1 applies to the two expected-calibration-error figures above, and every interval is marginal: no joint coverage is claimed across the panel's statistics.
